The Shadows Are Dirty

Lower ISO, add ambient light, and do not lift shadows hard; lifted black is noise.

Noisy shadows: lower ISO, add a little ambient light, and do not force the shadows up. Lifted blacks are noise.

On set

Drop to native ISO, add a small ambient light, and do not lift the shadows hard. Let some black stay.

Do this

  1. Set ISO to its native base; accept a slightly darker image.
  2. Add a quarter stop to the shadow with a small lamp or reflector; do not light the face straight on.
  3. Lift shadows only slightly in post and keep blacks black; do not excavate the noise.

Avoid

Do not use “cinematic” as an excuse for underexposure; crushed shadows do not come back.

Three causes

For “The Shadows Are Dirty”, check three directions first. Change one variable, record a 30-second comparison, and see which link the change follows.

CauseSignal on setFive-minute test
Underexposure + high ISOLifting shadows reveals noise and grey colourDrop ISO one stop and add face light
Log not transformedThe image is flat and black is not blackApply the correct colour transform first
Heavy compressionShadow blocking and smearing appearUse a higher bitrate or a ProRes intermediate

On-set diagnosis

  1. Open the waveform: check black level and whether shadows hold more than 5% detail.
  2. Judge after transforming Log, not on the flat image.
  3. Compare high and low bitrate versions to see whether blocking is compression or noise.

Fix

Dirty shadows usually mean too little light on set and too much lifting in post. Expose skin first, then decide how black the shadows should be.

  1. Add soft light to the face; let the background go dark before underexposing skin.
  2. Drop to native ISO and use ND to control highlights.
  3. Apply the correct LUT/transform before judging black level.
  4. Denoise shadows only until blocking disappears; keep some grain.

Failure cases

With “The Shadows Are Dirty”, these three moves make it worse. Pick one replacement action and do not change three variables at once.

FailureWhy it gets worseReplace with
Lift the whole imageNoise and blocking grow togetherRaise midtones and hold black
Maximum denoiseSkin turns plastic and hair smearsLocal denoise; keep texture
Use high ISOShadows get dirtier and highlights clip soonerAdd light instead of gain

Acceptance table

Acceptance is not “it feels better”; all four must be identifiable, playable and logged.

CheckPass lineLog
Skin exposureThe face is readable, not underexposedWaveform 60–70%

NoiseNo obvious blocking in shadows100% playback
ColourSkin is not green or magenta after transformColour chart or skin reference

Boundary

It does not guarantee a story. The template only orders structure; the person, obstacle and cost still need writing.

Alternative

If “Set ISO to its native base; accept a” is not the main problem, start with the basic move behind “Add a quarter stop to the shadow with”. Upgrade only after both hold.

60-second test

Run a 60-second test with the gear you own, focused on “Set ISO to its native base; accept a”, and record one number. If the number does not change, do not add budget.

When not to use

Do not use the template when “Set ISO to its native base; accept a” is still unclear or “Add a quarter stop to the shadow with” is only filler. Go back to the person and the problem.

Three failures

  • Every beat exists, but no one makes a decision.
  • The middle changes location, not information.
  • The ending is narrated, with no visible change.

3 checks

  • Post lifts shadows only slightly and does not excavate noise.
