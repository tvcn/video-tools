HDR: You Need HDR on Set and in the Room

HDR is not a brighter SDR; capture, monitoring, and delivery must all support it.

HDR is not lifting shadows infinitely; it preserves information from window to dark corner. Expose so highlights just avoid clipping, confirmed with false color or waveform; leave noise room in shadows and decide later how far to push.

HDR protects highlights before brightness

Check on the target display before delivery: an HDR master can look gray on a normal screen. Make a separate SDR version; do not post one master everywhere.

Limits

HDR is not a look filter. Without an HDR display and correct metadata, the audience may see worse than SDR. Confirm the delivery chain before shooting.

HDR needs a monitor before it needs a delivery spec

Grading HDR without an HDR monitor is grading blind. Confirm the chain: camera log → HDR monitor → PQ/HLG delivery. If any link is SDR, you are guessing.

  1. Set monitor luminance to the delivery standard, not the TV's “vivid” mode; keep room light low.
  2. Grade highlights in PQ first, then make the SDR trim. Do not reverse-engineer HDR from SDR.
  3. Watch the SDR version on a normal TV before delivery; highlights should not glare and shadows should not crush.

Acceptance: both HDR and SDR versions, skin consistent and highlights detailed. If only one works, the conversion is not reversible.

On set

Confirm the monitor supports HDR and the footage is 10-bit Log. Deliver HDR10 or HLG as its own pass; do not stretch an SDR grade.

Do this

HDR is not a brightened SDR image; capture, monitoring, and delivery must all support HDR.

  1. Confirm the monitor supports HDR and the footage is 10-bit Log.
  2. Make a separate pass for HDR10 or HLG delivery.
  3. Do not lift an SDR version; preserve HDR highlights and shadows.

Avoid

Do not call a lifted SDR image HDR; the highlights are fake and shadows carry no information.

Judge

Three signals are observable on set:

  • removing one shot makes the scene faster, not muddier
  • skin, highlights and blacks sit inside scope limits
  • loudness, subtitles and file specs pass one delivery check

Failures

These three moves erase the detail you just built:

  • cuts follow the music, not the action
  • style before unity: shots jump in color
  • loudness and subtitles are checked at the very end

Extend

10-minute exercise: Take 30 seconds of footage and make an A/B, changing only “Confirm the monitor supports HDR and the footage”. Note one timecode and one difference before keeping it.

3 checks

  • Footage is 10-bit Log and the monitoring chain truly supports HDR.
  • The HDR pass is separate, not an SDR lift.
  • Watch both HDR and SDR versions; each holds its own highlights and shadows.
