AI Disclosure: Visible, Specific, Honest

Disclosure must be visible, specific, and say what was generated; buried at the end is no disclosure.

The audience deserves to know what was not shot

Disclose generated content whenever it could affect a judgment about a real event, person or product. Put it before they see it, not at the end; name which section is generated, the tool, and whether a live plate exists.

News, advertising, education and personal work have different standards, but “do not pass it off as现场” is the floor.

Limits

Disclosure is not a disclaimer. Saying “AI generated” does not license a false claim; it clarifies provenance, not truth.

AI disclosure: ask “could viewers mistake it for real,” then ask the platform

AI disclosure is not a small line at the end. The test is whether an ordinary viewer could take synthetic content as a real record. If yes, disclose in the frame, the caption and the metadata.

Project-level checks

  1. List every synthetic shot as full AI, partial AI or AI-assisted. Do not write a blanket “made with AI.”
  2. If it depicts a real person, news event or historical footage, make the label more prominent; a corner footnote is not enough.
  3. Keep prompts, model versions and human edits for correction.
  4. Before publishing, ask someone uninvolved to watch ten seconds and answer “is this real?” If they get it wrong, strengthen the label.

Platform rules change. Recheck the current requirement; old rules are not a defense.

On set

Put a visible label in the first three seconds: some images in this film are AI-generated. Name the specific sections in the description.

Do this

AI disclosure must be visible, specific, and say which parts were generated; buried at the end of a description is no disclosure.

  1. Put a visible label in the opening or first three seconds: “Some images in this film were AI-generated.”
  2. Name the specific passages in the description.
  3. Show someone unaware and ask which part they think is not live-action.

Avoid

Do not bury the label at the end of the description; if it is unseen, it was not disclosed.

3 checks

  • The AI label is visible in the first three seconds without pausing.
  • The description names the generated passages, not just “some images.”
  • An unaware viewer can point to at least one non-live-action passage.

Go deeper: for people already shooting

AI disclosure is not a legal appendix. It is the precondition for the audience to judge whether the film can be trusted.

One step past the field note

Three levels of use

Fully generated, live-action with generated elements, live-action with AI-assisted post. Three different disclosures. Full generation goes on the first frame or under the title; assisted post can be described in the notes.

Do not call AI-assisted editing “AI-generated,” and do not hide full generation as “assisted.” Overstating and understating are both inaccurate.

What the audience needs to know

Which section is generated, whether the person or background is generated, whether the voice is synthetic, whether a real person was face-swapped. Four items tell the audience what to trust.

If synthetic content involves real people, brands, or news events, be more specific. Do not hide behind “some visuals were AI-assisted.”

Platform, client, own site

Use the platform disclosure. In client contracts, write “AI labels may not be removed.” On your own site, put it under the title, on the first frame, or the first line of the description; at least one visible.

If a client asks to remove it, ask why. For real people or news contexts, decline. Labeling is a floor, not a line item.

Five-minute drill: one disclosure line

Take a film with AI and write one line: which section, which model, what was generated, what live-action remains. Under forty words.

Show the line to someone who has not seen the film and ask which part is not real. If they can answer, the disclosure works.

When disclosure is enough

Disclosure is enough when the audience sees it before watching, can point to the generated section, can separate voice from image, and the platform and film match. Four pass.

If one fails, do not patch it with “it is at the end of the description.” The audience will not read that far.

Ten disclosure checks

  • Separate full generation from assisted post
  • Full generation on first frame or under title
  • Assisted post says what was used
  • Say which section is generated
  • Say person or background
  • Say whether voice is synthetic
  • Face swaps of real people stated specifically
  • Use platform disclosure
  • Contract forbids removing labels
  • Visible before watching

Disclosure is not liability avoidance. It tells the audience where to place the film before they watch it.
