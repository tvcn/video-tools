Dual-System Sound: Slate and Timecode

Sync dual-system sound with a clap or timecode. With neither, post is guessing.

The camera mic is scratch; the external recorder captures clean dialogue. Clap or slate before rolling so both files share a sync point; do not align by camera audio alone.

Dual system records scratch and clean sound

Record 48 kHz/24-bit, keep raw WAV; no denoise or compression on set.

Limits

Dual system is not “two copies of sound.” If the lav is misplaced or gain is wrong, the clean track is also ruined; monitor on set.

On set

Clap three times at the head, or sync the recorder to timecode. Announce the scene number at the end of every take so post never has to guess.

Do this

Dual-system sound needs a clap or timecode; without either, post is guessing. Leave a sync mark at the head and tail.

  1. Clap three times at the head so camera and recorder both capture it.
  2. Jam timecode or announce the scene and take at the end of each run.
  3. Sync on the clap frame or timecode in post, then check each take for drift.

Avoid

Do not rely on waveform auto-align; without a clap or timecode, drift grows as you fix it.

Judge

Three signals are observable on set:

  • highlights are not clipped and shadows keep recoverable detail
  • skin stays consistent across camera angles
  • sound has an independent track, not just the on-camera mic

Failures

These three moves erase the detail you just built:

  • exposure is right but the light has no direction
  • sound relies on the camera mic and dies in a noisy room
  • crossing the line leaves the audience without a map

Extend

10-minute exercise: Shoot three 10-second takes by the same window, changing only “Clap three times at the head so camera”. Compare highlights, skin and background; keep the one that is editable.

3 checks

  • Every take starts with three claps visible and audible.
  • Timecode or slate notes are complete enough for post to match audio by scene.
  • After sync, playback stays in lip-sync from head to tail.
