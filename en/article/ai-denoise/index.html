AI Denoise: Protect the Dialogue First

AI denoise must protect sibilance and word tails; do not turn lines into a robot.

On set

Keep the 2 to 4 kHz sibilance and line endings. After denoising, listen to s and sh; if they turn into th, back off by half.

Do this

AI denoise protects sibilance and word tails; do not turn the voice into a robot.

  1. Preserve the 2 to 4 kHz sibilance and sentence tails.
  2. After denoise, listen to s and sh; if they become th, back off by half.
  3. Check on a phone speaker for metallic artifacts.

Avoid

Do not max the denoise; losing sibilance makes the voice fake.

Processing order

Edit dialogue first, then denoise. Do not denoise after compression or the mix. Order: dialogue edit → mouth noise/pop repair → denoise → EQ → compression → mix. Denoise before EQ so you do not amplify the noise floor you just lifted.

If a local noise can be fixed with spectral repair, do not run broadband denoise; broadband processing eats sibilance and word tails.

Parameter limits

  1. Start at 6 dB attenuation and listen. Add up to 10 dB only if needed; do not go straight to maximum.
  2. Keep 2–4 kHz sibilance and word tails. After denoise, listen to s/sh; if they turn into th, back off.
  3. Leave a little room tone; do not make the background a vacuum. A vacuum makes dialogue sound fake.

More AI denoise is not better. Judge by “I cannot hear the processing”, not by a clean waveform.

Denoise failures

  1. Metallic or underwater: too much attenuation. Halve it and add room tone.
  2. Sibilance gone: s/sh becomes th. Preserve the high band or bypass locally.
  3. Word tails cut: the end of a sentence goes clean. Check the gate and the denoise tail.
  4. Pumping: noise moves with the voice. Use a steadier reduction or process in sections.

Dialogue acceptance log

  • A/B the raw and processed versions: s/sh are still sibilant and word tails are intact.
  • Listen on a phone and headphones: no metallic, underwater or pumping artefacts.
  • Noise floor is lower but the room is still there; no vacuum silence.
  • Log plugin, settings, processing order and monitoring; another person can reproduce the judgement.

Dialogue log

Log mic model, sample rate, plugin, attenuation, processing order, monitoring headphones and room tone. After a mic or room change, recheck s/sh; do not reuse the previous denoise setting.

When not to denoise

Do not run dialogue denoise on music, ambience or practical sound you need to keep; process them separately. If the voice is already clean, denoise only adds artefacts.

3 checks

  • S and sh remain sibilant, not turned into th or erased.
  • Sentence tails survive and remain audible.
  • On a phone speaker there is no metallic or underwater tone.
