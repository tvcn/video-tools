Wind Noise Outside

Use a windshield and find shelter; post cannot recover words eaten by wind.

Have the host say one sentence in the open, beside a wall, and with a body blocking the wind; record ten seconds in each. Compare on headphones and choose the position with the clearest consonants. Record ten seconds of ambience as a reference for post.

Find where the wind comes from

  • Steady crosswind: a furry windshield is not enough; move to a blimp or add a second cover, and do not point the capsule into the gust.
  • Gusts: lavs suffer first. Add a windshield and hide the capsule inside the collar.
  • Low-frequency rumble: usually wind hitting the capsule or boom, not a noisy floor. Move the boom and add protection before denoise.

When not to rely on post

Once wind eats the consonants, denoise only makes the voice duller. Move, shorten the mic distance and add protection on set; it is always cheaper. If dialogue already has wind, do not cover it with music.

On set

Use a wind screen, find shelter, and block the wind with your body. Post cannot recover words the wind ate.

Do this

Outdoor wind: put a windshield on the mic first, then find shelter. Post noise reduction cannot recover words the wind ate.

  1. Fit a furry windshield or cage on the shotgun; add a wind cover to lavs.
  2. Use your body, a backpack, or a wall as a barrier, and move the mic closer to the mouth.
  3. Record a sheltered reference track and use only light noise reduction later.

Avoid

Do not tell yourself post will fix it; wind takes consonants first.

Boundary

This page only covers “Wind Noise Outside”; it does not replace Career & Clients, Release & Distribution, Media Literacy: Five Questions for Any Video. When the problem belongs to one of those pages, use that page instead of adding more parameters here.

Alternative

If “The shotgun has a windshield and the lav has a furry cover” is not the main problem, start with “A body, backpack, or wall blocks the wind at the shooting spot”; only upgrade after both hold.

60-second test

Run one 60-second test against check 1: The shotgun has a windshield and the lav has a furry cover. If check 2 (A body, backpack, or wall blocks the wind at the shooting spot) does not change, do not add gear or budget.

Check

  • The shotgun has a windshield and the lav has a furry cover.
  • A body, backpack, or wall blocks the wind at the shooting spot.
  • Consonants survive the wind and post uses only light noise reduction.
