Deepfake Detection: Look Beyond the Face

Look beyond the face: teeth, ears, blinks, background text, and light direction.

Deepfakes usually fail on physics, not features: mismatched shadow direction, wrong glasses reflection, changing tooth count, hair passing through a shoulder, a neck that does not move with speech. Frame-by-frame relationships are more reliable than “does it look like them.”

Check physics before the face

Then check provenance: where is the original, who shot it, is there an uncompressed copy? Without a source chain, the eye alone cannot conclude.

Limits

Do not treat an AI artifact as proof of a fake. Compression, beauty filters and low light create similar flaws. State probability and evidence, not “obviously fake.”

On set

Check teeth, ears, blinks, background text, and light direction. Watch five seconds frame by frame for anomalies.

Do this

Deepfakes are not only about the face. Check teeth, ears, blinking, background text, and light direction.

  1. Step through five seconds: are teeth a single row, ears symmetric, blinks natural?
  2. Check background text, signs, and reflections in glasses for continuity.
  3. Find the original source, a response from the person, or a second angle; if all three fail, treat it as high risk.

Avoid

Do not conclude “it looks fake”; name the specific mismatches.

Deep dive: a deepfake is not a true/false switch

About 6 minutes. The goal is not a verdict but an evidence grade: can this clip be used as evidence, within what scope, and which check is still missing. “It looks fake” and “it has an AI artifact, so it is fake” both keep errors moving.

A three-layer evidence model

Physics: check whether the image is internally consistent. Are teeth a single row, ears symmetric, blinks natural, background text continuous, reflections in glasses aligned with the light, and the neck moving with speech? Physics produces suspicions, not a verdict.

Provenance: ask where the file came from. Is there an original camera file, is the uploading account the person it claims to be, does the timestamp match the event, and is there a second angle or an uncompressed copy? Provenance decides whether the clip can be traced, not whether it looks good.

Social chain: ask who has a motive, who is spreading it, and whether the subject has responded. Deepfakes are often a distribution problem: a ten-second excerpt out of context travels further than the full video.

Two conditions, two methods

Breaking news, decide within 30 minutes: freeze the original file and a screenshot of the page; record the URL, publish time, account and download time; do not repost before verification; keep “suspicious” and “verified” in two columns. The goal here is to stop secondary spread, not to complete a forensic examination.

Published material, 1–2 days: ask the publisher for the original file; find a second angle, event photo or long version; mark at least three physical inconsistencies frame by frame; draw the provenance chain as a timeline. You can now give a high/medium/low risk grade, but it is still not a court-grade examination.

Ten checks

  1. At least five seconds were examined frame by frame, not just the thumbnail.
  2. Teeth, ears and blinking were checked separately.
  3. Background text, signs and glasses reflections were checked against the light direction.
  4. The original URL, publish time, account and download time were recorded.
  5. The original file was found, or “not found” was written down.
  6. A second angle or long version was found, or “none” was written down.
  7. The subject or publisher responded, or “no response” was written down.
  8. Suspicions and verified facts sit in separate columns.
  9. The result is high/medium/low risk, not true/false.
  10. Publication says “suspected” or “verified”; speculation is not written as a conclusion.

Alternative

If the original source cannot be found, do not run a “true/false” examination. Produce an evidence-grade note instead: known facts, unknown facts, suspicious points and the next check. “Cannot confirm at this time” is more responsible than a forced verdict. If the material involves law, elections or personal safety, hand it to a qualified institution; do not conclude inside the page.
