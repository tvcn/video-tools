Keying: Edge First, Color Second

Keying checks edges before color. If the edge is mush, grading cannot save it.

Keying starts with the edge, then color. If the edge is mushy, grading cannot save it.

On set

Check the edge first: hair, fingers, a clear cup. Key coarse then fine, feather one or two pixels, and fix spill last.

Do this

  1. Check hair, fingers, and a transparent glass edge.
  2. Do a rough and then a fine pass, adding one to two pixels of feather.
  3. Fix spill and review at delivery size, not at 400 percent.

Avoid

Do not fix flaws at 400 percent that nobody sees at delivery size.

Get it right on set

Light the green screen evenly. Keep the subject 1.5–2m from it and keep shadows off the screen. Light the background first, then the subject; add an edge or rim light to separate hair and shoulders.

Do not push the shutter too high: 1/50 keeps natural motion blur. For heavy keying, 1/100 reduces edge smear but hardens motion. Test one take before committing; do not use one setting for the whole day.

Keying node order

  1. Do a primary grade first and even out the screen’s brightness and colour cast. Do not key a badly underexposed plate.
  2. Key coarse to fine: get the large matte, then fix edges, hair and transparent objects.
  3. Add 1–2px edge blur, then spill suppression; finish with light wrap so background light returns to the edge.

Do not force one keyer on glass, gauze and smoke. Split layers; keep the practical element when you can.

Edge failures

  1. Hair becomes a hard block: raise matte detail, do not just lower tolerance; roto the fine strands separately.
  2. Edges chatter in motion: check shutter and motion blur; fix per frame, not with overall blur.
  3. Green spill on the face: lower screen brightness first, then suppress spill; do not desaturate the whole face.
  4. Transparent objects vanish: split the layer and keep refraction and edge highlights.

Keying acceptance log

  • At delivery size, hair, fingers and transparent objects have no hard cut, jagged edge or black line.
  • Edges track the subject in motion; no frame jumps or sticks.
  • No green bounce on face and shoulders; a white collar stays white.
  • Log keyer, node order, edge blur and spill strength; redo after a lens or screen change.

Keying log

For every green-screen setup log screen type, subject distance, shutter, ISO, edge-light position, keyer and edge blur. After a screen or lens change, shoot a 10-second test before the take.

Judge

This page passes on three observable checks, not slogans:

  • 1. Hair, fingers, and transparent edges show no hard cut or jagged line.
  • 2. The edge tracks through motion without jitter or drift.
  • 3. At delivery size there is no green spill or black fringe.

Failures

Any one of these means stop and fix it before post:

  • 1. Check 1 is only staged (“Hair, fingers, and transparent edges show no h…”); another camera position or location exposes it.
  • 2. Check 2 is left to post or a verbal explanation (“The edge tracks through motion without jitter…”); nothing on set can be rechecked.
  • 3. Do not fix flaws at 400 percent that nobody sees at delivery size.

Extend

10-minute exercise: Take 30 seconds of footage and make an A/B, changing only “Check hair, fingers, and a transparent glass edge.”. Note one timecode and one difference before keeping it.
