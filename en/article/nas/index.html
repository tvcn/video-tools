NAS: Shared Storage, Shared Rules

NAS shares media, so set naming, permissions, and trash rules first; otherwise sharing means overwriting.

For one person and one project, power and management cost exceed a direct SSD. Do not buy complex storage early for a “team” feeling.

What to compare first

  • Bays and redundancy: at least two bays in mirror; a single-drive NAS is not backup, only a network disk.
  • Network: gigabit is enough for a small team; multicam editing needs 2.5G/10G NICs, switches and cabling.
  • Recovery: confirm rebuild after a failed drive and readability of old disks; RAID without a restore drill is reassurance only.

When not to use it

A NAS is a collaboration drive, not the only backup

A NAS solves sharing and versions, not fire, accidental deletion or ransomware. With a team NAS, add at least one offline drive and one offsite copy.

  1. Permissions by project: editors read/write, clients read-only; remove departing staff the same day.
  2. Enable snapshots for 7–30 days. Check a snapshot before deleting; do not empty the recycle bin first.
  3. Use at least 2.5GbE for multi-track editing; gigabit is enough for proxies only.

Acceptance: restore last week's version from a snapshot; unplug the NAS and confirm an offline copy opens. Both must pass before you call it a backup.

On set

Create user groups, read-only permissions, and a recycle bin. Have two people write the same file and test the conflict warning.

Do this

NAS shares media across the team, but naming, permissions, and recycle rules come first; otherwise sharing means overwriting.

  1. Create user groups and read-only permissions.
  2. Enable the recycle bin.
  3. Have two people write the same file and test the conflict warning.

Avoid

Do not give everyone read-write; two simultaneous edits erase a version.

3 checks

  • Groups and read-only permissions are set, not open write for all.
  • The recycle bin is on and deleted files are recoverable.
  • A two-writer test produces a conflict warning or lock.
