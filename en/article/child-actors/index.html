Directing Children: Give a Task, Not a Line Reading

With children, give a task, not an instruction: hand me the cup beats "look sad."

Put child-actor rules on the call sheet: maximum hours, breaks and meals, guardian always present, no closed room alone, no dangerous action.

Protect rest and guardianship before performance

Explain any content the child should not see in advance. Direct through games and concrete actions, not emotional manipulation like “imagine your mother died.”

Limits

A child's nod is not consent. The guardian can stop at any time; if the child becomes distressed later, delete the footage.

On set

Give the child a concrete task and a time limit: put three things from the table into a bag in 30 seconds. Never say act natural.

Do this

Direct children with tasks, not instructions: hand me the cup works better than “look sad.”

  1. Give a concrete task and a time limit: put three things in a bag in thirty seconds.
  2. Do not say “act natural”; state the task and the boundary.
  3. Give feedback right after the take and do not leave the child waiting.

Avoid

Do not ask a child to perform an emotion; a clear task produces the reaction.

Judge

Three signals are observable on set:

  • the want is visible in one action
  • the obstacle escalates inside the same scene
  • delete one expository line and the conflict still holds

Failures

These three moves erase the detail you just built:

  • the want is unclear and the scene is only mood
  • the obstacle is solved by coincidence, not a decision
  • dialogue repeats what the image already says

Extend

10-minute exercise: Write one 30-second scene with only “Give a concrete task and a time limit:” and “Do not say “act natural”; state the task”. Cut every explanation and ask one person to retell what the character wants.

3 checks

  • The child receives a concrete task and time limit, not an emotion direction.
  • Nobody says “be natural” or “look sad” during the take.
  • Feedback arrives right after the task and the child is not left waiting.
