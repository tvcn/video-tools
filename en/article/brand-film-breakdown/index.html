Brand Film Breakdown: Build a World in Ninety Seconds

A nine-beat breakdown of a ninety-second brand film: world, character, want, obstacle, turn, action, resolve, line, hold.

Fix Three Variables First

Fix three variables: ninety seconds, brand film rather than product ad, and one value made visible through action. The trap is “pretty empties plus a slogan”; watch the character and the cost.

Sample note: the timecodes below are a teaching sample for this format's usual length. They do not correspond to one specific film and are not frame-accurate analysis. Use the same positions on your own film; do not treat these beats as the only answer.

Timecode Breakdown: 9 Beats

Watch in order; do not read commentary first. For each beat record four things: picture, sound, job, and steal or do-not-copy.

  1. 0:00–0:05 | Picture: a world already running, no location title. Sound: ambience before music. Job: let viewers enter before being told. Steal: one action establishes the world. Do not copy: a drone wide for “premium.”
  2. 0:05–0:15 | Picture: one person doing one concrete task; wardrobe, hands, tool recognizable. Sound: action close. Job: attach the value to a person. Steal: person before brand. Do not copy: logo first.
  3. 0:15–0:25 | Picture: a concrete result he wants; the object is visible. Sound: one short line or breath. Job: establish want. Steal: want is an object or action. Do not copy: voice-over “we believe.”
  4. 0:25–0:40 | Picture: obstacle appears: time, weather, rules, relationships. Sound: room tightens, music still out. Job: give the value a cost. Steal: obstacle from the real world. Do not copy: invent a fake villain.
  5. 0:40–0:55 | Picture: he changes method, not goal; same action repeated and escalated. Sound: action rises. Job: turn. Steal: turn through action. Do not copy: turn through a slogan.
  6. 0:55–1:10 | Picture: action has consequence; someone gains or loses. Sound: real reaction, light music. Job: prove the value is not a slogan. Steal: consequence lands on one person. Do not copy: slow motion hides the consequence.
  7. 1:10–1:22 | Picture: result state in the same space, relationship changed. Sound: room returns. Job: show the change. Steal: return to the same space. Do not copy: cut to a new landscape.
  8. 1:22–1:28 | Picture: brand/product appears once, in the same frame as the action. Sound: one line, under twelve Chinese characters. Job: lock value to brand. Steal: brand as part of the action. Do not copy: brand as an end-card sticker.
  9. 1:28–1:30 | Picture: hold on an unfinished action or empty frame. Sound: silence. Job: let viewers take the last breath. Steal: stop on the consequence. Do not copy: stop on the logo.

Read the Structure

The spine is world, person, want, cost, turn, consequence, return. It need not finish a plot, but it must make one value visible.

Ask only three questions: what proof arrives in the first ten seconds; whether every ten to fifteen seconds adds information; whether the end lands one action, one change, or one question. If any is missing, a 90 sec film falls apart.

Budget Order of Magnitude

Budget is a rough mainland-China production range, excluding media spend; it is an order of magnitude, not a quote. At the same length, a stage, cast, effects, or music rights can each double the cost.

  • Low: RMB 10,000–30,000, three to five people, one real location, natural light or one light, one to two days.
  • Mid: RMB 30,000–100,000, eight to twelve people, set or controlled location, two to three days.
  • High: RMB 100,000–500,000+, director, camera, art, wardrobe, composer, multiple locations, four to eight days.

Three Structures You Can Steal

Steal structure, not images. These three still work when you change the subject.

  1. Establish the world with one action, not a drone.
  2. Let the obstacle come from the real world; the brand is not a savior.
  3. Return to the same space so the change is visible.

Three Reasons Not to Copy

Not “bad,” but built on different conditions; copying exposes your budget, platform, and audience.

  1. Do not copy “no dialogue, image only”: without brand recognition, viewers do not know who is speaking.
  2. Do not copy a cinematic drone open: without budget, it exposes the location.
  3. Do not copy a value slogan: without the cost before it, it is just a caption.

Breakdown Exercises

Do them before shooting; do not just read. Do the three in order, and the third must become a fifteen-second file.

  1. Watch muted; write one line for the want and one for the cost.
  2. Mark when the brand first appears; if before sixty seconds, ask why.
  3. Compress to thirty seconds: world, want, turn, consequence.

Regional note for US / UK / EU readers

This English page keeps mainland-China examples where they are part of the method. Read platform names as examples, not as the only market: Douyin ↔ TikTok/Reels, Bilibili ↔ YouTube, Xiaohongshu ↔ Instagram/Pinterest; RMB figures are not converted here. Platform names and monetisation rules differ by market; treat Douyin, Bilibili and Xiaohongshu as examples, then re-check TikTok, YouTube, Instagram or the local platform.

3 checks

  • The claim can be opposed, not a universally agreeable platitude.
  • Each shot is marked proof or decoration, with decoration under half.
  • Every product appearance has a reason, not a ten-second exposure timer.
