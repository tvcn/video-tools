Documentary Template: One Person, One Thing, One Change

One person, one concrete task, one visible change. Do not start with an era.

A documentary template is one person, one concrete task, one visible change; do not start with the era.

A usable 60-second segment table

SegmentTimePictureSound / pass line
Person and task0–10sThe person is already working, not standing for an interviewAmbience first; no explanatory music
Process10–30sContinuous action shows steps and difficultyKeep sync sound; interview only supports
Change30–45sA visible result: finished, failed, changed decisionDo not let the subject say “I changed”
Aftermath45–60sThe first action or space after the changeOne true line; no era summary

Fill three lines before rolling

  • One person: name and one task for today.
  • One change: what looks different on camera? Not “he grew.”
  • What not to shoot: list three things that would turn it into an ad.

On set

One person, one ongoing task, one visible change. Shoot the task first, not the interview.

Do this

  1. Choose one person and one task in progress.
  2. Shoot the task before the interview.
  3. Wait for a visible change before fixing the structure.

Avoid

Do not start with historical background; that is usually a thesis, not a film.

Judge

Three signals are observable on set:

  • the first three seconds give a believable reason
  • the middle adds information or resistance, not just mood
  • the ending shows a visible change instead of narrating it

Failures

These three moves erase the detail you just built:

  • the template is filled but the person and problem are not
  • no reason in the first three seconds, only title and music
  • no visible change at the end, only voice-over

Extend

10-minute exercise: Break down one 30-second reference, mark the timecodes for “Choose one person and one task in progress.” and “Shoot the task before the interview.”, then answer: if one disappeared, would the film still hold?

3 checks

  • The film has one person and one task in progress.
  • Interview does not open the piece; action comes first.
  • A visible change occurs, not only the subject saying so.
