Formula and units

File size in GB ≈ bitrate in Mbps × duration in seconds ÷ 8 ÷ 1000. Bitrate is megabits per second, storage counts bytes, so divide by 8; divide by 1000 for decimal GB.

Example: 4K at 100 Mbps for 10 minutes: 100 × 600 ÷ 8 ÷ 1000 = 7.5 GB. At 200 Mbps 4:2:2 10-bit, the same duration is 15 GB.

Three extras

  • Audio, proxies and metadata add 5–15%.
  • Cards and drives are sold in decimal capacity while the OS shows binary; “missing” gigabytes are normal.
  • Multiply by camera count for multicam, not just the A camera.

Limits

With variable bitrate this is an average, not every second. Card speed is sustained write; do not reverse this size formula into a card-speed claim.

On set

Enter minutes and Mbps to get GB. Add 20 percent headroom, then check the card and drive.

Do this

A bitrate calculator converts minutes and Mbps into file size; know whether the drive fits before delivery.

  1. Enter minutes and Mbps.
  2. Calculate GB and add 20 percent headroom.
  3. Compare with free space on card and drive.

Avoid

Do not read Mbps as MB; the factor of eight undercounts the drive.

Boundary

It does not replace an on-set reading. The number is a starting point; the monitor, scope or actual recording decides.

Alternative

If “Enter minutes and Mbps.” is not the main problem, start with the basic move behind “Calculate GB and add 20 percent headroom.”. Upgrade only after both hold.

60-second test

Run a 60-second test with the gear you own, focused on “Enter minutes and Mbps.”, and record one number. If the number does not change, do not add budget.

Check

  • Minutes and Mbps convert correctly to GB.
  • The result includes 20 percent headroom.
  • Free space on card and drive is checked before delivery.
