SRT subtitles protect UTF-8 encoding, timing, and punctuation; wrong encoding shows garbage on platforms.

On set

Save as UTF-8 with millisecond timing. Keep each subtitle under two lines and sixteen characters per line.

Do this

  1. Save as UTF-8.
  2. Time to the millisecond.
  3. No more than two lines per caption, sixteen characters per line.

Avoid

Do not save Chinese SRT as ANSI; platforms show question marks.

Three causes

For “SRT Subtitles: Encoding, Timing, Punctuation”, check three directions first. Change one variable, record a 30-second comparison, and see which link the change follows.

CauseSignal on setFive-minute test

On-set diagnosis

  1. Confirm UTF-8 in a text editor, not the system default.
  2. Watch the whole file in the target player and note the first drift.
  3. Measure the longest line; split anything over sixteen characters.

Fix

SRT is the simplest subtitle format and the easiest place to fail on encoding, frame rate and line length. Make it readable before translating it.

  1. Use UTF-8 without BOM; do not save from Word.
  2. Convert the timeline at the project frame rate and check first and last cues.
  3. Keep 12–16 characters per line, two lines max; never break mid-word.
  4. Use one punctuation system consistently, not a mix.

Failure cases

With “SRT Subtitles: Encoding, Timing, Punctuation”, these three moves make it worse. Pick one replacement action and do not change three variables at once.

FailureWhy it gets worseReplace with
Ship machine translationWord order and terms are wrong and the viewer cannot followDo at least one human pass
Fill one lineThe viewer cannot finish reading and the subtitle covers the imageSplit into two lines
Overlapping cuesTwo subtitles appear at onceCheck in/out times

Judge

Three signals are observable on set:

  • every spec maps to one concrete shooting problem
  • weight, power and connections hold on a real rig
  • a 60-second test leaves one comparable number

Failures

These three moves erase the detail you just built:

  • specs without connections, power or weight
  • batteries, storage or body do not match after purchase
  • no 60-second test, so the problem arrives on the shoot

Extend

10-minute exercise: Run a 60-second test with the gear you already own, measuring only “Save as UTF-8.”. Record two numbers: what you need and what the gear actually gives.

3 checks

  • The file is UTF-8 and Chinese renders correctly.
  • Timing is to the millisecond with no overlaps.
  • Captions stay within two lines of sixteen characters with punctuation.
