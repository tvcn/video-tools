Timecode: Sync Without Guessing

Timecode syncs cameras and recorders in the first minute of post; without it, sync is luck.

What to compare first

  • Sync method: confirm jam sync, wireless sync or audio timecode; different cameras support different modes.
  • Drift: record 30 minutes and compare again; syncing only at start is not a test.
  • Backup: timecode does not replace a clap; cameras without timecode still need manual sync.

When not to use it

For one camera, short clips or easy manual alignment in post, timecode is unnecessary. More devices make the sync chain more fragile.

Multi-camera sync: jam before the shoot, compare after wrap

Timecode is not “set once.” Jam sync before the first take and compare before wrap; battery changes and sleep can drift.

  1. Choose one master camera and jam the others; log the jam time.
  2. Each camera films a clap or hand clap with timecode as a backup sync point.
  3. Before wrap, compare head and tail timecode. If drift exceeds one frame, align by clap in post, not by timecode.

Acceptance: put all cameras on one timeline and pick a random action; frame offset should be one frame or less. More means drift.

On set

Jam the recorder and camera to the same timecode and shoot ten seconds. Sync in post and check for less than one frame of drift.

Do this

Timecode aligns multicamera and recorder in the first minute of post; without it, syncing is luck.

  1. Jam the recorder and camera to the same timecode.
  2. Shoot a ten-second test.
  3. Sync in post and check drift under one frame.

Avoid

Do not jam once and shoot all day; drift shows up in the afternoon.

3 checks

  • Recorder and camera display the same timecode.
  • The ten-second test syncs under one frame in post.
  • A second jam is logged mid-shoot, not only one at the start.
