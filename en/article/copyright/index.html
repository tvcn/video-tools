Rights: Check Five Things Before Publishing

Footage, music, fonts, likeness, and location are five separate permissions. CC is not a commercial license.

The five-item list

Footage: did you shoot it, or is it stock? Music: platform library, CC, or purchased? Fonts: personal free or commercial license?

Likeness: did the on-camera person sign? Location: does the mall, subway, or restaurant allow filming and publishing? One missing item can get the video pulled.

CC is not free commercial use

CC BY requires credit; CC BY-SA requires derivatives to share alike; CC BY-NC forbids commercial use; CC BY-ND forbids adaptations. These conditions often combine.

“Findable online” does not mean “licensed for commercial use.” For commercial work, buy a clear license or use only what you shot or made.

Platform rules and disclosure

Each platform has separate rules for music, ads, AI-generated content, health, and finance. Read the creator policy before publishing, not just other people’s videos.

Label AI generation, digital humans, and synthetic voice as required. Disclosure is not an apology; it tells the audience what they are watching.

3 checks

  • All five permissions have records or clear sources.
  • Commercial scope for music and fonts is written down.
  • AI generation or synthetic voice is labeled per platform.

Go deeper: for people already shooting

Copyright protects expression, not ideas. You can take the same subject; you cannot copy the specific expression.

One step past the field note

Idea and expression

“A person lost in a city” is an idea anyone can use. A specific shot, edit, line, or song is expression and cannot be taken. Changing color, speed, or filters does not make it yours.

Break reference films into structure and rhythm; do not copy shot for shot. Even with different actors, a shot-for-shot remake can infringe.

The clearance chain

Music needs composition, recording, and performance rights; fonts need commercial licenses; stock needs its original license; people need likeness releases; locations need permits. One missing link can take down the whole film.

Keep licenses, orders, emails, invoices, not just a chat screenshot. The scope must state media, term, territory, and whether it can be sublicensed.

Fair use is not a free pass

Under US law, commentary, teaching, and news may qualify as fair use; courts weigh purpose, nature of the work, amount used, and market effect. China’s “fair use” is an enumerated list, not this four-factor test, and other jurisdictions differ. Check the law where you publish; do not treat one country’s rule as global.

“I only used ten seconds” is not a defense; ten seconds can be the core. If licensed material exists, do not gamble on fair use.

Five-minute drill: one film's clearance list

List every asset in a three-minute film: music, fonts, drone shots, faces, locations, brands. Mark each licensed, pending, or cut. Send one email today for the pending items.

Highlight the three pending items in red. Replace what you can, cut what you cannot; do not wait until the night before publishing.

When rights are clear

Rights are clear when every asset has a document, the scope covers your distribution, the term covers your launch window, and the territory covers your media buy. Three pass.

If one item is only a verbal yes, put it in an email and get confirmation. Do not upload before the reply.

Ten copyright checks

  • Ideas can be borrowed
  • Specific shots and lines cannot
  • Recolor or speed change is not original
  • Music: composition, recording, performance
  • Fonts commercially licensed
  • Stock has original license
  • Likeness releases signed
  • Location permit secured
  • License states media, term, territory
  • Fair use depends on jurisdiction

Copyright is not a checklist before publishing. It is elimination from the first second you choose an asset.
