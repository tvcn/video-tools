LUT: A Starting Point, Not a Finish

A LUT is a start, not a finish. Return to primary correction and check blacks and highlights.

A LUT is a starting point, not a finish. After applying it, return to primary correction for blacks and highlights.

On set

Balance exposure and white balance before the LUT. After applying it, check for lifted blacks and stained highlights, then return to primary.

Do this

  1. Balance exposure and white balance before the LUT.
  2. Check whether blacks lifted or highlights picked up a cast.
  3. Fix in primary and then decide how much LUT strength to keep.

Avoid

Do not use a LUT as a repair; it only tints the problem one color.

Kinds of LUT

Separate three kinds: technical LUT (Log→Rec.709, colour-space conversion), creative LUT (a look) and calibration LUT (monitor or printer). A technical LUT belongs at the front of colour management; a creative LUT comes after the primary grade. Do not stack all three in one node.

Confirm the LUT’s input colour space and cube size. Put a Rec.709 LUT on Log footage and black level and highlights will be wrong.

Node order

  1. Convert to the working colour space first; do not apply a creative LUT to camera-native Log.
  2. Do the primary grade: exposure, white balance and black level. Bring the plate back to neutral.
  3. Apply the LUT at controlled strength; then fix black level, highlights and skin with curves or wheels.
  4. Finish with output transform and legalisation; check the waveform and scopes.

LUT misuse

  1. Using a LUT to fix exposure: on a blown plate it only tints the highlight; detail does not return.
  2. Running the LUT at 100%: blacks lift and skin turns orange. Drop to 50–70% and see if it still works.
  3. Applying one LUT across lenses without matching: each lens has its own cast. Match first.
  4. Treating a creative LUT as a technical transform: the colour space is wrong, so everything after is wrong.

Grade acceptance log

  • Before the LUT: exposure is correct and white balance neutral; black level neither lifts nor crushes on the waveform.
  • After the LUT: highlights are not visibly tinted and skin has not gone uniformly orange or green.
  • At 50% LUT strength the image still holds; that proves the base grade is right.
  • Log LUT name, input colour space, strength and node order; check once in Rec.709 before delivery.

Grade log

Log LUT name, input colour space, strength, node position, output transform and viewing standard. Watch the whole timeline in Rec.709 before delivery, not a single frame.

Judge

This page passes on three observable checks, not slogans:

  • 1. The footage is correctly exposed and neutral before the LUT.
  • 2. After the LUT, blacks are not lifted and highlights show no obvious cast.
  • 3. Lower the LUT strength and the image still returns to a deliverable state.

Failures

Any one of these means stop and fix it before post:

  • 1. Check 1 is only staged (“The footage is correctly exposed and neutral b…”); another camera position or location exposes it.
  • 2. Check 2 is left to post or a verbal explanation (“After the LUT, blacks are not lifted and highl…”); nothing on set can be rechecked.
  • 3. Do not use a LUT as a repair; it only tints the problem one color.

Extend

10-minute exercise: Take 30 seconds of footage and make an A/B, changing only “Balance exposure and white balance before the LUT.”. Note one timecode and one difference before keeping it.
