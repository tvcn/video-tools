Prop Continuity: Photograph the Table

Continuity lives in photos: shoot the table, hands, and wardrobe before and after the take.

Prop continuity uses photos and a sheet, not memory

Photograph the full prop layout before each scene: position, count, state—water level, ash, papers, wounds. Reset against the photo before every setup; do not rely on actor or script-supervisor memory.

Keep doubles of consumables: food, drinks, cigarettes, breakables. Every take starts from the same state.

Limits

Continuity is not OCD. The audience will not count water level, but they feel “that changed.” Protect only what they notice.

On set

Photograph the tabletop, the hands, and the wardrobe before rolling. Repeat at the end of every scene, including which way the cup handle faces.

Do this

Prop continuity lives in photos: shoot the table, hands, and wardrobe before rolling and again at wrap.

  1. Photograph the table, hands, and wardrobe before the take, noting the cup handle direction.
  2. Shoot the same frames at the end of each scene and compare side by side.
  3. If something moved, reset it or shoot an insert that hides the break.

Avoid

Do not trust memory for cups and wardrobe; photos are more reliable.

3 checks

  • Cup handle, collar, and table objects face the same way across cuts.
  • Every scene has before-and-after continuity photos.
  • A break is either reset or covered by an insert, not ignored.
