A wedding template runs opening, ceremony, blessing; the opening gives the feel of the day, not a dress close-up.

A usable 60-second segment table

SegmentTimePictureSound / pass line
Opening action0–10sA morning action: tie, shoes, closing a windowAmbience; not a dress still life
Preparation10–25sThe two people prepare in separate spacesLittle dialogue; no sentimental score
Ceremony25–45sThe ceremony happens whole, not in fragmentsKeep vows sync; pull music back
Vow45–55sOne face and the other person's reactionOne complete true line; do not cut it into quotes
Blessing55–60sThe first embrace or exit after the ceremonyNo “happy forever”; leave one concrete action

Fill three lines before rolling

  • Feeling of the day: which action explains today? Not the dress.
  • Vow: which sentence must stay whole? Confirm audio early.
  • True line: who will say one specific blessing? No stock phrase.

On set

Open with the feel of the day, keep the ceremony complete, and leave one true sentence of blessing. No wedding-dress close-up opener.

Do this

  1. Open with an action that sets the day, such as tying a tie in the morning.
  2. Keep the ceremony whole and vows uncut.
  3. Leave one true sentence in the blessing, not a stock phrase.

Avoid

Do not open on dress close-ups; clothing does not say what happened that day.

Judge

This page passes on three observable checks, not slogans:

  • 1. The opening is an action or sound, not a dress still life.
  • 2. The ceremony stays whole and vows are not chopped.
  • 3. The ending holds one specific true sentence, not “happily ever after.”

Failures

Any one of these means stop and fix it before post:

  • 1. Check 1 is only staged (“The opening is an action or sound, not a dress…”); another camera position or location exposes it.
  • 2. Check 2 is left to post or a verbal explanation (“The ceremony stays whole and vows are not chop…”); nothing on set can be rechecked.
  • 3. Do not open on dress close-ups; clothing does not say what happened that day.

Extend

10-minute exercise: Break down one 30-second reference, mark the timecodes for “Open with an action that sets the day,” and “Keep the ceremony whole and vows uncut.”, then answer: if one disappeared, would the film still hold?

Check

  • The opening is an action or sound, not a dress still life.
  • The ceremony stays whole and vows are not chopped.
  • The ending holds one specific true sentence, not “happily ever after.”
