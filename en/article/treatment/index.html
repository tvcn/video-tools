Treatment: Make the Film Visible on Paper

A treatment does not decorate a script; it lets the client see the film's shape, tone, and limits before the shoot.

A one-page treatment answers three questions

One page: who the audience follows, what blocks them, and what change is visible at the end. No shot list, no director's statement; those belong to later steps.

Write in the present tense, one action per paragraph. The treatment passes when the client can retell the story after one page.

Limits

A treatment is not literature. More adjectives leave the client less certain of what you will shoot; replace “warm” and “premium” with visible places and actions.

On set

Use twelve pages: one sentence, three reference frames, the shooting plan, and delivery limits. One idea per page so the client can see the film.

Do this

A treatment is not a script rewritten in fancy words; it lets the client see the film's look, tone, and boundary before the shoot.

  1. Use twelve pages: one sentence, three reference frames, shooting plan, delivery boundary.
  2. One idea per page.
  3. By the last page the client can say what the film looks like, what is delivered, and what is excluded.

Avoid

Do not use “premium, cinematic” as the plan; the client cannot see those words.

3 checks

  • Each of twelve pages carries one topic without text piles.
  • Three reference frames show look and tone.
  • The client can repeat deliverables, dates, price, and exclusions.
