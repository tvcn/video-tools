Subtitle delivery: SDH, multilingual, audio description

Subtitles are not typing; SDH, multilingual and audio description are three different audience tasks.

Subtitles are not typing; SDH, multilingual and audio description are three different audience tasks. One page of "SDH: More Than Dialogue" solves one local problem; joining "SDH: More Than Dialogue", "Multilingual Subtitles: One Language at a Time" and "Audio Description: Say What Matters" exposes the real failure: conditions are missing, too many variables move, and the result cannot be reproduced.

Problem

The problem is not a lack of methods; each step works alone, but together they fail because the condition was not written, too many variables moved and the result cannot be reproduced.

The order is condition, judgement, one variable and evidence. A step is complete only when the next person can repeat it.

Judgement

  • First gate in "SDH: More Than Dialogue": Each caption identifies the speaker and distinguishes phone or off-screen voice.
  • Second gate in "Multilingual Subtitles: One Language at a Time": Door, rain, music, and other key sounds appear in the captions.
  • Third gate in "Audio Description: Say What Matters": Captions stay off mouths and hands, within two lines, and read at pace.

If one gate fails, return to its single page; do not hide an unsolved point inside a longer chain.

Method

  1. Write the condition first: turn "Label speakers: male, female, phone." from "SDH: More Than Dialogue" into three rows: material, environment, acceptance.
  2. Run one minimal action: follow "SDH: More Than Dialogue" for 30 seconds and do not add the second page yet.
  3. Attach the second page: Write ambience as [door], [rain], and music as [music]..
  4. Use the third page as a control: Check placement and reading speed; captions never cover mouths or hands..
  5. Change one variable at a time; write the other conditions down instead of remembering them.
  6. Film the failure too: put failure and success side by side to see which step cannot be skipped.
  7. Ask the next person to reproduce from the record; if they cannot, the record is not finished.

Field test

A reproducible test, not a client case: take 30 seconds of material, make version A using "Each caption identifies the speaker and distinguishes phone or of…" from "SDH: More Than Dialogue", then version B using "Door, rain, music, and other key sounds appear in the captions." from "Multilingual Subtitles: One Language at a Time"; only one variable may differ. Put them side by side and record what breaks first in "Audio Description: Say What Matters": condition, action or acceptance. If it works on the first try, add one real constraint and run it again.

Acceptance

  • Each caption identifies the speaker and distinguishes phone or off-screen voice.
  • Door, rain, music, and other key sounds appear in the captions.
  • Captions stay off mouths and hands, within two lines, and read at pace.
  • Each line holds one language with no stacked bilingual text.
  • Tracks switch independently, or each version is complete on its own.
  • Type size and line count remain readable on a phone without shrinking for both languages.
  • Description lands in dialogue gaps and never covers a key line.
  • Each passage names who moves, where, and what is held.
  • No emotion is interpreted; the listener can judge from action and sound.
  • The condition from SDH: More Than Dialogue fits on one line and can be repeated by someone else.

Alternatives

  • For a single-point problem in "SDH: More Than Dialogue", go back to SDH: More Than Dialogue; do not use the whole chain.
  • To compare two actions, read Multilingual Subtitles: One Language at a Time and leave the third page alone.
  • For acceptance only, use Audio Description: Say What Matters as a self-test sheet.
