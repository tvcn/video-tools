Fashion & Beauty Structure: Six Beats

First decision: when to use it

Fashion and beauty is not a runway clip. Define texture first—fabric, skin, light—and prove it with close-ups and action, not a beauty filter.

Use it when there is one clear subject, one action, and a visible change in the final film. Do not use it when there is only a slogan, no person, and no process to shoot; go back to the brief before rolling.

What happened

  1. The first version used a beauty filter and ring light. Skin had no detail and the product had no texture. We turned the filter off and moved to side window light.
  2. The product entered through one action: squeeze, spread, press. Two seconds each, not the full routine.
  3. The ending did not show “brighter skin.” The model tucked her hair behind her ear and left in daylight, keeping her real skin tone.

The trade-off

We gave up flawless lighting and skin smoothing and put the budget into the model, window light and macro. Skin has information in side light; flat frontal soft light erases it. All efficacy language became “how it feels,” with no medical or whitening claim.

Reusable / not transferable

  • Reusable: beauty films show texture, action and light, not filters; one product proves one sensory experience.
  • Not transferable: skincare cannot copy a clinical before/after claim. Medical, anti-allergy and whitening language needs local regulatory review.

The three most common failures in this format

  1. Filter before skin. Smoothing and whitening erase texture and leave no product proof. Turn the filter off, then light.
  2. Face only, no action. Squeeze, spread and press carry more than expression. One product, one action.
  3. Efficacy promises. “Instant whitening, three-day acne cure” is unverifiable and may be illegal. Use sensory language and texture.

At acceptance, look at skin in side light: are pores and texture still there? If not, light or post has taken the realism away.

Six beats: one job per beat

Write the six beats as six sentences, each answering “what does the viewer know now.” Shoot in order, but cut out of order; do not add empty shots to fill the structure.

  1. Material close-up
  2. Body movement
  3. Product enters
  4. Light shift
  5. Detail proof
  6. Attitude ending

How to shoot it: six tasks

At least one primary shot per beat; do not shoot everything in one framing and rely on jump cuts. Shoot irreplaceable action first—hands, face, machine—then pickups.

  • Material: fabric/skin macro, keep texture
  • Movement: turn/raise hand/look back
  • Product: hand use, no pack rotation
  • Light: same shot soft to hard
  • Proof: apply/blend/edge
  • End: look/leave, no slogan

Common failures: three ways it collapses

1. Logo/drone first: the viewer does not know whose story this is. Replace beat one with a problem or action. 2. Narration in every beat: the images carry no information. Let one action speak. 3. Unverifiable result: “more efficient” with no before/after or third party. Shoot something checkable.

Cost and check: can it be shot in a day

Typically one shoot day plus two to three post days: half a day for primary shots, half for pickups; cut structure first, then sound. Check only three things: six beats complete, one primary shot per beat, result checkable. Do not accept “it feels premium.”

6 checks

  • Each of six beats is one clear sentence.
  • Beat one is a problem/action, not a logo.
  • At least one primary shot per beat.
  • Result has before/after or third party.
  • At least one irreplaceable action captured.
  • Six beats survive the rough cut.
