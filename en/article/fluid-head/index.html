Fluid Head: Pan and Tilt Need Drag

A fluid head is about drag: starts and stops must be smooth, not snappy.

For fast following, tight spaces or long handheld work, a fluid head is worse than a gimbal or body support. It is not a universal answer for “more stable.”

What to compare first

  • Payload and center of gravity: choose by the full rig, not the bare body; a long lens moves the center forward and needs more tilt range.
  • Counterbalance: let go at your working tilt angle; the head should hold or return slowly, not slam down.
  • Drag and locks: pan and tilt drag adjust separately, locks do not drift, and the plate returns to a repeatable position.

On set

Mount the full rig and set the drag. Do a three-second start, five-second pan, and three-second stop; check for a jerk.

Do this

A fluid head's value is damping: starts and stops must be smooth, not flying at a touch.

  1. Mount the full rig and set damping.
  2. Do a three-second start, five-second pan, three-second stop.
  3. Play back and check for jerks at start and stop.

Avoid

Do not back damping all the way out; a touch sends it flying and ruins starts and stops.

Check

  • A 3-5-3 second start-pan-stop runs with the full rig.
  • Playback shows no sudden jerk at start or stop.
  • Damping settings are logged and reproducible at a new position.
