Reference images and first frames

A reference is not a pretty image. It answers who the subject is, the ratio, where light comes from and what the background is. A first frame must also leave room for motion.

Fit and no-fit / 适用与不适用

  • Projects with product/person photos that warp after generation.
  • Shots needing first/last frames to lock composition and set.
  • Not for: unlicensed portraits or web images with brand logos.

Do this / 怎么做

  1. Choose one hero reference: largest subject, sharpest, unobstructed.
  2. Add one environment reference: background, light direction, temperature.
  3. One front, one profile, one full body; not ten selfies.
  4. Place subject at motion start in first frame and end in last.
  5. Use one aspect ratio across references to avoid stretching.

Avoid / 别踩

  • Using a filtered selfie; the model learns the filter, not you.
  • Reference ratio differs from delivery; the person stretches.
  • Using an unlicensed celebrity/influencer photo; legal risk.

Go deeper / 继续往下

References reduce guessing, not enable copying. Front, profile and full body teach face, hair and build; ten selfies from one angle teach one angle and collapse in profile.

First and last frames anchor composition, not expression. Lock position, background and light; leave expression and detail to generation. A frame that is too full leaves no motion room and the model jitters in place.

Checks / 验收

  • Hero reference has a short side ≥1024 and a clear subject.
  • Character has front/profile/full body, not ten similar selfies.
  • Reference ratio matches delivery.
  • Every portrait/product image has a permission record.
