AI Rotoscope: Hair and Edges

Check hair, fingers, and transparency. If those pass, the matte is usable.

Give AI a clean edge before tracking

AI keying is not one click. Shoot a clear edge first: color separation, moderate motion, even light; then track. Transparency, hair and fast occlusion still need manual work.

Check edges every ten to twenty frames and add keys on drift; do not run the whole shot blind.

Limits

AI keying does not replace rotoscope judgment. Whether an edge should be soft or keep motion blur is still a human call; hard edges look pasted.

On set

Test three frames: hair edge, finger gap, clear cup. If the edge flickers, add a manual mask; do not use one global click.

Do this

AI rotoscoping starts with hair, fingers, and transparent objects; pass those three and it is usable.

  1. Test three frames: hair edge, finger gaps, a glass.
  2. If edges flicker, add manual masks.
  3. Do not use one global key; fix the three hardest edges first.

Avoid

Do not deliver a one-click key; hair and fingers flicker frame to frame.

3 checks

  • Hair, finger gaps, and glass edges are clean in all three frames.
  • Edges do not flicker or eat the subject.
  • The three hardest edges have manual masks, not one global key.
