Vlog Breakdown: One Discovery in Ninety Seconds

An eight-beat breakdown of a ninety-second travel vlog: action, place, task, obstacle, failure, discovery, quiet, exit.

Fix Three Variables First

Fix three variables: ninety seconds, RED/Douyin, and the goal is one discovery. A vlog is not an itinerary; find the task, the failure, then the discovery.

Sample note: the timecodes below are a teaching sample for this format's usual length. They do not correspond to one specific film and are not frame-accurate analysis. Use the same positions on your own film; do not treat these beats as the only answer.

Timecode Breakdown: 8 Beats

Watch in order; do not read commentary first. For each beat record four things: picture, sound, job, and steal or do-not-copy.

  1. 0:00–0:03 | Picture: an action already happening: running, opening a door, boarding. Sound: ambience. Job: action first, no drone. Steal: action open. Do not copy: drone wide.
  2. 0:03–0:10 | Picture: place in three facts: where, with whom, what for. Sound: ambience plus one line. Job: set the task. Steal: three facts. Do not copy: voice-over history.
  3. 0:10–0:25 | Picture: first location does one thing: find, wait, ask, walk. Sound: ambience. Job: start the task. Steal: one complete action. Do not copy: three landmarks.
  4. 0:25–0:45 | Picture: obstacle: rain, closed door, wrong turn. Sound: room tightens. Job: make the task harder. Steal: real obstacle. Do not copy: manufacture drama.
  5. 0:45–1:00 | Picture: failure: wrong person/alley, keep eight seconds. Sound: dialogue/steps. Job: make discovery believable. Steal: one real failure. Do not copy: cut all failure.
  6. 1:00–1:15 | Picture: discovery: found/not found, one face/object. Sound: ambience plus one reaction. Job: give the film a landing. Steal: one concrete discovery. Do not copy: music faking emotion.
  7. 1:15–1:25 | Picture: quiet: one unguarded moment/empty frame. Sound: ambience. Job: let the pace land. Steal: one quiet shot. Do not copy: keep adding action.
  8. 1:25–1:30 | Picture: exit: person leaves/car goes/door closes. Sound: ambience out. Job: end. Steal: one exit action. Do not copy: add summary narration.

Read the Structure

The spine is action, place, task, obstacle, failure, discovery, quiet, exit. It is not “where I went,” but “what I looked for, what happened, what I found.”

Ask only three questions: what proof arrives in the first ten seconds; whether every ten to fifteen seconds adds information; whether the end lands one action, one change, or one question. If any is missing, a 90 sec film falls apart.

Budget Order of Magnitude

Budget is a rough mainland-China production range, excluding media spend; it is an order of magnitude, not a quote. At the same length, a stage, cast, effects, or music rights can each double the cost.

  • Low: RMB 1,000–3,000, one person, phone/one camera, one to two days.
  • Mid: RMB 3,000–10,000, one to two people, camera/stabilizer/mic, two to three days.
  • High: RMB 10,000–50,000+, three to five people, multi-camera/drone/post, three to seven days.

Three Structures You Can Steal

Steal structure, not images. These three still work when you change the subject.

  1. Action in the first three seconds, no drone.
  2. One thing per location.
  3. Keep one failure so the discovery is believable.

Three Reasons Not to Copy

Not “bad,” but built on different conditions; copying exposes your budget, platform, and audience.

  1. Do not copy itinerary editing: chronological order becomes a log.
  2. Do not copy a drone at every location: it cuts the task.
  3. Do not copy constant voice-over: it feels for the viewer.

Breakdown Exercises

Do them before shooting; do not just read. Do the three in order, and the third must become a fifteen-second file.

  1. Write “what I looked for” and shoot only that.
  2. Count locations; more than three, cut to two.
  3. Watch muted; do task and failure still read?

Regional note for US / UK / EU readers

This English page keeps mainland-China examples where they are part of the method. Read platform names as examples, not as the only market: Douyin ↔ TikTok/Reels, Bilibili ↔ YouTube, Xiaohongshu ↔ Instagram/Pinterest; RMB figures are not converted here. Platform names and monetisation rules differ by market; treat Douyin, Bilibili and Xiaohongshu as examples, then re-check TikTok, YouTube, Instagram or the local platform.

3 checks

  • The place is identifiable to a street or shop.
  • One person does something visible beyond walking.
  • A discovery or change is nameable, not just prettiness.
