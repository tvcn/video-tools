Animation Case: From Board to Final

Review the case through board, animatic, layout, animation, lighting, and comp, not just the final.

When studying an animation case, note three limits first: budget, schedule, team size. Style is the result of those limits, not the starting point. Translate “beautiful” into trade-offs: fewer characters, fewer sets, repeated shots, limited frame rate.

Read constraints before style in a case study

Then trace one action: how many frames, key poses, reused assets. If you can retell the production path, you understood the case.

Limits

Do not just save the image. A case without a production path is a reference image, not a method.

On set

Compare stage by stage: three board panels, animatic timing, layout camera, animation keys, light, and comp. Log one change per stage.

Do this

An animation case study compares boards, animatic, layout, animation, light, and comp stage by stage, not just praising the final.

  1. Compare three board panels, animatic timing, layout camera, keyframes, light, comp.
  2. Log one decision and one rework per stage.
  3. State what can be reused, not just “it looks good.”

Avoid

Do not look only at the final; the final hides the rework.

Judge

Three signals are observable on set:

  • the speed split between foreground, midground and background reads in one pan
  • a 0.5-second action still carries weight or anticipation
  • turn off any layer and the space still holds

Failures

These three moves erase the detail you just built:

  • more layers, same speed: the space stays flat
  • anticipation without follow-through: the weight is only half done
  • rendering before assets are locked: rework starts at the compositor

Extend

10-minute exercise: Draw three keys at 24 fps, change only “Log one decision and one rework per stage.”, and export two seconds. If the weight still does not read, add anticipation before adding blur.

3 checks

  • All six stages are compared, not just final stills.
  • Each stage logs one decision and one rework.
  • At least one reusable step is written so another person can follow it.
