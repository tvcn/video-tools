Multicam Sync: Timecode and Clap

Sync timecode first, clap as backup; with both, the edit can cut confidently.

Without timecode, use a clap, a line or a flash seen by every camera. Align by audio waveform in the edit; do not drag by “looks close.”

Give multicam one shared event

Match frame rate, shutter and audio sample rate before rolling. Mixed frame rates force speed changes that break sound sync.

Limits

Syncing once is not syncing forever. Long takes drift; re-sync every twenty to thirty minutes or use timecode.

Multi-camera sync: timecode first, clap as backup

The fear is “looks aligned but is two frames off.” Jam timecode if available; if not, clap on every camera. Align waveforms first, then trim frames.

  1. Set one frame rate and timecode before rolling. Do not mix frame rates on one timeline.
  2. Clap three times, one second apart, on every camera. Align on the first clap in post.
  3. After sync, pick a random action and check each camera. One frame is acceptable; two or more means drift.

Acceptance: all cameras film a stopwatch, pause on the same frame; readings should match. If not, return to timecode or frame rate.

On set

Sync timecode and clap once more. Build a multicam clip and switch using the camera map.

Do this

Multicam sync uses timecode first and a clap as backup; with both, the edit dares to cut.

  1. Sync timecode, then clap once more.
  2. Build a multicam clip in the edit.
  3. Switch by the camera map, not memory.

Avoid

Do not rely on timecode alone; one camera off-code throws the whole multicam group.

Judge

This page passes on three observable checks, not slogans:

  • 1. All cameras share timecode with a clap backup.
  • 2. A multicam clip exists with the correct camera count.
  • 3. Switching follows the camera map and every shot's source is nameable.

Failures

Any one of these means stop and fix it before post:

  • 1. Check 1 is only staged (“All cameras share timecode with a clap backup”); another camera position or location exposes it.
  • 2. Check 2 is left to post or a verbal explanation (“A multicam clip exists with the correct camera…”); nothing on set can be rechecked.
  • 3. Do not rely on timecode alone; one camera off-code throws the whole multicam group.

Extend

10-minute exercise: Run a 60-second test with the gear you already own, measuring only “Sync timecode, then clap once more.”. Record two numbers: what you need and what the gear actually gives.

Check

  • All cameras share timecode with a clap backup.
  • A multicam clip exists with the correct camera count.
  • Switching follows the camera map and every shot's source is nameable.
