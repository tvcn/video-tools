Talking Head Breakdown: Hold Attention for Sixty Seconds

An eight-beat breakdown of a sixty-second talking head: claim, reason, example one, example two, counter, proof, application, question.

Fix Three Variables First

Fix three variables: sixty seconds, Douyin/Channels, and the goal is the viewer can repeat one conclusion. Talking head is not reading a script; check whether the first line is the claim, then whether every ten seconds adds information.

Sample note: the timecodes below are a teaching sample for this format's usual length. They do not correspond to one specific film and are not frame-accurate analysis. Use the same positions on your own film; do not treat these beats as the only answer.

Timecode Breakdown: 8 Beats

Watch in order; do not read commentary first. For each beat record four things: picture, sound, job, and steal or do-not-copy.

  1. 0:00–0:03 | Picture: the person is already speaking; first line is the claim. Sound: sync. Job: no chance to leave. Steal: claim first. Do not copy: thank followers.
  2. 0:03–0:10 | Picture: medium, frontal; hands in frame. Sound: one reason. Job: ground the claim. Steal: one counterintuitive reason. Do not copy: three claims in one line.
  3. 0:10–0:18 | Picture: example one: one person/action/result. Sound: sync. Job: make it concrete. Steal: one real example. Do not copy: three people.
  4. 0:18–0:26 | Picture: example two: new setting/person, same conclusion. Sound: one transition. Job: more than one proof. Steal: a second setting. Do not copy: repeat example one.
  5. 0:26–0:34 | Picture: counter: the common objection. Sound: say it aloud. Job: handle objection early. Steal: admit the counter has a point. Do not copy: a straw man.
  6. 0:34–0:44 | Picture: proof: data/demo/before-after. Sound: key number. Job: make the claim credible. Steal: one verifiable proof. Do not copy: three numbers.
  7. 0:44–0:52 | Picture: application: what viewers can do today. Sound: one step. Job: give a takeaway. Steal: one concrete action. Do not copy: a five-step list.
  8. 0:52–1:00 | Picture: return to the person, ask one concrete question. Sound: sync, hold two seconds. Job: direct comments. Steal: one answerable question. Do not copy: “comment below.”

Read the Structure

The spine is claim, reason, example one, example two, counter, proof, application, question. It is not “cover a topic”; it is “leave one usable conclusion.”

Ask only three questions: what proof arrives in the first ten seconds; whether every ten to fifteen seconds adds information; whether the end lands one action, one change, or one question. If any is missing, a 60 sec film falls apart.

Budget Order of Magnitude

Budget is a rough mainland-China production range, excluding media spend; it is an order of magnitude, not a quote. At the same length, a stage, cast, effects, or music rights can each double the cost.

  • Low: RMB 1,000–5,000, one person, one camera and one mic, natural light, half a day.
  • Mid: RMB 5,000–20,000, two to three people, two cameras/teleprompter/light, one day.
  • High: RMB 20,000–80,000+, director, camera, light, post, stage, one to two days.

Three Structures You Can Steal

Steal structure, not images. These three still work when you change the subject.

  1. First line is the claim, no intro.
  2. Every ten seconds adds information: reason/example/counter/proof.
  3. End with a question answerable today.

Three Reasons Not to Copy

Not “bad,” but built on different conditions; copying exposes your budget, platform, and audience.

  1. Do not copy rapid cutting: without new information, speed is empty.
  2. Do not copy quote-speak: if every line is a quote, none is remembered.
  3. Do not copy a thank-you open: the first three seconds are the only attention; do not waste them.

Breakdown Exercises

Do them before shooting; do not just read. Do the three in order, and the third must become a fifteen-second file.

  1. Watch muted; can the first line be captioned as the claim?
  2. Count new information every ten seconds; if none, rewrite.
  3. Compress to fifteen seconds: claim, one example, one question.

Check

  • First line is the claim, not an intro.
  • A reason lands within ten seconds.
  • Two examples each have one person/action/result.
  • Counter is stated directly, not a straw man.
  • Proof is verifiable, not three numbers.
  • Application is one step.
  • Closing question is concrete and answerable today.
