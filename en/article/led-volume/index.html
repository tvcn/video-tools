LED Volume: Track First, Light Second

In LED volume work, lock tracking before lighting. If tracking drifts, no background will stick.

LED volume work starts with tracking, then light. If tracking drifts, a beautiful background will not stick.

On set

Shoot a pan and a push-in first and check background tracking. Match set light to the background direction, then grade.

Do this

  1. Shoot a pan and a push-in to check background tracking and latency.
  2. Match the on-set light direction to the background source before brightness.
  3. Grade last and confirm no scan lines, drift, or color shift in the background.

Avoid

Do not build the pretty background first; if tracking drifts, all of it is wasted.

Boot order and tracking

Match the LED wall refresh to the camera shutter before judging the image. At 24p/25p, 1/50 or 1/60 is common. If scan lines appear, change shutter or wall refresh first, not the grade.

  1. Run a pan, a push-in and a half-circle around the subject with a tracking chart; watch the background edge for lag.
  2. Put tracking latency, genlock and lens-distortion calibration on the boot checklist. Recalibrate after a lens change.
  3. Shoot a test with no subject, only wall and floor, to confirm perspective and horizon.

Wall light and colour temperature

The wall is background, not key light. Keep the key on the front side; run the wall 1–2 stops below the key so green/blue bounce does not tint the face.

Put wall and practicals on the same side of 5600K or 3200K; do not mix warm and cool. Keep the actor at least 1.5m from the wall to reduce spill and moiré.

Virtual-production failures

  1. Tracking drift: the background looks like a sticker and slips when the actor moves. Recalibrate; do not fix it with post tracking.
  2. Scan lines or moiré: shutter and refresh disagree. Change shutter or shooting distance.
  3. Wall bounce: the face turns green. Lower wall brightness; add negative fill or a black flag.
  4. Wrong perspective: horizon and floor do not meet. Adjust camera height and lens; do not stretch the background.

Tracking acceptance log

  • Ten-second pan and push-in: no lag, drift or frame jump at the background edge.
  • Actor at 1.5m from the wall: no visible green/blue spill on the face.
  • No scan lines or moiré at delivery size; artefacts visible only at 100% are not a failure.
  • Log refresh rate, shutter, lens, tracking software version and calibration date.

Boot log

Log wall refresh, shutter, genlock state, tracking software version, lens-distortion calibration date and actor distance. If any one changes, retest tracking and colour; do not reuse the previous setup.

Judge

This page passes on three observable checks, not slogans:

  • 1. The background does not lag or drift during the pan and push-in.
  • 2. Light direction on the subject matches the source inside the background.
  • 3. At delivery size there are no scan lines, moire, or obvious color shift.

Failures

Any one of these means stop and fix it before post:

  • 1. Check 1 is only staged (“The background does not lag or drift during th…”); another camera position or location exposes it.
  • 2. Check 2 is left to post or a verbal explanation (“Light direction on the subject matches the sou…”); nothing on set can be rechecked.
  • 3. Do not build the pretty background first; if tracking drifts, all of it is wasted.

Extend

10-minute exercise: Shoot three 10-second takes by the same window, changing only “Shoot a pan and a push-in to check”. Compare highlights, skin and background; keep the one that is editable.

Check

  • The background does not lag or drift during the pan and push-in.
  • Light direction on the subject matches the source inside the background.
  • At delivery size there are no scan lines, moire, or obvious color shift.
