Performance Scale: Closer Camera, Smaller Action

The closer the camera, the smaller the action; a gesture that works wide looks acted in a close-up.

Framing sets the size of performance

Small action is invisible in a wide; exaggeration looks false in a close-up. Lock the framing, then the scale: wides use body and blocking, closes use breath, eyes and fingers.

When a scene has both, let the actor play two scales. Do not use one scale for every shot size.

Limits

“Cinematic” does not mean permanently restrained. What should be big must read in a wide; what should be small must not mug in a close.

On set

Rehearse the wide with large gestures, then halve them for the close-up. Before the close-up, shoot a hand insert to check the action does not look performed.

Do this

The closer the shot, the smaller the action; a gesture that works wide looks theatrical close.

  1. Rehearse the wide with larger actions so spatial relationships read.
  2. Before the close-up, ask the actor to halve the action.
  3. Shoot a hand close-up first and confirm it does not look performed.

Avoid

Do not use one performance scale for every size; the close-up magnifies acting.

3 checks

  • Wide and close actions differ visibly, with the close halved.
  • The hand close-up looks natural in joints and pressure.
  • Put the close action back into the wide and it feels too small, proving the scale.
