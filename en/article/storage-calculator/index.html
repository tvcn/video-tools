Formula

Record time in minutes ≈ capacity in GB × 8 × 1000 ÷ bitrate in Mbps ÷ 60. This is a decimal-GB ceiling; reserve 10–15% for formatting, audio and the last file.

Example: a 128 GB card at 200 Mbps: 128 × 8 × 1000 ÷ 200 ÷ 60 ≈ 85 minutes. At an 80% safety line, plan 68 minutes.

Field table

  • 100 Mbps: 128 GB ≈ 170 min; 256 GB ≈ 341 min.
  • 200 Mbps: 128 GB ≈ 85 min; 256 GB ≈ 170 min.
  • 400 Mbps: 128 GB ≈ 42 min; 256 GB ≈ 85 min.

Limits

Usable capacity, file system and camera buffer all change the result; long takes also depend on heat and card speed. Do not replace a field test with “record time.”

On set

4K at 100 Mbps is about 45 GB per hour. Add two backups and keep at least 20 percent free on the card.

Do this

A storage calculator uses bitrate, duration, and backup copies; do not trust the camera's remaining-time estimate.

  1. 4K at 100 Mbps is about 45 GB per hour.
  2. Add two backups, so total times three.
  3. Keep at least 20 percent headroom on the card.

Avoid

Do not trust camera remaining time; it assumes the current bitrate and breaks when the format changes.

Storage calculator

Uses decimal GB (1GB=8000 Mb); formatted capacity and recording overhead will reduce it.

Boundary

It does not replace an on-set reading. The number is a starting point; the monitor, scope or actual recording decides.

Alternative

If “4K at 100 Mbps is about 45 GB” is not the main problem, start with the basic move behind “Add two backups, so total times three.”. Upgrade only after both hold.

60-second test

Run a 60-second test with the gear you own, focused on “4K at 100 Mbps is about 45 GB”, and record one number. If the number does not change, do not add budget.

3 checks

  • Bitrate, duration, and backup count use real values.
  • The total includes three copies and 20 percent headroom.
  • The result is written into the call sheet, not read off the camera.
