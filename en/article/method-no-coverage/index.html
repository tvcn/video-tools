You Have No Coverage

Coverage fails when every shot is one size; add wide, close, and insert.

You cannot cut because every setup has only one shot size. Add wides, close-ups, and inserts.

On set

Add a wide, a close, an insert, and a reverse. Get at least two takes of each.

Do this

  1. For each action, shoot at least a wide, a medium, and a close-up.
  2. Add inserts: hands, feet, props, the gap in a door.
  3. Leave three seconds of handle at the head and tail of every take.

Avoid

Do not shoot one long take and wrap; you will have no alternatives in the edit.

Three causes

For “You Have No Coverage”, check three directions first. Change one variable, record a 30-second comparison, and see which link the change follows.

CauseSignal on setFive-minute test
Master onlyOne wide runs from start to finish; by the second line there is nothing to cut toSplit every dialogue beat into two angles
No insertsAction detail lives only in performance and cannot be cutShoot hands, doors, cups, phone screens
No reactionsThe cut only shows the speaker, never the listenerAdd one reaction per exchange

On-set diagnosis

  1. List every line as a cut point: who speaks, who listens, what the hands do.
  2. Play the scene on a timeline and count usable angles per dialogue beat.
  3. Write the gaps as a pickup list: missing wide, insert or reaction.

Fix

Coverage is not more footage; it is one shot for each relationship, action and reaction the cut needs. Secure two angles per beat before chasing beauty.

  1. Shoot one wide per scene to hold positions and spatial relations.
  2. Shoot two mediums per dialogue beat: one favoring the speaker, one the listener.
  3. Add three inserts: hands, prop, environment; one action each.
  4. Add one reaction per exchange with two seconds of handle before and after.
  5. Shoot two clean seconds of tail and head for every transition.

Failure cases

With “You Have No Coverage”, these three moves make it worse. Pick one replacement action and do not change three variables at once.

FailureWhy it gets worseReplace with
Repeat the wideTime stops advancing; the audience sees the same faceCut on inserts and reactions
Cross the line for coverageSpace flips and the audience loses directionDraw the axis before pickups
Pretty empty shotsNo action, so the shot reads as a pausePut one recognizable action in every insert

Acceptance table

Acceptance is not “it feels better”; all four must be identifiable, playable and logged.

CheckPass lineLog
Angles per beatEvery beat has a usable wide and mediumMark the timeline
InsertsEach action detail has its own shotPickup list
ReactionsEvery exchange has a listener reactionReview dialogue block
HandlesTwo clean seconds around each transitionInspect tail/head

Boundary

It does not guarantee a story. The template only orders structure; the person, obstacle and cost still need writing.

Alternative

If “For each action, shoot at least a wide,” is not the main problem, start with the basic move behind “Add inserts: hands, feet, props, the gap in”. Upgrade only after both hold.

60-second test

Run a 60-second test with the gear you own, focused on “For each action, shoot at least a wide,”, and record one number. If the number does not change, do not add budget.

When not to use

Do not use the template when “For each action, shoot at least a wide,” is still unclear or “Add inserts: hands, feet, props, the gap in” is only filler. Go back to the person and the problem.

Three failures

  • Every beat exists, but no one makes a decision.
  • The middle changes location, not information.
  • The ending is narrated, with no visible change.
