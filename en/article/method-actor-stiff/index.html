The Actor Is Stiff

Give a concrete task and make them walk; movement loosens lines.

A stiff actor needs a concrete task and a reason to move. Once the body works, the lines loosen.

On set

Give a task: put the cup on the table, then turn. Make them walk one step and the lines loosen.

Do this

  1. Turn the line into an action: look for someone, open a door, hand something over.
  2. Have them actually do it, not perform the idea of doing it.
  3. Walk it twice before rolling and shoot the most natural rhythm.

Avoid

Do not tell them to relax; the more you say it, the stiffer they get.

Three causes

For “The Actor Is Stiff”, check three directions first. Change one variable, record a 30-second comparison, and see which link the change follows.

CauseSignal on setFive-minute test
Lines before actionThe actor stands still and only moves the mouth; the hands have no jobRemove the lines and give one physical task
Abstract direction“Just relax” makes the body stifferReplace it with a verb: open, hand over, find
Camera fearThe eyes hunt for the lens and the action lands half a beat lateAsk for one take facing away, then turn back

On-set diagnosis

  1. Shoot one silent action take and watch whether hands and feet do the same job.
  2. Then add the lines after the action and compare breathing and pauses.
  3. Shoot one wide and one close. If only the close works, the action has not built a space.

Fix

Replace “play the feeling” with “finish the task”. The actor does not need to feel relaxed first; he needs something that must be done.

  1. Turn lines into actions: find someone, open a door, hand over an object, hide something.
  2. Give the action an obstacle: a stuck door, an overfull cup, a missing key.
  3. Walk it twice before rolling: first silent, then with lines.
  4. Shoot a wide for space, then a close for reaction; do not start with faces only.

Failure cases

With “The Actor Is Stiff”, these three moves make it worse. Pick one replacement action and do not change three variables at once.

FailureWhy it gets worseReplace with
Repeat “relax”The actor performs relaxation; shoulders and hands become less believableName a concrete action and obstacle
Ask for “nervous”Shaking hands and shoulders become a generic trickGive a real deadline or consequence
Ten takes in a rowEnergy drops and the last takes repeatStop at two different rhythms and review

Acceptance table

Acceptance is not “it feels better”; all four must be identifiable, playable and logged.

CheckPass lineLog
Body taskHands and feet do one job, not a poseReview the silent take
Line placementLines follow the action instead of replacing itCheck take two
SpaceThe wide shows who is where and what they doKeep one wide
RhythmTwo takes have different pauses and both are usableLog the difference

Boundary

Alternative

If “Turn the line into an action: look for someone, open a door, hand something over” is not the main problem, start with “Have them actually do it, not perform the idea of doing it”; only upgrade after both hold.

60-second test

Run one 60-second test against check 1: The line is now a concrete action. If check 2 (The actor actually does it rather than performing the idea) does not change, do not add gear or budget.

Check

  • The line is now a concrete action.
  • The actor actually does it rather than performing the idea.
  • Two walk-throughs happened before rolling and the rhythm is natural.
