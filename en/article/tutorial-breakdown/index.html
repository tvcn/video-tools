Tutorial Breakdown: Teach in Five Minutes

A ten-beat breakdown of a five-minute tutorial: result, materials, three steps, check, failure, recap, next, action.

Fix Three Variables First

Fix three variables: five minutes, Bilibili/Channels, and the goal is the viewer can do it. A tutorial is not “watch me”; it is “you can.” Count steps and checks, not facts.

Sample note: the timecodes below are a teaching sample for this format's usual length. They do not correspond to one specific film and are not frame-accurate analysis. Use the same positions on your own film; do not treat these beats as the only answer.

Timecode Breakdown: 10 Beats

Watch in order; do not read commentary first. For each beat record four things: picture, sound, job, and steal or do-not-copy.

  1. 0:00–0:10 | Picture: show the finished result, no table of contents. Sound: result sound/one promise. Job: show what they can do after. Steal: result first. Do not copy: open with an outline.
  2. 0:10–0:25 | Picture: materials/tools, all in frame. Sound: count each. Job: show what to prepare. Steal: all materials in one frame. Do not copy: read ten items.
  3. 0:25–0:55 | Picture: step one, one take or minimal cuts. Sound: key action. Job: let viewers follow. Steal: one complete action. Do not copy: five angles.
  4. 0:55–1:30 | Picture: step two repeats the logic. Sound: one reminder. Job: build a method. Steal: same logic, new object. Do not copy: a new method.
  5. 1:30–2:10 | Picture: step three, the hardest. Sound: slow down, hold key frame two seconds. Job: make the hard part followable. Steal: pause plus close-up. Do not copy: speed through.
  6. 2:10–2:50 | Picture: check: how to know it is right. Sound: three criteria. Job: self-check. Steal: three visible standards. Do not copy: “close enough.”
  7. 2:50–3:30 | Picture: failure: the most common mistake. Sound: wrong sound plus fix. Job: help viewers avoid. Steal: one real failure. Do not copy: act an exaggerated error.
  8. 3:30–4:10 | Picture: recap: three steps on one card/list. Sound: one summary. Job: make the method memorable. Steal: a screenshot-able list. Do not copy: repeat the whole segment.
  9. 4:10–4:40 | Picture: next: variation/advanced use. Sound: one suggestion. Job: continue. Steal: one variation. Do not copy: three advanced paths.
  10. 4:40–5:00 | Picture: action: do one step now. Sound: one action. Job: from watching to doing. Steal: a one-minute action. Do not copy: “try it later.”

Read the Structure

The spine is result, materials, step one, step two, step three, check, failure, recap, next, action. Every step must be followable; if it cannot be followed, it is a showreel, not a tutorial.

Ask only three questions: what proof arrives in the first ten seconds; whether every ten to fifteen seconds adds information; whether the end lands one action, one change, or one question. If any is missing, a 5 min film falls apart.

Budget Order of Magnitude

Budget is a rough mainland-China production range, excluding media spend; it is an order of magnitude, not a quote. At the same length, a stage, cast, effects, or music rights can each double the cost.

  • Low: RMB 3,000–10,000, one to two people, one camera plus tabletop, one day.
  • Mid: RMB 10,000–40,000, three to five people, two cameras/overhead/light, two days.
  • High: RMB 40,000–150,000+, director, camera, art, motion, post, stage, three to seven days.

Three Structures You Can Steal

Steal structure, not images. These three still work when you change the subject.

  1. Result first, no outline.
  2. All materials in one frame.
  3. Hold the hardest step two seconds plus a close-up.

Three Reasons Not to Copy

Not “bad,” but built on different conditions; copying exposes your budget, platform, and audience.

  1. Do not copy fast-cut tutorials: if steps cannot be followed, viewers save and never do.
  2. Do not copy an expert tone: without failure, viewers do not know where it breaks.
  3. Do not copy a ten-step list: five minutes holds three steps; more means none.

Breakdown Exercises

Do them before shooting; do not just read. Do the three in order, and the third must become a fifteen-second file.

  1. Watch muted; can you follow the steps?
  2. Count checkpoints; fewer than three means no self-check.
  3. Compress to sixty seconds: result, three steps, one check, action.

Check

  • Finished result in the first ten seconds.
  • Materials fit in one frame.
  • Three steps, each a complete action.
  • Hardest step has a pause/close-up.
  • Three visible checkpoints.
  • One real failure demo.
  • One one-minute action at the end.

Regional note for US / UK / EU readers

This English page keeps mainland-China examples where they are part of the method. Read platform names as examples, not as the only market: Douyin ↔ TikTok/Reels, Bilibili ↔ YouTube, Xiaohongshu ↔ Instagram/Pinterest; RMB figures are not converted here. Platform names and monetisation rules differ by market; treat Douyin, Bilibili and Xiaohongshu as examples, then re-check TikTok, YouTube, Instagram or the local platform.
