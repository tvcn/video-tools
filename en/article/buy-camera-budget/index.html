Buying a Camera: Budget Before Body

On a limited budget, the body is not first. Find whether you lack low light, autofocus, 10-bit, or audio. The wrong body forces every accessory to change with it.

First decision: which link is missing

If you already have a 4K body and one working lens, do not replace the body first. Ask which of your last three films was lost because the camera was not enough. Low light: fast lens and light first. Audio: microphone first. Autofocus: then the body. Under ¥10k, the body should not exceed half the budget.

Fits:

  • First paid work, ¥8,000–20,000
  • Interview, event, product, 4K delivery
  • 10-bit/Log for a simple grade

Does not fit:

  • 4K120 or global shutter
  • One hour without overheating
  • RAW and dual-card recording

Test protocol: reproducible, no invented scores

Bring your own Log footage and a 64GB card. Spend five minutes on each of the five checks. Do not just hold the body and listen to the shutter; a spec sheet will not show rolling shutter, heat, or AF breathing.

Alternatives and upgrades: do not add money on the wrong axis

  • A fast prime first
  • An audio kit first
  • A used previous-gen flagship

Buying steps: bring footage, not spec sheets

Run these in order before paying. If one fails, do not tell yourself post will fix it.

  1. Rent one day in your usual scene
  2. Test adapters and AF with your lenses
  3. Confirm HDMI, monitoring, and power
  4. Price cards, batteries, cage, monitor
  5. Check warranty and repair turnaround

Check

  • Body is at most half the total budget.
  • Five tests have footage logs.
  • Total includes cards, batteries, monitor, adapters.
  • At least one lens covers the main scene.
  • Heat and rolling shutter are acceptable.
  • Warranty and repair turnaround are known.

Decision tree: project first, body second

Do not start with “which body is best”. Start with what the project cannot lose. Interviews need continuous recording, autofocus and audio; products need resolution, colour and close focus; narrative needs dynamic range, Log and cooling. Pick the wrong project and no body can buy it back.

  1. Write the project type, delivery platform and longest single take.
  2. Choose three pass lines: autofocus, 10-bit, cooling, audio or low light.
  3. Then choose the mount; existing lenses decide the body, not the reverse.
  4. Finally total the cards, batteries, monitor, adapter and repair cost.

Test scope: measured and not measured

Bring your own Log footage, a 64GB card and a common lens. Run five minutes on each of five checks: rolling shutter, overheating, autofocus, battery and audio. Record room temperature, battery level, card type and firmware. Not measured: integrating sphere, noise meter, lab-grade dynamic range. Those numbers are not printed here.

Buying order: rent before buying

Do not let a spec sheet choose the body. Rent once, shoot the project you actually want, then total the cost.

  1. Rent the target body and a common lens once; shoot a real project.
  2. Confirm mount, adapter, autofocus and power.
  3. Total the cards, batteries, monitor, storage and repair.
  4. Keep 10% for surprises and consumables.
  5. Buy the second lens after the first delivery.

Regional note for US / UK / EU readers

This English page keeps mainland-China examples where they are part of the method. Read platform names as examples, not as the only market: Douyin ↔ TikTok/Reels, Bilibili ↔ YouTube, Xiaohongshu ↔ Instagram/Pinterest; RMB figures are not converted here. Voltage, plug, warranty, import duty and radio certification differ by market; check the local spec sheet before ordering gear or batteries.
