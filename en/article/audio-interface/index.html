Audio Interface: Inputs and Gain

Check input count, gain range, and noise floor; a expensive interface with low gain cannot record a whisper.

If you only need one lav into a camera, an interface is an extra link; solve mic distance and gain first. Do not replace recording basics with more boxes.

What to compare first

  • Inputs and gain: confirm the preamp has enough gain for your mic and the noise floor at maximum is acceptable.
  • Channels and monitoring: how many independent inputs, direct monitoring and headphone output; live work also needs a return.
  • Connection and power: is USB bus power stable, or does it need a supply? Will a long cable add noise?

On set

Connect a dynamic mic at 70 percent gain and record thirty seconds of silence. Listen to the noise floor and check the maximum gain.

Do this

An audio interface is judged by input count, gain range, and noise floor; a pricey box with too little gain cannot record quiet speech.

  1. Connect a dynamic mic and set gain to 70 percent.
  2. Record thirty seconds of silence and listen to the noise floor.
  3. Check whether maximum gain is enough for quiet dialogue.

Avoid

Do not count inputs only; without gain, quiet dialogue is all noise floor.

Check

  • A dynamic mic records thirty seconds of silence at 70 percent gain.
  • The noise floor is described, not just “fine.”
  • Maximum gain captures quiet dialogue with acceptable noise.

Failures

Three common failures all come from interface specs that never met the room:

  • 1. Trusting “24-bit / 192 kHz” without measuring the noise floor at maximum gain; quiet dialogue turns into hiss.
  • 2. Running a high-current condenser mic from USB bus power; the interface drops out or produces periodic crackle halfway through a take.
  • 3. Treating headphone monitoring as record monitoring and never watching input level; playback reveals a clipped left channel.

Extend

10-minute drill: connect a dynamic mic, start at 30 percent gain, and record thirty seconds of silence at each step while noting the noise floor. Raise gain until quiet dialogue one metre away is intelligible. With the same headphones, pick the highest step with acceptable noise and no clipping, and write it into the recording note.
