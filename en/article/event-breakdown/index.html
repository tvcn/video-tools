Event Breakdown: Make a Live Event Clear in Ninety Seconds

An eight-beat breakdown of a ninety-second event film: arrival, scale, key moment, detail, people, climax, aftermath, end card.

Fix Three Variables First

Fix three variables: ninety seconds, Channels/institutional, and the goal is “let people who were absent know what happened.” The trap is a schedule record; find the key moment, then reactions.

Sample note: the timecodes below are a teaching sample for this format's usual length. They do not correspond to one specific film and are not frame-accurate analysis. Use the same positions on your own film; do not treat these beats as the only answer.

Timecode Breakdown: 8 Beats

Watch in order; do not read commentary first. For each beat record four things: picture, sound, job, and steal or do-not-copy.

  1. 0:00–0:05 | Picture: arrival: door, check-in, first look. Sound: event ambience. Job: bring viewers into the space. Steal: enter through an action. Do not copy: drone wide.
  2. 0:05–0:15 | Picture: scale: three things show crowd/set/theme. Sound: ambience plus one line. Job: how big, what is it. Steal: three details. Do not copy: a statistics caption.
  3. 0:15–0:30 | Picture: key moment: reveal, opening, first line. Sound: sync. Job: the main event. Steal: one action completes. Do not copy: five angles fragmented.
  4. 0:30–0:45 | Picture: detail: hands, props, screen, table. Sound: detail sound. Job: make the event believable. Steal: three touchable details. Do not copy: stage only.
  5. 0:45–1:00 | Picture: people: audience reaction, crew, guest. Sound: real talk/laughter. Job: turn scale into people. Steal: one reactive face. Do not copy: staged group photo.
  6. 1:00–1:15 | Picture: climax: award, signing, performance, applause. Sound: live peak, music later. Job: emotional peak. Steal: let sync peak first. Do not copy: push with music.
  7. 1:15–1:25 | Picture: aftermath: exit, cleanup, empty venue. Sound: ambience falls. Job: give the event an end. Steal: empty room/chairs/cups. Do not copy: cut straight to logo.
  8. 1:25–1:30 | Picture: end card: theme/date/host, once. Sound: one closing line. Job: land the information. Steal: same frame as the empty venue. Do not copy: three lines of titles.

Read the Structure

The spine is arrival, scale, key moment, detail, people, climax, aftermath, end card. It need not cover every schedule item; it must let absent viewers understand what happened and why it matters.

Ask only three questions: what proof arrives in the first ten seconds; whether every ten to fifteen seconds adds information; whether the end lands one action, one change, or one question. If any is missing, a 90 sec film falls apart.

Budget Order of Magnitude

Budget is a rough mainland-China production range, excluding media spend; it is an order of magnitude, not a quote. At the same length, a stage, cast, effects, or music rights can each double the cost.

  • Low: RMB 3,000–10,000, one to two people, single camera, available light, one day.
  • Mid: RMB 10,000–40,000, three to five people, two cameras plus sound, one to two days.
  • High: RMB 40,000–150,000+, director, multi-camera, vision mixer, post, two to five days.

Three Structures You Can Steal

Steal structure, not images. These three still work when you change the subject.

  1. Open on an entrance action, not a drone.
  2. Three details show scale, not a statistics caption.
  3. Let live sound reach the peak before music.

Three Reasons Not to Copy

Not “bad,” but built on different conditions; copying exposes your budget, platform, and audience.

  1. Do not copy multi-camera switching without a vision mixer; five angles fragment.
  2. Do not copy a schedule film: covering every item means no focus.
  3. Do not copy “VIP first”: viewers recognize people, not titles.

Breakdown Exercises

Do them before shooting; do not just read. Do the three in order, and the third must become a fifteen-second file.

  1. List three key moments and shoot only those.
  2. Watch muted; can the picture explain how big and what it is?
  3. Compress to thirty seconds: arrival, key moment, people, end card.

Check

  • An arrival action in the first five seconds, not a drone.
  • Scale/theme clear within fifteen seconds.
  • One complete key moment.
  • At least three touchable details.
  • One real reaction face, not staged.
  • Live sound peaks before music at the climax.
  • An aftermath/empty venue before the end card.
