Notes to Actors: One Executable Sentence

One executable sentence is enough for an actor; three suggestions add up to none.

One executable note is enough; three suggestions equal none.

On set

Write the note as one change for the next take: walk half a beat slower, look at the cup before the person. Say it, let them do it, and stop talking.

Do this

  1. Write the next take as one change: walk half a beat slower.
  2. Or eyes go to the cup first, then the person.
  3. Say it, let the actor do it, and stop adding explanation.

Avoid

Do not give three notes at once; the actor can execute one.

Three causes

For “Notes to Actors: One Executable Sentence”, check three directions first. Change one variable, record a 30-second comparison, and see which link the change follows.

CauseSignal on setFive-minute test
Too many adjectives“More natural” or “more pain” leaves the actor guessingReplace with one verb and one object
Three notes at onceThe actor fixes one and loses the othersGive one note, shoot, then give the next
Negative notes“Don’t do that” does not say what to doSay what to do instead

On-set diagnosis

  1. Ask the actor to repeat the note; if they cannot, it is too abstract.
  2. In playback, ask what they were doing before giving the note.
  3. Compress the note into one verb sentence: put the cup down, then look at him.

Fix

A note to an actor is not a review of performance; it is the next action. One executable sentence beats five feelings.

  1. Use verb + object + timing: hold half a beat before the door, then look.
  2. Give one note at a time: shoot, review, then the next.
  3. Name what works before the adjustment; do not restart from zero.
  4. Ask the actor to repeat the note so you can hear whether it landed as action.

Failure cases

With “Notes to Actors: One Executable Sentence”, these three moves make it worse. Pick one replacement action and do not change three variables at once.

FailureWhy it gets worseReplace with
Say “be more natural”The actor does not know natural toward whatGive a concrete action
Give five notesOnly the last one survivesOne at a time
Criticise in front of the crewThe actor defends and tightensGive notes privately

Acceptance table

Acceptance is not “it feels better”; all four must be identifiable, playable and logged.

CheckPass lineLog
ExecutableThe note is an action, not an adjectiveActor repeats it
CountOne note at a timeSet log
EffectThe next take shows a visible changeCompare playback
RespectThe note addresses the work, not the personActor feedback

Judge

This page passes on three observable checks, not slogans:

  • 1. Each note contains one executable action.
  • 2. The actor can do it immediately without asking “how exactly.”
  • 3. The next take changes only that one thing and holds the rest.

Failures

Any one of these means stop and fix it before post:

  • 1. Check 1 is only staged (“Each note contains one executable action”); another camera position or location exposes it.
  • 2. Check 2 is left to post or a verbal explanation (“The actor can do it immediately without asking…”); nothing on set can be rechecked.
  • 3. Do not give three notes at once; the actor can execute one.

Extend

10-minute exercise: Write one 30-second scene with only “Write the next take as one change: walk” and “Or eyes go to the cup first, then”. Cut every explanation and ask one person to retell what the character wants.
