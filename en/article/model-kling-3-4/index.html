Kling 3.x / 4.0: people, motion and 4K

Kling 4.0 extends single-pass duration to 30s with 10 keyframes and 4K; Kling 3.0 remains the cheaper 15s multi-shot option. People and motion are its home turf.

Fit and no-fit / 适用与不适用

  • Performance, dance/motion, short-drama multi-shot, 15–30s continuous scenes.
  • Shots needing keyframes for narrative beats or 4K/HDR delivery.
  • Not for: pure product macro, text-heavy screen capture, ultra-low-cost batch.

Do this / 怎么做

  1. Use Kling 3.0 for the 10–15s motion structure and character first.
  2. Switch to 4.0 only for 15–30s or ten-keyframe control.
  3. Place keyframes at story beats, not every two seconds.
  4. Use reference images plus a locked description, not repeated rerolls.
  5. Use 4K only for finals; draft at 720p/1080p.

Avoid / 别踩

  • Treating 30s as “one prompt tells a story”; it still needs beats.
  • Keyframes too dense; motion becomes a slideshow.
  • Using a low-res selfie as the character anchor; the face starts soft.

Go deeper / 继续往下

Kling is strongest when people move. Walking, turning, dance and fights are where models slide or turn rubbery; Kling 4.0 improves dynamics and physical interaction, but still needs action split into preparation, event and follow-through.

Kling 4.0's 30s is not free: longer clips drift more in character, wardrobe and light. Write it as three 10s beats, one action and one camera per beat, keyframes only at transitions; stability beats a single 30s take.

Checks / 验收

  • Use 3.0 under 15s; use 4.0 only beyond 15s or for ten keyframes.
  • Leave at least two seconds of motion between keyframes.
  • Character reference has a short side ≥1024 and clear face, wardrobe and hair.
  • 4K only for finals; drafts at 1080p or below.
