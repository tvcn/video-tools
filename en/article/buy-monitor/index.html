Buying a Monitor: Brightness, Gamut, and Latency

A monitor is not a bigger screen. It must be bright enough outdoors, calibratable, and low-latency for focus; if one fails, the camera screen is better.

First decision: which link is missing

500 nits is enough indoors; outdoors needs 1000+ or a hood. Do not buy on specs: bring the camera, your Log footage, load a LUT, and shoot a moving object to check latency.

Fits:

  • Exterior/high light, high nits
  • Grade/multicam, LUT and calibration
  • Focus pulling, low latency

Does not fit:

  • Indoor locked-off, camera screen is enough
  • Client viewing only, no calibration
  • Very low budget, buy light/audio first

Test conditions: how to check before buying

This is not a cross-brand shootout and it does not settle brands. These three checks are a field protocol you can repeat in a shop or with a loaner; cells without measurements say “not measured” rather than inventing numbers. Record body, firmware, room temperature, recording format and card; change one condition and the result can change.

#How to testStatus
1Full white field for peak brightness and uniformityNot measured
2Connect camera; measure 4K input latencyNot measured
3Check shadows and focus assist under the hoodNot measured

Pass line: Visible outdoors, latency does not break follow focus, and shadows keep separation.

Sources: editorial standards · test protocols; manufacturer specs come from public spec sheets, and untested values are not turned into numbers.

If one of the three can only be answered by “I heard” or a spec sheet, do not order yet. Write the condition into your shoot plan, then decide whether it changes your kit.

Test protocol: reproducible, no invented scores

Take the monitor to a window/outdoors and see whether shadows remain visible at 1000 nits. Connect the camera, load the project LUT, compare with the camera screen. Shoot a moving object and check smear/lag.

Alternatives and upgrades: do not add money on the wrong axis

  • Camera screen + hood
  • Rent a high-bright monitor
  • Recorder with monitoring first

Buying steps: bring footage, not spec sheets

Run these in order before paying. If one fails, do not tell yourself post will fix it.

  1. Bring camera and Log footage
  2. View at a window at 1000 nits
  3. Load 3D LUT and compare
  4. Moving object latency
  5. Confirm SDI/power

Regional note for US / UK / EU readers

This English page keeps mainland-China examples where they are part of the method. Read platform names as examples, not as the only market: Douyin ↔ TikTok/Reels, Bilibili ↔ YouTube, Xiaohongshu ↔ Instagram/Pinterest; RMB figures are not converted here. Voltage, plug, warranty, import duty and radio certification differ by market; check the local spec sheet before ordering gear or batteries.

3 checks

  • At least 1000 nits with Rec.709 and HLG support.
  • Waveform, false color, LUTs, and ports match the camera.
  • A hood and NP-F batteries were tested outdoors for ten minutes.
