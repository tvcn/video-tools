Travel Kit

Travel light: one body, one lens, small stabilizer, two batteries, one ND, one pouch.

Weight budget before focal length

The first constraint on a travel shoot is the bag and the walk, not the number of lenses. If body, two lenses, audio and tripod exceed what you can lift after an hour, you start missing shots.

  • Light: one body, one 24–70mm-equivalent zoom, one small shotgun or lav, one ND, two cards, one SSD, one lightweight reversing tripod.
  • Full: add one wide or tele, spare batteries and a small fill light; everything fits one carry-on.
  • Leave behind: a drone with no shot plan, a third duplicate focal length, and lights the location cannot power.

Three things at the end of each day

  1. Copy the day to the SSD, then play back from the card to confirm the files are not empty.
  2. Charge batteries; format a card only after the backup is verified.
  3. Write tomorrow's first location and light time; do not decide at the location.

Field test

Before leaving, wear the final bag, walk ten minutes and climb one floor, then shoot 30 seconds. If a strap cuts, the tripod wobbles or an audio cable is missing, remove gear at the table, not at the destination.

On set

One body, one lens, a small gimbal, two batteries, ND, and a waist bag. Weigh it and walk a day before going.

Avoid

Do not carry three lenses “just in case”; after half a day you cannot shoot anything.

3 checks

  • Total weight is measured and survives a full-day walk.
  • One body and one lens only, no extra optics.
  • Batteries, ND, and gimbal sit in the waist bag for one-hand access.
