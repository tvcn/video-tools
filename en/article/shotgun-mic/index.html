Shotgun Mic: Point It, Get Close

A shotgun is directional; distance gives you room, not dialogue. Get close before denoising.

In hard-wall rooms, seated groups or when the mic must be hidden, a lav beats a shotgun. Do not use one shotgun for every scene.

What to compare first

  • Pattern and off-axis: check pickup angle and off-axis rejection; too narrow misses movement, too wide collects the room.
  • Power and output: confirm phantom power, battery and output level; camera input gain may not be enough.
  • Wind protection and boom: a shotgun needs a blimp, furry cover and shock mount; without isolation, handling noise enters every line.

On set

Record the same line at one, two, and three meters. Check the directionality on playback; past two meters, switch to a lav or boom.

Do this

A shotgun is a directional tool; far away it only captures ambience. Move closer before discussing noise reduction.

  1. Record one line at one, two, and three meters.
  2. Play back for directionality and ambience ratio.
  3. Beyond two meters, switch to a lav or boom.

Avoid

Do not rescue a distant shotgun with noise reduction; dialogue is already buried in ambience.

Check

  • One line is recorded at one, two, and three meters.
  • Playback reveals dialogue buried past two meters.
  • Shots beyond two meters have a lav or boom alternative.
