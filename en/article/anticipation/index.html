Anticipation: Show the Next Move

Anticipation tells the audience what is coming; without it, action feels pasted in.

Before an action, play a small opposite move: crouch before a jump, pull the hand back before a punch, lean back before a run. The reverse frame lets the audience anticipate force.

Go opposite before going forward

Keep it short, usually two to four frames; too long reads as hesitation, too short is invisible.

Limits

Not every action needs anticipation. In fast consecutive action it drags; use it before key moves only.

Anticipation leads by only 2–4 frames

Too much anticipation and the viewer guesses; too little and the action pops. At 24fps, give the main action a small opposite move 2–4 frames before it starts.

  1. Press the shoulder three frames before raising the arm; bend the knees four frames before a jump; move the eyes two frames before a head turn.
  2. The anticipation is smaller than the main action and moves the other way. Equal size turns it into two main actions.
  3. In a fast cut, skip the anticipation and use sound or a glance instead.

Acceptance: cover the main action and watch only the lead-in. The viewer should know something is about to happen, but not exactly how.

On set

Add four to six frames of reverse anticipation: the body moves five centimeters back before going forward. Let the audience predict the direction.

Do this

Anticipation tells the audience what is about to happen; without it, action feels cut in.

  1. Add four to six frames of reverse anticipation before the move.
  2. Move the body back five centimeters, then forward.
  3. Let the audience predict direction instead of hiding it with a cut.

Avoid

Do not replace anticipation with a cut; without prediction, the action has no force.

Judge

Three signals are observable on set:

  • the speed split between foreground, midground and background reads in one pan
  • a 0.5-second action still carries weight or anticipation
  • turn off any layer and the space still holds

Failures

These three moves erase the detail you just built:

  • more layers, same speed: the space stays flat
  • anticipation without follow-through: the weight is only half done
  • rendering before assets are locked: rework starts at the compositor

Extend

10-minute exercise: Draw three keys at 24 fps, change only “Move the body back five centimeters, then forward.”, and export two seconds. If the weight still does not read, add anticipation before adding blur.

3 checks

  • Four to six frames of reverse action precede the main move.
  • The body goes back before forward and the direction is predictable.
  • Remove anticipation and the action feels sudden, proving it worked.
