Blockout: Proportions and Camera First

Blockout sets scale, camera, and timing before detail. Get it wrong and everything is redone.

Lock scale, camera and timing first

A blockout solves three things: character-to-set scale, camera placement and how long the action takes. Use spheres, boxes and simple props; do not add detail yet.

Make a playblast with no materials or lights and check silhouette and composition. If it does not read now, detail will not make it read later.

Limits

Blockout is not a low-poly final. Do not fight over faces and cloth here; scale, weight and camera must already work.

Blockout watches only three things

Blockout is not a low-poly preview; it decides scale, camera and shot order. Use gray boxes or mannequins with no materials or lights. Get these three wrong and everything downstream redoes.

  1. Scale: compare character and set height with a known measurement; do not eyeball it.
  2. Camera: lock lens and height before motion; one shot solves one spatial relationship.
  3. Order: cut by edit point, not by production convenience; build the version that tells the story.

Acceptance: play the blockout with all materials off. You should still understand who is where and what they are doing. If not, fix the camera before animation.

On set

Block the figure with three boxes: head, chest, pelvis. Lock the camera and a ten-second duration before modeling.

Do this

Blockout fixes proportion, camera, and duration before detail; a wrong blockout reworks everything.

  1. Use box figures to set head, chest, and pelvis proportions.
  2. Lock the camera and a ten-second duration.
  3. Only model after proportion and camera are approved.

Avoid

Do not animate performance before the camera is locked; it will be reworked.

3 checks

  • The box figure reads head, chest, and pelvis proportions.
  • Camera and ten-second duration are locked with screenshots and settings.
  • The final shot matches the blockout camera without a composition change.

Go deeper: for people already shooting

Blockout is not a draft. It is the simplest shapes deciding what happens in the shot.

One step past the field note

Proportion and camera first

Use boxes for person, door, table; set height and distance first. If the person-to-door relationship is wrong, accurate modeling will not save it. Check at the real focal length; do not hide the problem with a wide lens.

Set three cameras: main, reverse, one detail. When all three explain the space, continue.

Blocking in time

Do not pose one frame. Walk a simple proxy through the shot and mark start, turn, end. Give each beat one action, not “walks over.”

Shoot a live-action version on the phone and compare it with the blockout. If a person cannot do it, animation will look fake too.

Gray box solves three things

Space, proportion, motion. Material, light, and detail do not belong in blockout. Adding materials early makes you unwilling to change structure.

Blockout passes when, muted, someone can say who is where, what they want, and what blocks them.

Five-minute drill: one room, three cameras

Build one room, one person, one obstacle. Set three cameras, each solving one piece of information. Render three gray frames; no materials.

Show them to one person and ask where the person wants to go and what blocks them. If they cannot answer, change the camera, not the model.

When the blockout passes

Muted, someone who has not read the script can say who is where, what they want, what blocks them, and why the camera is there. Four answers right means the blockout passes.

If they cannot answer, change the camera and blocking; do not add model detail. Detail makes the problem harder to see, not gone.

Ten blockout checks

  • Boxes set person and door height
  • Distance relationship first
  • Composition at real focal length
  • Main, reverse, detail cameras
  • Proxy walks the shot
  • Start, turn, end marked
  • One action per beat
  • Phone live-action comparison
  • No material or light in gray box
  • Muted, viewer knows who, where, wants what

The only job of blockout is to stop everyone downstream from guessing the space.
