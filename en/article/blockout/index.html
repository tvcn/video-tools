Blockout: Proportions and Camera First

Blockout sets scale, camera, and timing before detail. Get it wrong and everything is redone.

A blockout solves three things: character-to-set scale, camera placement and how long the action takes. Use spheres, boxes and simple props; do not add detail yet.

Lock scale, camera and timing first

Make a playblast with no materials or lights and check silhouette and composition. If it does not read now, detail will not make it read later.

Limits

Blockout is not a low-poly final. Do not fight over faces and cloth here; scale, weight and camera must already work.

Blockout watches only three things

Blockout is not a low-poly preview; it decides scale, camera and shot order. Use gray boxes or mannequins with no materials or lights. Get these three wrong and everything downstream redoes.

  1. Scale: compare character and set height with a known measurement; do not eyeball it.
  2. Camera: lock lens and height before motion; one shot solves one spatial relationship.
  3. Order: cut by edit point, not by production convenience; build the version that tells the story.

Acceptance: play the blockout with all materials off. You should still understand who is where and what they are doing. If not, fix the camera before animation.

On set

Block the figure with three boxes: head, chest, pelvis. Lock the camera and a ten-second duration before modeling.

Do this

Blockout fixes proportion, camera, and duration before detail; a wrong blockout reworks everything.

  1. Use box figures to set head, chest, and pelvis proportions.
  2. Lock the camera and a ten-second duration.
  3. Only model after proportion and camera are approved.

Avoid

Do not animate performance before the camera is locked; it will be reworked.

Judge

Three signals are observable on set:

  • the speed split between foreground, midground and background reads in one pan
  • a 0.5-second action still carries weight or anticipation
  • turn off any layer and the space still holds

Failures

These three moves erase the detail you just built:

  • more layers, same speed: the space stays flat
  • anticipation without follow-through: the weight is only half done
  • rendering before assets are locked: rework starts at the compositor

Extend

10-minute exercise: Draw three keys at 24 fps, change only “Lock the camera and a ten-second duration.”, and export two seconds. If the weight still does not read, add anticipation before adding blur.

3 checks

  • The box figure reads head, chest, and pelvis proportions.
  • Camera and ten-second duration are locked with screenshots and settings.
  • The final shot matches the blockout camera without a composition change.
