Talking Head Template: One Claim, Three Proofs, One Action

A usable 60-second segment table

SegmentTimePictureSound / pass line
Claim0–3sMedium frontal, subject to camera, one object on the deskOne claim under 15 words; no reading; repeatable
Example 13–13sHand or object close-up, one complete actionSync action sound; the image proves it without narration
Example 213–23sNew space or angle, same claimOne explanation, max 20 words; no second center
Example 323–33sCounter-example or before/afterDo not mock the counter-example; name the difference
Action33–45sOne imitable action or a single entry pointOne next step only; no slogan

Fill three lines before rolling

  • Claim: which sentence should remain? Write it, cut to fifteen words.
  • Three proofs: for each, write what the camera sees, not “it is convincing.”
  • One action: what can the viewer do now? Open settings, comment or buy—pick one.

On set

Write one claim, three shootable examples, and one closing action. Keep each example under ten seconds.

Do this

A talking-head template is one claim, three examples, one action; examples must be visible on camera.

  1. Write one claim that fits in a sentence.
  2. Add three shootable examples, each under ten seconds.
  3. End with one action, such as open settings now.

Avoid

Do not use three adjectives as examples; if the camera cannot show it, viewers will not remember.

3 checks

  • The claim fits one sentence with no second center.
  • All three examples are filmable and each runs under ten seconds.
  • The ending gives one action so viewers know what to do next.
