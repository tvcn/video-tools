Directing Debut: Protect Performance Before Coverage

On a first directing job, protect the performance and the scene before chasing more coverage.

Your first feature should be the one you can finish

First-time directors overestimate three things: shoot days, actor availability and post patience. Write three to five scenes, two to three characters, one main location. Every extra location adds a permit, a move and weather risk.

Compress the must-get list to one page and delete every “nice to have” shot before rolling.

Limits

Low budget is not an excuse. Fewer locations is not less content; spend the saved time on rehearsal and sound, not extra shots.

On set

On day one, stage one scene only. Shoot a wide and two close-ups; simplify light and camera so the actor gets full performance time.

Do this

On a first directing job, protect the actor and one scene before adding setups.

  1. Schedule only one scene on day one.
  2. Shoot a wide and two close-ups so a full performance exists.
  3. Simplify light and camera and leave time for rehearsal and another take.

Avoid

Do not schedule ten scenes on day one; more setups mean no complete performance.

3 checks

  • Day one has one scene and the actor gets full rehearsal time.
  • A wide and two close-ups exist, so more than one complete performance is usable.
  • Light and setup count stay low enough to wrap on time.
