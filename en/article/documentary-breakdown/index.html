Documentary Breakdown: One Person, One Event, One Change

An eight-beat breakdown of a three-minute documentary: action, person, want, obstacle, attempt, cost, change, after.

Fix Three Variables First

Fix three variables: three minutes, Bilibili/Channels, and the goal is not “profile a person” but “see what changed.” Count actions and waiting, not interview quotes.

Sample note: the timecodes below are a teaching sample for this format's usual length. They do not correspond to one specific film and are not frame-accurate analysis. Use the same positions on your own film; do not treat these beats as the only answer.

Timecode Breakdown: 8 Beats

Watch in order; do not read commentary first. For each beat record four things: picture, sound, job, and steal or do-not-copy.

  1. 0:00–0:10 | Picture: an action already in progress: hands, door, rain, tool. Sound: sync, no explanation. Job: let viewers see before being told. Steal: start mid-action. Do not copy: wide plus narrator introducing the person.
  2. 0:10–0:30 | Picture: person and place; three facts: where, with whom, what for. Sound: ambience plus one or two lines. Job: establish a person, not a résumé. Steal: let them work. Do not copy: let the interview explain identity.
  3. 0:30–0:55 | Picture: a concrete result wanted; the object is visible. Sound: action close. Job: establish want. Steal: want is something filmable. Do not copy: want is an abstract ideal.
  4. 0:55–1:30 | Picture: obstacle appears: time, money, body, relationships; do not resolve quickly. Sound: room tightens, no music. Job: make waiting cost something. Steal: let obstacle happen. Do not copy: manufacture conflict.
  5. 1:30–2:00 | Picture: he tries once, fails or partly succeeds. Sound: real reaction. Job: show method. Steal: one complete attempt. Do not copy: fragment it into a montage.
  6. 2:00–2:25 | Picture: cost appears: time passes, something breaks, a relationship changes. Sound: ambience, silence. Job: change is not free. Steal: mark cost with an object or time. Do not copy: use music to force tears.
  7. 2:25–2:45 | Picture: the same action after the change; result differs. Sound: sync. Job: make change visible. Steal: repeat the opening action. Do not copy: narrate “he grew.”
  8. 2:45–3:00 | Picture: stop after the change, no answer. Sound: ambience out. Job: let viewers judge. Steal: stop as the person keeps working. Do not copy: stop on an interview summary.

Read the Structure

The spine is action, person, want, obstacle, attempt, cost, change, after. Interview only fills what action cannot explain; if an interview can be cut, the film is better.

Ask only three questions: what proof arrives in the first ten seconds; whether every ten to fifteen seconds adds information; whether the end lands one action, one change, or one question. If any is missing, a 3 min film falls apart.

Budget Order of Magnitude

Budget is a rough mainland-China production range, excluding media spend; it is an order of magnitude, not a quote. At the same length, a stage, cast, effects, or music rights can each double the cost.

  • Low: RMB 5,000–20,000, two to three people, one real location, natural light, two to three days.
  • Mid: RMB 20,000–80,000, four to six people, multiple locations, basic light/sound, five to ten days.
  • High: RMB 80,000–300,000+, director, camera, sound, producer, travel, fifteen to thirty days.

Three Structures You Can Steal

Steal structure, not images. These three still work when you change the subject.

  1. Start mid-action, not with a profile.
  2. Let the obstacle happen; do not manufacture conflict.
  3. Repeat the opening action so change shows itself.

Three Reasons Not to Copy

Not “bad,” but built on different conditions; copying exposes your budget, platform, and audience.

  1. Do not copy interview-driven structure: without action, interview is radio.
  2. Do not copy “slow cinema” waiting: without cost, a long take is just slow.
  3. Do not copy a thematic summary: viewers do not need you to conclude a person.

Breakdown Exercises

Do them before shooting; do not just read. Do the three in order, and the third must become a fifteen-second file.

  1. Watch muted; write what the person wants and what blocks it.
  2. Count complete actions; fewer than three means too much interview.
  3. Copy the opening action and check whether the end repeats or changes it.

Regional note for US / UK / EU readers

This English page keeps mainland-China examples where they are part of the method. Read platform names as examples, not as the only market: Douyin ↔ TikTok/Reels, Bilibili ↔ YouTube, Xiaohongshu ↔ Instagram/Pinterest; RMB figures are not converted here. Platform names and monetisation rules differ by market; treat Douyin, Bilibili and Xiaohongshu as examples, then re-check TikTok, YouTube, Instagram or the local platform.

3 checks

  • The change has a minute and second.
  • Before and after show different behavior on screen.
  • If nothing changed by minute ten, the cut is already shorter.
