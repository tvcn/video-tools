Record thirty seconds of empty room per space

Room tone is not silence; it is the space's floor. At each main setup, record thirty seconds with everyone still and quiet, same mic, same gain. Use it to fill gaps between lines so the sound does not die.

Re-record after changing room, mic position or gain; tones from different conditions cannot be mixed.

Limits

Room tone cannot fix noise inside dialogue. It supplies continuous floor; wind, echo or hum on the line needs a re-record or separate repair.

On set

Record thirty seconds per scene with the crew still. Listen back for air conditioning and refrigerator noise.

Do this

Record thirty seconds of room tone per scene with the crew silent; without it, dialogue cuts sound like a vacuum.

  1. Record thirty seconds per scene.
  2. The whole crew stays still and silent.
  3. Play back and confirm no air conditioner or refrigerator hum.

Avoid

Do not grab room tone during a move; footsteps and chatter come with it.

Judge

This page passes on three observable checks, not slogans:

  • 1. Every scene has a thirty-second room-tone file.
  • 2. The crew stays still with no footsteps or chatter.
  • 3. Playback contains no sustained air-conditioner or fridge hum.

Failures

Any one of these means stop and fix it before post:

  • 1. Check 1 is only staged (“Every scene has a thirty-second room-tone file”); another camera position or location exposes it.
  • 2. Check 2 is left to post or a verbal explanation (“The crew stays still with no footsteps or chat…”); nothing on set can be rechecked.
  • 3. Do not grab room tone during a move; footsteps and chatter come with it.

Extend

10-minute exercise: Run a 60-second test with the gear you already own, measuring only “Record thirty seconds per scene.”. Record two numbers: what you need and what the gear actually gives.

Check

  • Every scene has a thirty-second room-tone file.
  • The crew stays still with no footsteps or chatter.
  • Playback contains no sustained air-conditioner or fridge hum.
