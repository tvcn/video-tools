AI Looks Uncanny

Check eyes, hands, lip sync, and motion; any one breaks the illusion.

3 min read · Field noteTVCN EditorialEdited 2026-11

  2. A two-second test

  4. On set
  5. Do this
  6. Avoid

Identify which kind of “fake”

  • Skin and light: too-clean skin, catchlights in the wrong place, background and subject lit from different directions. Fix the source first, not with more smoothing.
  • Motion: knees bending backwards, fingers merging, clothes without inertia. Shorten the action or shoot it live.
  • Detail: garbled text, asymmetric earrings, the wrong number of teeth. Hide it wide; replace it close.

A two-second test

Take the frame closest to the face and place it beside a live-action frame from the same scene on a phone. If you notice skin or catchlights before performance, it is not usable. Then watch the clip muted and check whether the motion gives it away.

When not to use it

Do not replace a hero close-up, long dialogue, a real interview or a news scene with generated images. Use AI for inserts, backgrounds, concept frames and pickups that cannot be shot.

On set

Check eyes, hands, lips, and motion. If any one breaks, regenerate or change the shot.

Do this

An AI image feels fake: check eyes, hands, lip sync, and motion. When one of these breaks, the audience leaves.

  1. Zoom into eye highlights, finger joints, teeth, and ears.
  2. Check lip closure and motion direction, especially turns and walks.
  3. Reframe the failure: hands out of frame, face in profile, shorter shot.

Avoid

Do not hide it with filters or skin smoothing; the more you polish, the faker it gets.

3 checks

  • Eye highlights, finger joints, teeth, and ears were checked at full size.
  • Lip closure and motion direction were checked.
  • Failures were reframed: hands out, profile, or a shorter shot.
