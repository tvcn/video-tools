Cloud Render: Cost and Transfer

Calculate upload time and output cost; with big media, upload is slower than render.

2 min read · Field noteTVCN Tools DeskEdited 2026-11

Do three calculations

  • Upload: total media times upstream bandwidth is often longer than the render; test real upload, not the plan's claim.
  • Render: priced by frame, node minute or GPU hour; run one ten-second test shot to get the real unit cost.
  • Download and rerun: failures, versions and final download all count; first-pass pricing underestimates.

When not to use it

With small media, short renders and a team in one city, a local machine is faster. Cloud rendering is for peak queues and temporary capacity, not a replacement for daily editing.

On set

Upload 10 GB as a test and calculate the transfer time. Render 100 frames and compare local and cloud cost.

Do this

Cloud rendering starts with upload time and cost per frame; large media uploads slower than it renders.

  1. Upload 10 GB as a test and calculate upload time.
  2. Render 100 frames and compare local versus cloud cost.
  3. If upload exceeds render time, do not go cloud.

Avoid

Do not look at price per frame alone; uploading 10 GB can take longer than rendering.

3 checks

  • A 10 GB upload is timed.
  • Local and cloud costs are compared over 100 frames.
  • Upload time is compared with render time and a conclusion is stated.
