A news template runs scene, context, impact; every segment answers a viewer question.

A usable 60-second segment table

SegmentTimePictureSound / pass line
Scene0–10sWhat is happening now, or the latest scene footageOne “what happened” line; time and place clear
Verification10–25sWho confirmed, which document, which officeSource on screen; unknowns named as unknown
Context25–40sOnly one or two images needed to explain this eventNo history lecture; one cause line
Impact40–55sWho is affected and howSeparate what happened from what may happen
Next55–60sOne actionable step or a clear uncertaintyDo not conclude for the audience

Fill three lines before rolling

  • What happened: one fact without adjectives.
  • Who confirmed: name, document and date; write “unverified” if missing.
  • Next step: what can the viewer do, or what must they wait for?

On set

What happened, why, and what happens next. Each section answers one audience question.

Do this

  1. What happened on scene.
  2. Why it happened.
  3. What happens next and what viewers can do.

Avoid

Do not turn context into a history lecture; viewers need why this happened now.

Judge

This page passes on three observable checks, not slogans:

  • 1. The three parts answer what happened, why, and what next.
  • 2. Context keeps only what directly explains the current event.
  • 3. The ending gives a concrete next step or names the uncertainty.

Failures

Any one of these means stop and fix it before post:

  • 1. Check 1 is only staged (“The three parts answer what happened, why, and…”); another camera position or location exposes it.
  • 2. Check 2 is left to post or a verbal explanation (“Context keeps only what directly explains the…”); nothing on set can be rechecked.
  • 3. Do not turn context into a history lecture; viewers need why this happened now.

Extend

10-minute exercise: Break down one 30-second reference, mark the timecodes for “What happened on scene.” and “Why it happened.”, then answer: if one disappeared, would the film still hold?
