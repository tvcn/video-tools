Audio Starter Kit

Audio starter: shotgun, lav, recorder, headphones; dialogue first.

3 min read · Field noteTVCN EditorialEdited 2026-11

A shotgun carries the main dialogue, a lav is the backup, a recorder keeps the tracks separate, and headphones catch noise on the spot. Missing one of the four turns the shoot into guesswork.

One chain that can stand alone

  • Minimum: shotgun into camera, lav backed up on a phone or second body; only for a quiet interior and one speaker.
  • Working: shotgun into a 4-channel recorder, lav on its own track, headphones live; enough for two-person interviews and exteriors.
  • Insured: add a second lav, a backup recorder and timecode; one broken chain does not end the shoot.

Levels and redundancy

Keep dialogue peaks around -12 to -6 dBFS. Do not treat 32-bit float as insurance: it can rescue a clipped peak, not a missing lav, a stopped recorder, or wind inside the capsule. Every camera position needs at least one independent audio track.

Thirty-second acceptance

  1. Have one person speak normally while shotgun and lav record; each track must be intelligible alone.
  2. Record 30 seconds of a quiet room at the gain you will actually use; listen for an acceptable noise floor.
  3. Record 10 seconds outdoors in wind; confirm the blimp and shelter work and consonants survive.

Do not hide it in post

Denoise removes steady hiss, not wind, clothing rustle or room reflection. Change position, mic distance or wind protection on set; it is always cheaper than repair.

On set

Shotgun, lav, recorder, and headphones. Record thirty seconds of dialogue and check the noise floor.

Avoid

Do not bring only a shotgun; indoor dialogue without a lav backup dies in a draft.

Check

  • Shotgun and lav record simultaneously and each works alone.
  • A thirty-second dialogue test reveals the noise floor.
  • Someone wears headphones during recording, not just watching meters.
