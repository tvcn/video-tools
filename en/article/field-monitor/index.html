Field Monitor: Brightness, Scopes, LUT

A field monitor needs brightness, scopes, and LUT support; a pretty screen does not mean correct footage.

What to compare first

  • Brightness: ask whether it is readable outdoors before resolution; 500–1000 nits is the divide, and a hood is still needed.
  • LUT and scopes: can it load a monitoring LUT and show waveform, zebra or false color? Do not judge by “pretty color” alone.
  • Latency and power: check HDMI/SDI delay, battery type and hot-swap support; losing monitoring is more common than losing recording.

When not to use it

If the delivery only needs the camera screen, do not add a monitor for “professional look.” It adds weight, cables and another power failure point.

On-set monitor: brightness, waveform, LUT — three checks

A monitor is not “brighter is better.” Set it to standard brightness first, then judge waveform and LUT. Otherwise you are grading the monitor, not the image.

  1. Brightness: use a built-in grayscale or hood; set it so 5% and 10% black are distinct. “Looks comfortable” is not a setting.
  2. Waveform: use it for midtones. When monitor and waveform disagree, trust the waveform.
  3. LUT: use the on-set LUT for style, not exposure. Judge exposure on the log waveform.

Acceptance: play the same clip on the monitor and the computer; brightness and skin should not differ a stop. If they do, calibrate the monitor.

On set

Set the monitor to 800 nits outdoors and load the LUT. Shoot a grey card and compare the waveform and skin with the monitor.

Do this

A field monitor is judged by brightness, waveform, and LUT; a pretty screen does not mean correct footage.

  1. Set 800 nits outdoors and load the LUT.
  2. Shoot a gray card.
  3. Check waveform and skin against the monitor.

Avoid

Do not trust a pretty monitor image; without waveform, brightness and color can lie.

3 checks

  • Outdoor brightness reaches 800 nits and stays readable.
  • The LUT is loaded and the gray card has a waveform reading.
  • Skin judgment matches waveform, not eye alone.
