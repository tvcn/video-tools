Insert Shot: One Detail Beats One Line

An insert can replace a line: a shaking hand, a cup rim, an open door.

Inserts are the edit's breath, not random scenery

Prepare two or three inserts per scene: hand action, prop state, environment detail. They must cut anywhere into the master and help hide jumps, pace the scene or add information.

Shoot five seconds of head and tail around the action; one second at the center leaves no room.

Limits

Inserts cannot replace a missing master. If the space is unclear, ten close-ups will not explain it; protect the master first.

On set

List three shootable details per scene: fingers, a cup rim, a door gap. Cut to one for 1 to 2 seconds before the key line, with no explanation.

Do this

An insert can replace a line: a shaking hand, a cup rim, an unclosed door says more than explanation.

  1. List three filmable details per scene: fingers, cup rim, door gap.
  2. Cut one in one to two seconds before the key line.
  3. Do not add explanation; let the detail speak.

Avoid

Do not use an insert as decoration; if it carries no information, cut it.

3 checks

  • The insert carries a specific piece of information, not just beauty.
  • It runs one to two seconds and cuts before the key line.
  • Remove it and information goes missing, proving it replaced a line.
