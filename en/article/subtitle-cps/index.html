Subtitle CPS: Can They Read It?

Check characters per second; above reading speed, the viewer pauses and the film breaks.

Formula

CPS (characters per second) = characters ÷ display seconds. Chinese should stay under 9–12 CPS, English under 15–20; if over, split the line or hold it longer.

Example: 24 Chinese characters for 2 seconds is 12 CPS, already at the limit; the same line for 3 seconds is 8 CPS and readable.

How to lower CPS

  • Cut filler first, then split into two lines; do not shrink the type.
  • Give key lines 0.3–0.5 extra seconds and remove “um” and “like.”
  • Do not show two long bilingual lines at once; protect the primary language.

Limits

CPS is an average; numbers, names and fast speech need a separate check. Watch it at phone size, not only on the timeline.

On set

Keep Chinese under nine characters per second and English under fifteen. If over, split the line or hold longer.

Do this

A subtitle CPS calculator checks characters per second; beyond reading speed, viewers pause and the film breaks.

  1. Chinese under nine characters per second, English under fifteen.
  2. Split the sentence if over.
  3. Or extend the duration; do not shrink it into two tiny lines.

Avoid

Do not shrink the font to fit; viewers cannot finish and will pause or quit.

3 checks

  • Every caption stays under nine Chinese characters or fifteen English characters per second.
  • Over-limit lines are split or extended, not shrunk.
  • At playback speed, each caption reads in one pass without pausing.
