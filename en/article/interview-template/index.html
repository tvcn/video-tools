An interview template uses person, question, scene; shoot where they work before sitting down.

A usable 60-second segment table

SegmentTimePictureSound / pass line
Scene action0–10sThe person in the place where they actually work, hands busyAmbience; no résumé question first
Work10–25sOne complete work action shows difficultySync sound; interview does not cover the action
Question 125–40sSit-down interview, work traces still in the backgroundQuestion about the action just seen
Question 240–50sReaction shot and hand detailAsk about cost, not birthplace
Close50–60sThe person returns to work or looks at the spaceOne concrete line; no life summary

Fill three lines before rolling

  • Place: where do they really work? Why not a meeting room?
  • Action: what will you shoot first? It must be complete.
  • Questions: all three circle that action; delete résumé questions.

On set

Person, question, place. Shoot where they can work before sitting them down.

Do this

  1. Choose a place where they can do their work.
  2. Shoot the work first, then sit for the interview.
  3. Base questions on one concrete action, not a résumé.

Avoid

Do not let the subject read a résumé; a résumé has no images.

Judge

This page passes on three observable checks, not slogans:

  • 1. The scene is where they actually work, not an empty meeting room.
  • 2. One action is captured before the sit-down interview.
  • 3. Questions center on the action, not birthplace or résumé.

Failures

Any one of these means stop and fix it before post:

  • 1. Check 1 is only staged (“The scene is where they actually work, not an…”); another camera position or location exposes it.
  • 2. Check 2 is left to post or a verbal explanation (“One action is captured before the sit-down int…”); nothing on set can be rechecked.
  • 3. Do not let the subject read a résumé; a résumé has no images.

Extend

10-minute exercise: Break down one 30-second reference, mark the timecodes for “Choose a place where they can do their” and “Shoot the work first, then sit for the”, then answer: if one disappeared, would the film still hold?

Check

  • The scene is where they actually work, not an empty meeting room.
  • One action is captured before the sit-down interview.
  • Questions center on the action, not birthplace or résumé.
