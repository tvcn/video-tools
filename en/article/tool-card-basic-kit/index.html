Minimum Kit

Minimum kit: a camera, one standard zoom, a tripod, and a mic that works.

3 min read · Field noteTVCN EditorialEdited 2026-11

Build backwards from the job

  • Minimum: one camera with manual exposure, one 24–70mm-equivalent lens, one lav or on-camera mic, one dimmable light, two cards, one portable drive. Finish a 30-second dialogue scene and ten shots first.
  • Working: add a prime, a shotgun mic and recorder, ND filters, and a monitor. Independent sound and exposure are the entry ticket to style.
  • Backup: a second body, a second audio chain, spare batteries, and a separate backup drive. A paid shoot should survive one failed link.

Where the first money goes

Sound first, light second, lenses last. Viewers forgive a flat image; they do not forgive dialogue they cannot hear. The body sets the ceiling; sound and lighting set the floor.

Three tests before you shoot

  1. Record 30 seconds of dialogue, listen on headphones for noise and sibilance, then play it back without monitoring.
  2. Shoot a frame with a bright window: check that highlights hold and shadows still carry detail.
  3. Copy the test clip to the drive, eject the card, and play it back from the drive only.

When not to upgrade

If the bottleneck is script, blocking or editing, a fourth lens will not help. Finish three complete short films with the current kit, then buy against a recorded failure.

On set

A body, standard zoom, tripod, and lav. Keep 20 percent of the budget for storage and batteries.

Avoid

Do not pick a camera body first; write the four jobs it must do.

3 checks

  • Each of four items maps to one named capability.
  • Storage and batteries take about 20 percent of budget.
  • Every missing item has a workable substitute.
