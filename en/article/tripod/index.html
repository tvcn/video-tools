Tripod: Load and Head

Check payload and head before max height; camera, lens, and accessories weigh more than the spec.

What to compare first

  • Payload: calculate body, lens, battery, monitor and head, with at least 30% headroom.
  • Height and stability: do not buy by maximum height; raising it amplifies shake, test at your working height.
  • Legs and head: test leg locks, leveling and head return; a sandbag hook is more useful than a spec.

When not to use it

For fast movement, tight spaces or low angles, a monopod, tabletop rig or gimbal beats a tripod. Do not trade “can shoot it” for “steady.”

On set

Mount the body, lens, monitor, and battery and lock the head. Push it three times and check the legs and head for spring.

Do this

A tripod is judged by payload and head, not maximum height; body, lens, and accessories usually weigh more than the rating.

  1. Mount body, lens, monitor, and batteries.
  2. Lock the head and push three times.
  3. Check whether legs and head rebound.

Avoid

Do not size the tripod to a bare body; with lens and batteries the payload may already be over.

3 checks

  • Full rig weight is measured against the tripod rating.
  • Three pushes with the head locked show no leg slip or rebound.
  • Maximum height is not the main reason to buy; stability comes first.
