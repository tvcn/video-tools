SDH: More Than Dialogue

SDH names speakers, ambience, and important effects; it does not just transcribe dialogue.

SDH writes what is heard

SDH is not only dialogue; it names sound events: door, footsteps, phone, music mood. Mark speaker changes by name or position; overlap gets “together.”

Describe music in brackets by style and feeling, not just “music.” Key plot sounds include direction: approaching from back left.

Limits

SDH is not a full sound list. Caption only what drives understanding or emotion; labeling every sound buries the image.

On set

Mark the speaker: male, female, phone. Write [door] or [rain] for effects and [music] for score; do not just transcribe dialogue.

Do this

SDH captions name the speaker, ambience, and key effects, not just dialogue.

  1. Label speakers: male, female, phone.
  2. Write ambience as [door], [rain], and music as [music].
  3. Check placement and reading speed; captions never cover mouths or hands.

Avoid

Do not place captions over mouths or hands; the audience needs the performance.

Image note. Covers, diagrams and step images on this page are editorial illustrations or structure diagrams, not photographs of a test performed here; they are not evidence of measured gear performance. Where an image is AI-assisted, the caption says so. Read the test conditions in the text before treating an image as proof.

3 checks

  • Each caption identifies the speaker and distinguishes phone or off-screen voice.
  • Door, rain, music, and other key sounds appear in the captions.
  • Captions stay off mouths and hands, within two lines, and read at pace.
