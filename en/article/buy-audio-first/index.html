Buy Audio First: Mic, Noise Floor, and Gain

Audiences forgive a plain image; they do not forgive unclear sound. On a limited budget, put money into a microphone and recorder before a second lens.

First decision: which link is missing

Decide whether you lack clear speech or clean ambience. For interior interviews, a dynamic/shotgun mic plus recorder is steadier than a lav; for moving exteriors, a lav with wind protection is more useful. Do not buy an expensive mic and feed it through a camera’s auto gain.

Fits:

  • Interview/voiceover, clear speech
  • Controlled interior, close to talent
  • Limited budget, intelligibility first

Does not fit:

  • Distant multi-person events
  • Windy exterior without protection
  • Multi-channel sync recording

Test protocol: reproducible, no invented scores

Record 30 seconds of room tone in a quiet room, then normal speech and a close shout. Listen for noise, clipping, sibilance, and plosives—on headphones and once on a speaker.

Buying steps: bring footage, not spec sheets

Run these in order before paying. If one fails, do not tell yourself post will fix it.

  1. Record room tone
  2. Test normal/shout clipping
  3. Test 30° off-axis
  4. Handheld walk for cable noise
  5. Confirm power and runtime

Check

  • Quiet-room noise is acceptable.
  • Normal speech does not clip.
  • 30° off-axis stays intelligible.
  • Handling has no rub/line noise.
  • Headphone monitoring and manual gain exist.
  • Runtime covers the shoot day.

Decision tree: say what you are recording

Interior interview, moving exterior and multi-person scene are three different answers. Interior interview favors a dynamic/shotgun mic plus recorder; moving exterior favors lavs with wind protection; multi-person favors a multichannel recorder and timecode. Do not force one kit to cover all three.

  1. Write the distance: fixed under 1m, moving under 3m, multi-person under 10m.
  2. Interior: directional mic; exterior: lav/wireless; multi-person: multichannel.
  3. Confirm manual gain, headphone monitoring and 48V/plug-in power.
  4. Total the batteries, cards, wind protection and backup recording.

Test scope: room tone and dialogue separately

Record 30 seconds of room tone, 30 seconds of normal speech and one close shout. Listen on headphones for noise, clipping, sibilance and plosives, then play it once at low speaker volume. Not measured: frequency response, THD, lab isolation. Those numbers are not printed here.

Buying order: rent before buying

Rent a lav/shotgun and recorder, record one real interview, then decide what to buy.

  1. Rent the target body and a common lens once; shoot a real project.
  2. Confirm mount, adapter, autofocus and power.
  3. Total the cards, batteries, monitor, storage and repair.
  4. Keep 10% for surprises and consumables.
  5. Buy the second lens after the first delivery.
