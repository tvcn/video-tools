Sponsorship Disclosure: Make the Ad Visible

Disclose sponsorship before the recommendation, not in the last line of the description.

3 min read · Field noteTVCN Research DeskEdited 2026-11

  1. The audience must know who paid
  2. Limits
  3. On set
  4. Do this
  5. Avoid
  6. 3 checks

The audience must know who paid

Disclose a commercial relationship before the viewer forms a purchase judgment, not in small print at the end. Say at the top “this film is supported by X,” or keep an “ad” label on screen so they can keep watching with that knowledge.

Be specific: who paid, what they paid, and whether they have final approval. “Partnership” is not disclosure.

Limits

Disclosure does not automatically destroy trust; hiding the relationship and being caught does. Do not stage an ad as an independent review; that is the fastest way to lose the audience.

On set

Put the ad label in the first three seconds or the title, not the last line of the description.

Do this

  1. Say “this video is sponsored by X” in the first three seconds or the first line of voice-over.
  2. Add an on-screen label and repeat it in the first line of the description.
  3. State whether the sponsor reviewed or changed the content; do not hide the interest.

Avoid

Do not use “thanks for the support” instead of “sponsored”; the audience needs to know who paid.

3 checks

  • The first three seconds or first voice-over line discloses the sponsor.
  • An on-screen label and the first description line repeat it.
  • It states whether the sponsor reviewed or changed the content.
