Mirrorless for Video: Heat, Rolling Shutter, Crop

Check three things: overheating, rolling shutter, and whether video crops the frame.

3 min read · Field noteTVCN Tools DeskEdited 2026-11

What to compare first

  • Heat and record limits: confirm real continuous runtime at room temperature, not the claim; summer and 4K high frame rates stop first.
  • Rolling shutter and readout: check whether fast pans and lateral motion skew; large sensors especially need readout speed.
  • Crop and ports: whether 4K crops, HDMI is clean, and external power works; these decide whether it fits a workflow.

When not to use it

For long interviews, extreme low light or professional audio, a mirrorless body is a compromise; use a actively cooled camera or camcorder. Do not hide overheating behind “cinematic.”

On set

Record thirty minutes of 4K 25p and watch for a heat warning. Pan across a railing for rolling shutter, then switch to 4K 60p and check the crop.

Do this

A mirrorless video camera must answer three things: overheating, rolling shutter, and crop in video mode.

  1. Record thirty minutes of 4K 25p and watch for a heat warning.
  2. Pan across a railing to see rolling shutter.
  3. Switch to 4K 60p and check whether the angle narrows.

Avoid

Do not test for three minutes; overheating often appears around minute twenty.

3 checks

  • A thirty-minute 4K 25p run logs temperature or warnings.
  • Rolling shutter on the railing is visible and described.
  • The 4K 60p crop has a before-and-after angle comparison.
