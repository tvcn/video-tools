Risk Assessment: Fill One Page Before You Roll

One page before rolling: who can get hurt, what can fall, who is in charge, and where the nearest hospital is.

A risk sheet is not every possible bad thing; it is the three to five that would make today unshootable: weather, key person absent, gear failure, location refused, transport cut. For each, write trigger, backup and decision time.

Name the item that would stop the shoot

Put the latest decision moment on the call sheet: if it is not solved by then, switch to Plan B without an on-set debate.

Limits

A risk sheet is not insurance. It cannot remove risk, only decide earlier; real safety still needs an on-set owner with stop-work authority.

On set

Fill one page with four boxes: who can get hurt, what can fall, who is responsible, and the nearest hospital. Read it to the crew before rolling.

Do this

A risk assessment is one page: who can get hurt, what can fall, who is responsible, and where the nearest hospital is. Read it to the crew.

  1. Fill four boxes: who can be hurt, what can fall, who owns the risk, and the nearest hospital.
  2. Read it aloud before rolling and assign an owner to each risk.
  3. Update it when the set changes and confirm at wrap that nothing was missed.

Avoid

Do not fill the form and lock it in a drawer; if nobody heard it, it was not done.

Judge

Three signals are observable on set:

  • highlights are not clipped and shadows keep recoverable detail
  • skin stays consistent across camera angles
  • sound has an independent track, not just the on-camera mic

Failures

These three moves erase the detail you just built:

  • exposure is right but the light has no direction
  • sound relies on the camera mic and dies in a noisy room
  • crossing the line leaves the audience without a map

Extend

10-minute exercise: Shoot three 10-second takes by the same window, changing only “Fill four boxes: who can be hurt, what”. Compare highlights, skin and background; keep the one that is editable.

3 checks

  • All four boxes contain specifics, not “be careful.”
  • Every risk has a named owner and crew can state their own item.
  • The sheet was updated after set changes and checked again at wrap.
