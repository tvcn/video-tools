Log Footage: Shoot for the Grade

Log is not "flat looks pro"; it preserves highlights and shadows for post, if exposure and color are handled.

Log is not “brighter is better.” Expose with one to one-and-a-half stops of highlight headroom, midtones in the middle of the waveform; shadows may underexpose but must not crush. Use false color or zebra, not the monitor LUT.

Log protects highlights, then midtones

Apply the conversion LUT first, then primary and look. Do not use one LUT across different cameras and ISOs.

Limits

Log is not free latitude. Noise still exists at high ISO; light the scene rather than lifting in post.

On set

Expose the face slightly above middle grey and use the waveform to keep highlights off the ceiling. Shoot a color chart before the take so post has a reference.

Do this

Log is not a “flat look”; it reserves highlight and shadow range for post, provided exposure and color management are right. Shoot for the grade.

  1. Expose the face slightly above middle gray and confirm on the waveform.
  2. Keep highlights below the ceiling; do not blow the sky for a brighter face.
  3. Shoot a color chart before rolling to give post a transform reference.

Avoid

Do not treat a flat image as a style; without correct exposure and color management, Log is harder to rescue.

Judge

Three signals are observable on set:

  • highlights are not clipped and shadows keep recoverable detail
  • skin stays consistent across camera angles
  • sound has an independent track, not just the on-camera mic

Failures

These three moves erase the detail you just built:

  • exposure is right but the light has no direction
  • sound relies on the camera mic and dies in a noisy room
  • crossing the line leaves the audience without a map

Extend

10-minute exercise: Shoot three 10-second takes by the same window, changing only “Expose the face slightly above middle gray and”. Compare highlights, skin and background; keep the one that is editable.

3 checks

  • Skin sits slightly above middle gray, shadows are not crushed, and highlights are unclipped.
  • A color chart was shot for every setup and post can apply one transform.
  • You can state the exposure intent of the Log take instead of “we'll fix it later.”
