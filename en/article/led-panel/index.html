LED Panel: CRI and Brightness

Check CRI and real output, not wattage on the box; green skin means the color is wrong.

3 min read · Field noteTVCN Tools DeskEdited 2026-11

What to compare first

  • Color rendering: shoot skin, red cloth and green plants; check real performance beyond CRI/TLCI claims. Cheap panels often push green dirty.
  • Dimming: sweep from high to low and look for flicker, cast and steps; it shows most at low output.
  • Diffusion and control: is the built-in diffusion even, and can it take a grid? Edge spill shows when the panel is close to a face.

When not to use it

For strong direction, long throws or large areas, a panel loses to a COB with a softbox. It is best for close, low-power and fast setups.

On set

Set the panel to 5600K at full power and shoot white paper and a hand. Check for green skin and measure the output at one meter.

Do this

An LED panel is judged by color rendering and actual output, not box wattage; green skin means bad color.

  1. Set the light to 5600K at 100 percent and shoot white paper and a hand.
  2. Check whether the hand turns green.
  3. Measure illuminance at one meter to see whether it can key.

Avoid

Do not buy by wattage; bad color rendering makes a bright light usable only as fill.

3 checks

  • White paper and hand are sampled at 5600K full power.
  • Green or magenta skin cast is named.
  • One-meter illuminance is measured and judged against key-light needs.
