AI Audio Tool: Rights and Quality

Check rights and audio quality before features; a cloned voice's license matters more than timbre.

2 min read · Field noteTVCN Tools DeskEdited 2026-11
On this page
  1. What to compare first
  2. When not to use it
  3. On set
  4. Do this
  5. Avoid
  6. 3 checks

What to compare first

  • Rights: commercial license, voice-clone scope and output ownership; do not stop at “commercial use,” read the full terms.
  • Quality: test Chinese, English, numbers, names and emotional lines; a demo is not enough, use your own script.
  • Control: can it import your recording and control pauses and stress? A voice you cannot control does not enter dialogue editing.

When not to use it

News, documentary, testimony and real-person endorsement must not replace the original voice with a clone. Unlicensed voice does not enter delivery.

On set

Have the tool read thirty seconds of dialogue and listen for sibilance, phrasing, and endings. Then check commercial rights and clone scope.

Do this

An AI audio tool is judged by license and quality before features; clone scope matters more than timbre.

  1. Have it read a thirty-second line.
  2. Listen for sibilance, pauses, and tails.
  3. Then check commercial license and clone scope.

Avoid

Do not judge timbre alone; a personal-use license still blocks commercial work.

3 checks

  • Sibilance, pauses, and tails are assessed on a thirty-second line.
  • Commercial license and clone scope have written terms.
  • Output clears the current project without extra licensing.
