Parallel Editing: When Do Two Lines Meet?

Parallel editing needs a shared problem, not constant switching

Two lines intercut because they share a question: the same time, the same money, the same person. Every return must tighten the question, not just change location.

Give the lines different rhythms—one slow, one fast—and connect on action, sound or graphic match so the viewer does not get lost.

Limits

More intercutting is not more tension. Without a meeting point, fast switching is two films taking turns.

On set

Give each line a countdown: A has ten minutes left, B has three. Cross them when the audience starts doing the math.

Do this

Parallel editing is not constant cutting; it makes the audience ask when the two lines will collide.

  1. Give each line a countdown: A has ten minutes, B has three.
  2. Cross when the audience starts calculating, not every ten seconds.
  3. After the cross, let the lines share an object, sound, or action.

Avoid

Do not cut back and forth for technique; without a countdown, the cross has no pressure.

3 checks

  • Each line has a readable countdown so the audience can calculate who arrives first.
  • The cross happens at rising pressure, not at even intervals.
  • After the cross, the lines share an element instead of running separately.
