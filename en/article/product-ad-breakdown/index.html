Product Ad Breakdown: Problem Becomes Proof

A nine-beat breakdown of a sixty-second product ad: problem, failed fix, product, demo, proof, objection, second proof, result, action.

Fix Three Variables First

Fix three variables first: sixty seconds, feed/commerce platform, and one goal (finish, remember, or buy). Without them, every timecode looks right and none works.

Sample note: the timecodes below are a teaching sample for this format's usual length. They do not correspond to one specific film and are not frame-accurate analysis. Use the same positions on your own film; do not treat these beats as the only answer.

Timecode Breakdown: 9 Beats

Watch in order; do not read commentary first. For each beat record four things: picture, sound, job, and steal or do-not-copy.

  1. 0:00–0:03 | Picture: the problem happens, fumbling or one failed action. Sound: sync, room before music. Job: let viewers recognize their own problem. Steal: open on action, not product. Do not copy: turn the problem into a spectacle.
  2. 0:03–0:08 | Picture: the old fix fails, reaction; product still absent. Sound: a sigh or object drop. Job: make the problem cost something. Steal: make failure visible. Do not copy: narrate “it's hard.”
  3. 0:08–0:12 | Picture: product enters, one object only. Sound: product sound, light music. Job: answer the problem, no brand history. Steal: the product solves that one failure. Do not copy: open on a turntable.
  4. 0:12–0:20 | Picture: one function demo that can be repeated. Sound: sync, key action emphasized. Job: prove it actually works. Steal: one continuous demo. Do not copy: five close-ups pretending to be a demo.
  5. 0:20–0:32 | Picture: before/after on the same setup and light. Sound: two seconds of sync each. Job: give verifiable proof. Steal: same-setup comparison. Do not copy: change camera or light to flatter the after.
  6. 0:32–0:42 | Picture: price, spec, who it is for and not for. Sound: voice only reads key numbers. Job: handle objection without a manual. Steal: say who it is not for. Do not copy: read the whole spec sheet.
  7. 0:42–0:52 | Picture: second proof: real user, second setting, or stress test. Sound: real reaction, no added effects. Job: more than one proof. Steal: one real reaction. Do not copy: cast an actor to look surprised.
  8. 0:52–0:58 | Picture: result returns to the person, not a product close-up. Sound: room returns. Job: show life after use. Steal: result is an action. Do not copy: result is a pile of logos.
  9. 0:58–1:00 | Picture: one action: tap, claim, try; one button only. Sound: one line. Job: close. Steal: one action. Do not copy: ask for three things at once.

Read the Structure

The spine is problem, cost, answer, proof, objection, second proof, result, action. Every ten seconds needs new information; before the product appears, viewers need a reason to keep watching.

Ask only three questions: what proof arrives in the first ten seconds; whether every ten to fifteen seconds adds information; whether the end lands one action, one change, or one question. If any is missing, a 60 sec film falls apart.

Budget Order of Magnitude

Budget is a rough mainland-China production range, excluding media spend; it is an order of magnitude, not a quote. At the same length, a stage, cast, effects, or music rights can each double the cost.

  • Low: RMB 3,000–8,000, one person, one camera, natural light, one day.
  • Mid: RMB 8,000–30,000, three to five people, product table and two lights, one to two days.
  • High: RMB 30,000–100,000+, director, camera, art, post, set or stage, two to four days.

Three Structures You Can Steal

Steal structure, not images. These three still work when you change the subject.

  1. Open on a failed action, not the product.
  2. Same-setup before/after so proof is visible.
  3. Say who it is not for; handle objection early.

Three Reasons Not to Copy

Not “bad,” but built on different conditions; copying exposes your budget, platform, and audience.

  1. Do not copy “must explode in three seconds”: without media spend, speed does not buy attention.
  2. Do not copy premium “no captions, image only”: feeds are silent by default.
  3. Do not copy “product as hero”: without brand recognition, viewers recognize the problem first.

Breakdown Exercises

Do them before shooting; do not just read. Do the three in order, and the third must become a fifteen-second file.

  1. Watch muted; write the problem you see in the first three seconds.
  2. Count when the product first appears; if before five seconds, recut.
  3. Compress to fifteen seconds: problem, one proof, one action.

Check

  • The first three seconds show a concrete problem, not a turntable.
  • Product enters no earlier than five seconds.
  • One same-setup, verifiable proof exists mid-section.
  • Price/spec reads only key numbers, not a manual.
  • Second proof is not an acted surprise.
  • One action at the end.
  • No narration concludes for the viewer.

Regional note for US / UK / EU readers

This English page keeps mainland-China examples where they are part of the method. Read platform names as examples, not as the only market: Douyin ↔ TikTok/Reels, Bilibili ↔ YouTube, Xiaohongshu ↔ Instagram/Pinterest; RMB figures are not converted here. Platform names and monetisation rules differ by market; treat Douyin, Bilibili and Xiaohongshu as examples, then re-check TikTok, YouTube, Instagram or the local platform.
