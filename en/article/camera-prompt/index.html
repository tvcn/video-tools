Camera Prompt: Framing, Angle, Lens

Camera prompts need framing, angle, and lens. Without them, the model guesses.

Camera prompts name position before motion

“Cinematic camera move” carries no information. Write: camera 1.5 m left-front of the subject, waist height, 50mm, slow 30 cm rightward pan. Position and motion are visible, so the model knows how to move.

One prompt, one move: push, pull, pan, track, follow. Do not combine orbit and push-in.

Limits

Camera motion cannot replace staging. If the subject does not act and the space is unclear, an orbiting camera is only scenery.

A camera prompt has four fields

“Cinematic” and “premium” cannot be executed. Write shot size, angle, lens and motion. With all four, a shoot or generation has a repeatable starting point.

  1. Size: close-up, medium, wide. Avoid “medium-close.”
  2. Angle: level, high, low; state camera height.
  3. Lens: 24/35/50/85mm. A lens is more useful than “blur.”
  4. Motion: static, push, pull, track, follow; state speed and start/end.

Acceptance: give the prompt to another shooter; if they cannot reproduce the frame, one field is missing.

On set

Write medium shot, eye level, 35mm, subject on the right third, then add motion. Keep shot size and focal length separate.

Do this

A camera prompt states size, angle, and lens; without them the model guesses.

  1. Write medium shot, eye level, 35mm.
  2. Place the subject in the right third and add one movement.
  3. Keep size and lens separate; do not merge them into an adjective.

Avoid

Do not write only “cinematic camera”; missing size, angle, or lens makes the model guess.

3 checks

  • The prompt separately names size, angle, and lens.
  • Subject placement is a specific third, not “nice composition.”
  • The output matches medium shot, eye level, and a 35mm view.
