Scene Transitions: Carry an Action Across the Cut

Carry an action, a sound, or an object across the cut instead of using a title card to explain time.

A scene change is not “then he went to the office.” Write what the last action left behind: he slams the door and it is still ringing; she sets down a cup and someone else holds it. Consequence connects the spaces.

Write the consequence of an action, not “then”

End each scene on an unfinished action and catch it at the next opening; the audience fills in time.

Limits

A transition is not a title card. “Three days later” is weaker than stubble, light or wardrobe; titles are the last resort.

On set

Find a shared action between scenes: close a door, then open one; pick up a cup, then put it down. Let action carry the time jump.

Do this

Connect scenes with action, sound, or an object, not a title card saying “three days later.”

  1. Find a shared action: door closes in one scene, opens in the next.
  2. Or a shared object: cup picked up, then set down.
  3. Use sound arriving early or late to bridge the spaces without a card.

Avoid

Do not use a title card for time; if an action can bridge it, do not write it.

Judge

Three signals are observable on set:

  • the want is visible in one action
  • the obstacle escalates inside the same scene
  • delete one expository line and the conflict still holds

Failures

These three moves erase the detail you just built:

  • the want is unclear and the scene is only mood
  • the obstacle is solved by coincidence, not a decision
  • dialogue repeats what the image already says

Extend

10-minute exercise: Write one 30-second scene with only “Find a shared action: door closes in one” and “Or a shared object: cup picked up, then”. Cut every explanation and ask one person to retell what the character wants.

3 checks

  • Adjacent scenes share at least one action, object, or sound.
  • The time jump reads without a title card.
  • Remove the card and the audience can still say how much time passed or where they are.
