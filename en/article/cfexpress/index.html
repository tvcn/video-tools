CFexpress: Speed and Heat

Check sustained write and heat, not peak speed; on long takes, thermal throttling hurts before capacity.

For low-bitrate stills or short clips, do not pay for the fastest spec. Once the card exceeds the camera's write ceiling, extra speed does not become image quality.

What to compare first

  • Sustained write: look at sustained, not peak; a card that throttles after a few minutes will stop a long take.
  • Heat: cards and readers get hot; a reader that slows or disconnects during a long copy lacks cooling.
  • Capacity and backup: calculate from one day's footage and dual-card backup, not the advertised “record time.”

When not to use it

For low-bitrate stills or short clips, do not pay for the fastest spec. Once the card exceeds the camera's write ceiling, extra speed does not become image quality.

On set

Record ten minutes at the highest bitrate and feel the card temperature. Copy to an SSD and measure sustained write speed.

Do this

CFexpress cards are judged by sustained write and heat, not peak speed; on long takes, thermal throttling hurts more than capacity.

  1. Record ten minutes at maximum bitrate and feel the card temperature.
  2. Copy to SSD and measure sustained write for a drop.
  3. If it drops, change card or lower bitrate.

Avoid

Do not trust peak write; thermal throttling after ten minutes breaks long takes.

Check

  • A ten-minute maximum-bitrate take completes and card temperature is logged.
  • Sustained copy to SSD shows no sharp drop.
  • A card change or bitrate reduction is prepared if it drops.
