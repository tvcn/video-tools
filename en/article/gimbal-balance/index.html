Gimbal Balance: Five Minutes Before Power

Balance each axis with the others locked; five minutes before power saves half an hour on set.

Power off, balance, then mount the lens

Balance order: lock axes, mount camera, adjust fore-aft and left-right, check that all three axes rest at any angle. Rebalance after every lens or filter change; an unbalanced motor drains power, heats and jitters.

Walk a slow circle and listen for motor noise before fast follow.

Limits

Balance is not a substitute for walking. The gimbal saves only part of vertical bounce; practice heel-first, soft-knee steps.

Balance order: lock axes, then power on

Gimbal balance is not “turn it on and auto-calibrate.” Balance mechanically first, then power. Wrong order makes the motors fight and the image shake.

  1. Lock tilt, roll and pan. Adjust tilt front/back first, then roll left/right, then pan.
  2. Tilt each axis 45° by hand; the camera should stay. If it falls, move the center of gravity.
  3. Power on and auto-calibrate. Rebalance after a lens or battery change.

Acceptance: powered off, the camera should hold at any angle you release it. If it falls one way, do not let the motors hold it.

On set

Lock tilt to set roll, lock roll to set pan, and set pan last. Push the gimbal after each adjustment to check for centering.

Do this

Gimbal balance goes axis by axis, locking one before adjusting the next; five minutes before power-on saves half an hour on set.

  1. Lock pan and adjust tilt.
  2. Lock tilt and adjust roll.
  3. Finally adjust pan, nudging after each to check centering.

Avoid

Do not loosen all three axes at once; everything moves and never balances.

3 checks

  • Axes are locked and adjusted in pan, tilt, roll order.
  • Each axis returns to center after a nudge without drifting.
  • Balance completes before power-on and motors do not constantly correct.
