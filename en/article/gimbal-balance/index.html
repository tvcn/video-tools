Gimbal Balance: Five Minutes Before Power

Balance each axis with the others locked; five minutes before power saves half an hour on set.

Power off, balance, then mount the lens

Walk a slow circle and listen for motor noise before fast follow.

Limits

Balance is not a substitute for walking. The gimbal saves only part of vertical bounce; practice heel-first, soft-knee steps.

Balance order: lock axes, then power on

Gimbal balance is not “turn it on and auto-calibrate.” Balance mechanically first, then power. Wrong order makes the motors fight and the image shake.

  1. Lock tilt, roll and pan. Adjust tilt front/back first, then roll left/right, then pan.
  2. Tilt each axis 45° by hand; the camera should stay. If it falls, move the center of gravity.
  3. Power on and auto-calibrate. Rebalance after a lens or battery change.

Acceptance: powered off, the camera should hold at any angle you release it. If it falls one way, do not let the motors hold it.

On set

Lock tilt to set roll, lock roll to set pan, and set pan last. Push the gimbal after each adjustment to check for centering.

Do this

Gimbal balance goes axis by axis, locking one before adjusting the next; five minutes before power-on saves half an hour on set.

  1. Lock pan and adjust tilt.
  2. Lock tilt and adjust roll.
  3. Finally adjust pan, nudging after each to check centering.

Avoid

Do not loosen all three axes at once; everything moves and never balances.

Judge

This page passes on three observable checks, not slogans:

  • 1. Axes are locked and adjusted in pan, tilt, roll order.
  • 2. Each axis returns to center after a nudge without drifting.
  • 3. Balance completes before power-on and motors do not constantly correct.

Failures

Any one of these means stop and fix it before post:

  • 1. Check 1 is only staged (“Axes are locked and adjusted in pan, tilt, rol…”); another camera position or location exposes it.
  • 2. Check 2 is left to post or a verbal explanation (“Each axis returns to center after a nudge with…”); nothing on set can be rechecked.
  • 3. Do not loosen all three axes at once; everything moves and never balances.

Extend

10-minute exercise: Run a 60-second test with the gear you already own, measuring only “Lock pan and adjust tilt.”. Record two numbers: what you need and what the gear actually gives.

Check

  • Axes are locked and adjusted in pan, tilt, roll order.
  • Each axis returns to center after a nudge without drifting.
  • Balance completes before power-on and motors do not constantly correct.

Deep dive: balancing is about centre of gravity, not “level”

About 5 minutes. The three motors cancel shake; they do not hold the camera up. The goal of balancing is that the camera does not fall in any direction when the power is off and your hand is away. If the centre is off, the motors fight gravity, the image shakes, the head heats and the battery drains.

Three axes, three centres

Pitch: front-to-back. A heavier or further-forward lens makes the camera nod; move the plate until it stays put when you let go.

Roll: left-to-right. Battery, monitor and follow-focus all shift the side-to-side centre; check both sides, not one.

Yaw: up-down. The arm height sets the inverted-pendulum balance; too low is top-heavy, too high is bottom-heavy.

Two conditions, two methods

Light body + small lens (<1.5 kg): pitch first, roll second, yaw last; raise motor strength gradually, never start at maximum.

Heavy body + long lens (>2.5 kg): confirm the payload limit, support the lens, then run a 30-second power-off test to see whether it slowly tips.

Ten checks

  1. With power off, the camera does not fall in pitch, roll or yaw.
  2. Let go for three seconds; the frame does not drift to either side.
  3. The plate is locked and the lens is supported.
  4. Battery, monitor and cables are mounted before balancing.
  5. Motor strength starts low; it does not lock at power-on.
  6. A 360° yaw turn does not pull or snag a cable.
  7. Thirty seconds of shooting needs no hand support and shows no low-frequency swing.
  8. A lens change triggers a fresh balance, not the old settings.
  9. You know the payload limit and do not exceed it.
  10. Motor strength is zeroed before packing, so it does not heat in transit.

Alternative

If it still shakes after balancing, do not keep raising motor strength. Use a lighter lens, add counterweight or switch to a shoulder rig. If the only option is handheld, turning the stabiliser off can be more reliable than half-stabilised footage. A gimbal is not a permit; it solves one kind of shake.
