Loop Cycle: Match the First and Last Frame

The first and last frames must match, or the loop twitches every cycle.

Walk, run and breathing cycles must connect first and last frame invisibly. Build key poses, then in-betweens; overlap one frame at the seam so the loop does not pop.

Loops live or die at the seam

A loop is not identical repetition. Add one small variation per cycle—a hand higher, a step slower. Perfect sameness bores in three seconds.

Limits

Loop length comes from the action, not the music. Cutting before a step completes makes the foot slide; finish the action, then match the beat.

On set

Draw the same frame at both ends with eight to twelve frames between. Loop it and check for a jump from last to first.

Do this

A loop needs matching first and last frames, or it hiccups every cycle.

  1. Draw the same pose at the head and tail.
  2. Fill eight to twelve frames between them.
  3. Play on loop and check the last-to-first transition for a jump.

Avoid

Do not leave a one-frame mismatch at the seam; repetition makes the hitch obvious.

Judge

This page passes on three observable checks, not slogans:

  • 1. First and last frames are the same pose in position and shape.
  • 2. Eight to twelve frames fill the middle at an even rhythm.
  • 3. Ten loops play with no hitch or stick at the seam.

Failures

Any one of these means stop and fix it before post:

  • 1. Check 1 is only staged (“First and last frames are the same pose in pos…”); another camera position or location exposes it.
  • 2. Check 2 is left to post or a verbal explanation (“Eight to twelve frames fill the middle at an e…”); nothing on set can be rechecked.
  • 3. Do not leave a one-frame mismatch at the seam; repetition makes the hitch obvious.

Extend

10-minute exercise: Draw three keys at 24 fps, change only “Fill eight to twelve frames between them.”, and export two seconds. If the weight still does not read, add anticipation before adding blur.

Check

  • First and last frames are the same pose in position and shape.
  • Eight to twelve frames fill the middle at an even rhythm.
  • Ten loops play with no hitch or stick at the seam.
