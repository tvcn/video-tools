Live Encoding: Bitrate, Uplink, Backup

Calculate uplink before bitrate, and always keep a local recording as backup.

Set bitrate at 70% of upload, keyframe every two seconds, audio 128–192 kbps. High resolution without bitrate turns motion into blocks; stable 720p beats stuttering 4K.

Live starts with staying up, then looking good

Test ten minutes before: dropped frames, latency, audio sync, backup network. Do not discover upload limits after going live.

Limits

Live is not recorded. Post cannot fix it; state information once, prepare captions and graphics in advance.

On set

Measure upload speed first: you need 1.5 times the target bitrate for headroom. Set a primary stream and a hotspot backup, and record a local high-bitrate copy.

Do this

Live encoding starts with uplink bandwidth, then bitrate. Always keep a local recording as backup.

  1. Measure upload speed; 1.5 times the target bitrate is the safe margin.
  2. Set a primary stream, a hotspot backup, and a high-bitrate local recording.
  3. Rehearse a disconnection and record the dropout time and recovery steps.

Avoid

Do not run one path or treat the stream as one-shot; a dropout kills more than soft image quality.

Judge

Three signals are observable on set:

  • highlights are not clipped and shadows keep recoverable detail
  • skin stays consistent across camera angles
  • sound has an independent track, not just the on-camera mic

Failures

These three moves erase the detail you just built:

  • exposure is right but the light has no direction
  • sound relies on the camera mic and dies in a noisy room
  • crossing the line leaves the audience without a map

Extend

10-minute exercise: Shoot three 10-second takes by the same window, changing only “Measure upload speed; 1.5 times the target bitrate”. Compare highlights, skin and background; keep the one that is editable.

3 checks

  • Uplink is at least 1.5 times the target bitrate and the margin is recorded.
  • When the primary drops, the hotspot backup and local recording still run.
  • The rehearsal log shows the dropout time, cause, and recovery duration.
