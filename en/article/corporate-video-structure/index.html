Corporate Video Structure: Six Beats

First decision: when to use it

Corporate video is not a company profile. Write a question the viewer wants answered, then prove with one person and one action that the company solves it.

Use it when there is one clear subject, one action, and a visible change in the final film. Do not use it when there is only a slogan, no person, and no process to shoot; go back to the brief before rolling.

Six beats: one job per beat

Write the six beats as six sentences, each answering “what does the viewer know now.” Shoot in order, but cut out of order; do not add empty shots to fill the structure.

  1. A concrete problem
  2. One person enters
  3. Old way fails
  4. Company intervenes
  5. Verifiable result
  6. One next step

How to shoot it: six tasks

At least one primary shot per beat; do not shoot everything in one framing and rely on jump cuts. Shoot irreplaceable action first—hands, face, machine—then pickups.

  • Problem: hands/desk close-up, no logo
  • Person: medium, act before talking
  • Failure: repeat the action, slow the cut
  • Intervention: product/process in one shot
  • Result: before/after or third-party proof
  • End: person leaves, one action remains

Common failures: three ways it collapses

1. Logo/drone first: the viewer does not know whose story this is. Replace beat one with a problem or action. 2. Narration in every beat: the images carry no information. Let one action speak. 3. Unverifiable result: “more efficient” with no before/after or third party. Shoot something checkable.

Cost and check: can it be shot in a day

Typically one shoot day plus two to three post days: half a day for primary shots, half for pickups; cut structure first, then sound. Check only three things: six beats complete, one primary shot per beat, result checkable. Do not accept “it feels premium.”

Check

  • Each of six beats is one clear sentence.
  • Beat one is a problem/action, not a logo.
  • At least one primary shot per beat.
  • Result has before/after or third party.
  • At least one irreplaceable action captured.
  • Six beats survive the rough cut.
