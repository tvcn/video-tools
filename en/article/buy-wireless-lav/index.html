Buying Wireless Lavs: Range, Dropouts, and Latency

A “500m” wireless lav range is open line-of-sight, not a mall or concert. Test dropouts, noise, latency, and battery in your real scene before choosing.

First decision: which link is missing

Check the scene: 2.4GHz is fine for indoor interviews; malls, concerts, and multiple kits favor UHF or digital systems with frequency hopping. Do not buy the cheapest dual kit; a dropout re-shoots the film. Test clothing rub and wind on the lav itself.

Fits:

  • Interview/voiceover/events
  • Solo/duo moving
  • Untethered audio

Does not fit:

  • Many kits in one venue
  • Heavy RF/long range
  • Broadcast-level redundancy

Test conditions: how to check before buying

This is not a cross-brand shootout and it does not settle brands. These three checks are a field protocol you can repeat in a shop or with a loaner; cells without measurements say “not measured” rather than inventing numbers. Record body, firmware, room temperature, recording format and card; change one condition and the result can change.

#How to testStatus
1Walk 10 m indoor and 30 m outdoorNot measured
2Block with one wall/body; log dropoutsNot measured
3Watch lip sync on monitor; measure latencyNot measured

Pass line: No drop at normal distance, recoverable dropouts, and latency under one frame.

Sources: editorial standards · test protocols; manufacturer specs come from public spec sheets, and untested values are not turned into numbers.

If one of the three can only be answered by “I heard” or a spec sheet, do not order yet. Write the condition into your shoot plan, then decide whether it changes your kit.

Test protocol: reproducible, no invented scores

Walk the real venue: turn away, cover with a hand, go behind a crowd, approach metal. Record 30 seconds each and listen for dropouts/noise. Clap for latency. Run two kits together for cross-interference.

Alternatives and upgrades: do not add money on the wrong axis

  • Wired lav + recorder
  • Rent pro wireless
  • Shotgun + recorder

Buying steps: bring footage, not spec sheets

Run these in order before paying. If one fails, do not tell yourself post will fix it.

  1. Walk the real venue
  2. Cover/turn dropout
  3. Clap latency
  4. Two-kit interference
  5. Price batteries/backup mic

Regional note for US / UK / EU readers

This English page keeps mainland-China examples where they are part of the method. Read platform names as examples, not as the only market: Douyin ↔ TikTok/Reels, Bilibili ↔ YouTube, Xiaohongshu ↔ Instagram/Pinterest; RMB figures are not converted here. Voltage, plug, warranty, import duty and radio certification differ by market; check the local spec sheet before ordering gear or batteries.

Image note. Covers, diagrams and step images on this page are editorial illustrations or structure diagrams, not photographs of a test performed here; they are not evidence of measured gear performance. Where an image is AI-assisted, the caption says so. Read the test conditions in the text before treating an image as proof.

6 checks

  • No dropout in the real venue.
  • No loss behind body/turns.
  • Lip-sync latency is acceptable.
  • Battery covers 8 hours.
  • Two kits do not cross.
  • Lav has no clothing rub/wind noise.
