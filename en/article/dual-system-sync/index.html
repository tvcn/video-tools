Dual-System Sync: Clap and Timecode

Clap first, then timecode; when both match, post does not have to listen to every take.

Dual-system sync claps first, then checks timecode; both must align before post stops listening shot by shot.

On set

Clap three times and record it on the recorder. Sync by waveform, then timecode, and keep drift under one frame.

Do this

  1. Clap three times while the recorder runs.
  2. Sync by waveform, then by timecode.
  3. Pass only under one frame of drift.

Avoid

Do not clap only once; if it misses, there is no second reference.

Three causes

For “Dual-System Sync: Clap and Timecode”, check three directions first. Change one variable, record a 30-second comparison, and see which link the change follows.

CauseSignal on setFive-minute test
No clapCamera and recorder share no marker, so sync is a guessClap before rolling; capture it in picture and sound
Timecode not syncedWrong jam or frame rate makes drift growJam once before the shoot
Wrong sample rate48kHz and 44.1kHz mixed together change pitchUse 48kHz throughout

On-set diagnosis

  1. Align the clap in the NLE and check whether the waveform peak meets the picture.
  2. Check frame rate and sample rate before aligning; unify first.
  3. Inspect the last section for drift; drift means timecode or clock trouble.

Fix

Dual-system sync does not rely on ears; it relies on a shared marker. A clap is the cheapest timecode; a timecode box is the steadier version.

  1. Clap for three seconds before every take and keep it in picture and sound.
  2. When using timecode, jam before the shoot and check again at wrap.
  3. Use 48kHz/24-bit and record a camera reference track.
  4. After syncing, check the whole take, especially the last section.

Failure cases

With “Dual-System Sync: Clap and Timecode”, these three moves make it worse. Pick one replacement action and do not change three variables at once.

FailureWhy it gets worseReplace with
Align by earTwo or three frames off reads as bad lip-syncClap/timecode
Check only the startThe head syncs and the tail driftsCheck the end
Ignore sample ratePitch rises or fallsUse 48kHz

One line for the set

No clap means no evidence; no timecode means no certainty.

Judge

This page passes on three observable checks, not slogans:

  • 1. Three claps are recorded by both camera and recorder.
  • 2. Waveform and timecode alignment are both performed.
  • 3. The two tracks drift under one frame.

Failures

Any one of these means stop and fix it before post:

  • 1. Check 1 is only staged (“Three claps are recorded by both camera and re…”); another camera position or location exposes it.
  • 2. Check 2 is left to post or a verbal explanation (“Waveform and timecode alignment are both perfo…”); nothing on set can be rechecked.
  • 3. Do not clap only once; if it misses, there is no second reference.

Extend

10-minute exercise: Run a 60-second test with the gear you already own, measuring only “Clap three times while the recorder runs.”. Record two numbers: what you need and what the gear actually gives.

Check

  • Three claps are recorded by both camera and recorder.
  • Waveform and timecode alignment are both performed.
  • The two tracks drift under one frame.
