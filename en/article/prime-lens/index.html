Prime Lens: Move Your Feet

A prime forces you to step in, back, and change position; that is often better than turning a zoom ring.

3 min read · Field noteTVCN Tools DeskEdited 2026-11

  4. Avoid
  5. Check
  6. Experience note

For fast coverage of many shot sizes or tight spaces, a zoom is more practical; a prime is not “more professional,” only more constrained.

What to compare first

  • Focal length and working distance: choose from camera-to-subject distance, not “most popular”; a 50mm can be too tight indoors.
  • Aperture and breathing: fast glass gives shallow depth and harder focus; focus breathing affects the shot.
  • Mount and weight: confirm mount, flange distance and filter thread; a heavy prime needs gimbal rebalance.

On set

Shoot a half-body frame at 35mm and move two meters forward and back to find the composition. Get used to walking before choosing 24 or 50.

Do this

A prime lens does not forbid zooming; it forces you to walk, back up, and change position, which is often more useful than turning a zoom ring.

  1. Shoot the same half-body portrait on a 35mm.
  2. Walk two meters forward and back to find the frame.
  3. Get used to moving before deciding between 24 and 50.

Avoid

Do not stay glued to the tripod; the prime's value is making you change position.

Check

  • At least three positions cover the same person at one focal length.
  • The background and perspective change from two meters of walking is described.
  • The 24 vs 50 decision comes from walking, not review videos.
