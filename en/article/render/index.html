Render: Samples and Noise

Samples and noise come before resolution; unresolved noise at 4K is just bigger dirt.

Lock the timeline, then the settings

Confirm frame rate, resolution, color space and audio sample rate; render settings must match the delivery sheet. Do not edit while rendering; the file will not match.

Play three points after render: opening, mid action, end credits. Check black frames, freezes, audio dropouts and caption overflow. Verify size and duration.

Limits

Rendering is not delivery. A successful render proves the file exists, not that it is correct; QC is a separate pass.

Rendering: lock samples before denoising

Render noise is not something denoise “wipes away.” With too few samples, denoise flattens detail. With enough samples, denoise is the last 10%. Stabilize samples and light bounces first, then denoise.

SettingTune firstSymptom
SamplesStart at 64, double upShadow noise, edge flicker
Bounces3–4, by scene complexityCrushed or washed shadows
DenoiseLast, preserve detailPlastic skin, lost texture
OutputMulti-channel EXRNo separate light control in post

Project-level checks

  1. Render a 50% test first to confirm composition, materials and light. Do not wait for 4K to discover a problem.
  2. Double samples; noise should drop clearly. If it does not, the problem is lighting or materials, not samples.
  3. Save before/after denoise and compare pores, cloth and hair. If detail disappears, lower the strength.
  4. Output EXR with Beauty, Light, Reflection and Depth. Keep an adjustable version, not only the MP4.

When the farm is queued, submit a low-sample preview to hold the shot, then render final. Do not use final settings as a test.

On set

Render at 50 percent resolution with low samples to see where noise lives. Raise light and material samples separately; do not just raise the total.

Do this

Rendering checks samples and noise before resolution; unresolved noise at 4K is just bigger dirt.

  1. Render at 50 percent resolution and low samples to see noise distribution.
  2. Adjust light samples and material samples separately.
  3. Do not only raise total samples; find which source causes the noise.

Avoid

Do not brute-force noise with total samples; without the source, render time burns.

3 checks

  • A 50 percent low-sample pass shows whether noise comes from light or material.
  • Light and material samples were adjusted separately.
  • Final noise is traced to a specific source, not brute-forced.
