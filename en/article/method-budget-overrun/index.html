The Budget Is Over

Stop nonessential gear first; overruns usually come from scope, not unit price.

Find which bucket is over

  • Prep: gear, location or crew days grew. Cut scope, never insurance or safety.
  • Shoot: losing one shot a day for three days sinks post. Re-rank the remaining shots by “can we deliver without it?”
  • Post: unlimited revisions or more footage than budgeted. Put revision rounds in the contract and price them.

A ten-minute table

Split the budget into prep, shoot, post and contingency. Keep at least 10% buffer in each. List actual and forecast overruns in two columns, then work only the top three forecast items. Do not hide the gap with “we'll figure it out.”

When to stop

When an overrun threatens safety, insurance or delivery quality, stop and renegotiate scope. That is more professional than pushing to a film nobody can use. A client can accept one missing shot; not an unusable master.

When the budget overruns, start with three sheets

Overruns are rarely one expensive item. They are missing line items, changes and cash timing. Split the budget into cost, cash flow and change sheets. If the three do not reconcile, an unwritten assumption is still hiding.

SheetWhat goes inCommon omissions
CostShoot days, post days, gear, locations, travel, taxTransport, insurance, overtime, backups, platform fees
Cash flowWhen money arrives, when it leavesClient approval cycles, invoice mail, deposit returns
ChangeScope, schedule and cost of each additionVerbal promises, free pickups, late script changes

Contingency is not profit

Keep 10–15% contingency on total cost, on its own line, outside margin. It has three uses: pickups, delays and gear replacement. Record every spend. If unused, do not distribute it early as a bonus; the next overrun will have no buffer.

The order of response after an overrun

  1. Stop new purchases and confirm what is already owed.
  2. Classify change-sheet items as client-caused, self-caused or force majeure.
  3. Client-caused gets a change order; self-caused comes from contingency; force majeure follows the contract.
  4. Do not hide it as “the next job will make it up.” The next job has its own costs.

Run a 30-minute close-out: which line exceeded 10%, why, and which sheet it enters next time. “Control the budget” is not an action; write the specific move.

On set

Stop nonessential gear and change scope. Overruns usually come from scope, not unit price.

Do this

Over budget: stop optional gear first, then talk about people. Overruns usually come from scope, not unit price.

  1. List spending as incurred and pending; cut pending nonessentials first.
  2. Compare with the original budget and name the scope change: extra locations, days, revision rounds.
  3. Quote the addition to the client; do not absorb it silently.

Avoid

Do not fill the hole with a lower price; the next job gets harder.

Boundary

It does not guarantee a story. The template only orders structure; the person, obstacle and cost still need writing.

Alternative

If “List spending as incurred and pending; cut pending” is not the main problem, start with the basic move behind “Compare with the original budget and name the”. Upgrade only after both hold.

60-second test

Run a 60-second test with the gear you own, focused on “List spending as incurred and pending; cut pending”, and record one number. If the number does not change, do not add budget.

When not to use

Do not use the template when “List spending as incurred and pending; cut pending” is still unclear or “Compare with the original budget and name the” is only filler. Go back to the person and the problem.

Three failures

  • Every beat exists, but no one makes a decision.
  • The middle changes location, not information.
  • The ending is narrated, with no visible change.

3 checks

  • Spending is split into incurred and pending; pending nonessentials are cut.
  • The scope change is named: locations, days, or revision rounds.
  • The addition is quoted to the client, not absorbed.
