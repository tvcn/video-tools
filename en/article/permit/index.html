Permits: Parks, Transit, Shops

A permit is not paper; it decides whether you can stand a light, block a walkway, or frame a sign.

Permits have three layers: private property needs the owner; public space may need city or police permission; special areas—airport, subway, park, historic building—have their own authority. Call first; do not gamble on the day.

Ask whose ground, road and air first

Put permit number, contact, valid window and restrictions on the call sheet so everyone knows what not to touch.

Limits

A small crew is not a waiver. Fewer people and a small camera may lower the approval level, but do not remove it; enforcement varies by city.

On set

Ask three questions: can you rig lights, can you block the sidewalk, can you show the storefront? Put the permit contact on the call sheet.

Do this

A permit is not a piece of paper; it decides whether you can light, block a walkway, or show a storefront. Ask three questions before choosing the shot.

  1. Ask: can we set a light, can we block the pavement, can we show the storefront?
  2. Put the answers and permit contact in the call sheet and mark restricted areas.
  3. If one answer is unclear, change the shot or the plan; do not wing it on the day.

Avoid

Do not treat “we'll shoot quickly and leave” as permission; security can stop both the camera and the footage.

Judge

Three signals are observable on set:

  • highlights are not clipped and shadows keep recoverable detail
  • skin stays consistent across camera angles
  • sound has an independent track, not just the on-camera mic

Failures

These three moves erase the detail you just built:

  • exposure is right but the light has no direction
  • sound relies on the camera mic and dies in a noisy room
  • crossing the line leaves the audience without a map

Extend

10-minute exercise: Shoot three 10-second takes by the same window, changing only “Ask: can we set a light, can we”. Compare highlights, skin and background; keep the one that is editable.

3 checks

  • Lighting, pavement, and storefront questions all have clear answers.
  • The call sheet lists the permit contact, valid hours, and restricted areas.
  • If one item was denied, the shot or plan changed instead of being forced on location.
