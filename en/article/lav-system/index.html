Wireless Lav: Frequency and Backup

Check frequency and interference, and record a backup track; the set gives no second chance.

3 min read · Field noteTVCN Tools DeskEdited 2026-11

  2. When not to use it

What to compare first

  • Band and power: choose a band legal where you shoot; more power is not better, and crowded city bands drop more often.
  • Redundancy and monitoring: at least two channels or two systems; onboard recording and receiver monitoring are what make a backup.
  • Batteries and connectors: confirm battery type, runtime and locking connectors; a dead battery or loose plug is worse than average sound.

When not to use it

In heavy RF, long range or many simultaneous speakers, wireless lav is not the only answer. Add a shotgun, recorder or wired backup; one wireless link is not insurance.

On set

Scan frequencies for two minutes and pick a clean band. Hide the transmitter at the back waist and record a 32-bit backup.

Do this

Wireless lavs need a frequency scan and a backup track; the set gives no second chance.

  1. Scan for two minutes and pick a clean frequency.
  2. Hide the transmitter at the lower back with the cable inside clothing.
  3. Record a 32-bit backup track.

Avoid

Do not record on the factory frequency; one interference hit ruins the whole take.

3 checks

  • A two-minute scan logs a clean frequency.
  • The transmitter sits at the lower back with no visible cable.
  • A 32-bit backup track exists and works independently.
