A usable 60-second segment table

SegmentTimePictureSound / pass line
Result0–5sShow the finished result firstOne “what you will get” line
Goal5–15sCompare result and starting pointState prerequisites and materials
Step 115–25sHands operate; key control enlargedOne action per step; an on-screen cue
Step 225–40sChange one condition, still completesName the common error; do not skip it
Step 340–55sFinish and replay the resultThe viewer can judge success alone
Check55–60sFreeze result, list checks on screenThree self-checks; never “done is enough”

Fill three lines before rolling

  • Result: what does the viewer end with? A screenshot or file.
  • Three steps: for each, write what proves it worked, not “click here.”
  • Failure: which step goes wrong most? Demonstrate it early.

On set

Goal, three steps, check. Show the finished result before asking the viewer to follow.

Do this

A tutorial template uses goal, steps, acceptance; every step has an observable result.

  1. State the goal: what the viewer will have at the end.
  2. Split into three steps, each with an observable result.
  3. Give acceptance: how viewers know they did it right.

Avoid

Do not use “when done” as acceptance; viewers do not know what done means.

Image note. Covers, diagrams and step images on this page are editorial illustrations or structure diagrams, not photographs of a test performed here; they are not evidence of measured gear performance. Where an image is AI-assisted, the caption says so. Read the test conditions in the text before treating an image as proof.

3 checks

  • The opening states what the viewer will have.
  • Each of three steps has an observable result, such as an on-screen prompt.
  • Acceptance can be self-checked, not felt.
