News Breakdown: Keep the Unknown in Ninety Seconds

An eight-beat breakdown of a ninety-second news feature: verified fact, scene, witness, archive, context, known/unknown, unresolved, next.

Fix Three Variables First

Fix three variables: ninety seconds, institutional/news platform, and the goal is separating known, unknown, and inferred. Do not watch for dramatic shots; watch for a source behind every fact.

Sample note: the timecodes below are a teaching sample for this format's usual length. They do not correspond to one specific film and are not frame-accurate analysis. Use the same positions on your own film; do not treat these beats as the only answer.

Timecode Breakdown: 8 Beats

Watch in order; do not read commentary first. For each beat record four things: picture, sound, job, and steal or do-not-copy.

  1. 0:00–0:05 | Picture: one verified fact/place, no emotional verdict. Sound: sync. Job: certainty first. Steal: open on a fact. Do not copy: open on viral footage.
  2. 0:05–0:15 | Picture: scene: signs, street, notice board. Sound: ambience. Job: give the fact a place. Steal: three geographic details. Do not copy: staged empties.
  3. 0:15–0:30 | Picture: witness/subject, no conclusion for them. Sound: sync. Job: give the story a person. Steal: ask what they saw. Do not copy: ask “are you angry.”
  4. 0:30–0:45 | Picture: archive, labeled date/source. Sound: voice reads facts only. Job: add time. Steal: label plus one source line. Do not copy: pass archive as firsthand.
  5. 0:45–1:00 | Picture: context: rules/timeline/what happened before. Sound: ambience lowers. Job: give the fact context. Steal: one timeline. Do not copy: a long narration.
  6. 1:00–1:12 | Picture: known/unknown two-column card. Sound: voice reads both columns. Job: show the boundary. Steal: two columns. Do not copy: fill the unknown with speculation.
  7. 1:12–1:25 | Picture: unresolved: unverified point/refused interview. Sound: sync/silence. Job: keep the unknown. Steal: say “we do not know.” Do not copy: music as conspiracy.
  8. 1:25–1:30 | Picture: next: how it will be checked/when updated. Sound: one closing line. Job: give an action. Steal: one concrete check. Do not copy: “stay tuned.”

Read the Structure

The spine is fact, place, person, archive, context, known/unknown, unresolved, next. It need not tell a complete story; it must show what is certain and what is not.

Ask only three questions: what proof arrives in the first ten seconds; whether every ten to fifteen seconds adds information; whether the end lands one action, one change, or one question. If any is missing, a 90 sec film falls apart.

Budget Order of Magnitude

Budget is a rough mainland-China production range, excluding media spend; it is an order of magnitude, not a quote. At the same length, a stage, cast, effects, or music rights can each double the cost.

  • Low: RMB 2,000–8,000, one to two people, single camera, one day.
  • Mid: RMB 8,000–30,000, three to five people, two cameras, sound, post, one to two days.
  • High: RMB 30,000–100,000+, reporter, camera, producer, legal, multiple locations, three to seven days.

Three Structures You Can Steal

Steal structure, not images. These three still work when you change the subject.

  1. Open on one verified fact, not viral footage.
  2. Make known/unknown a two-column card.
  3. Label every archive shot with date and source.

Three Reasons Not to Copy

Not “bad,” but built on different conditions; copying exposes your budget, platform, and audience.

  1. Do not copy breaking-news speed: without verification, speed amplifies error.
  2. Do not copy emotional music: news is not a tearjerker; music implies a verdict.
  3. Do not copy archive montage: without labels, archive becomes fake firsthand.

Breakdown Exercises

Do them before shooting; do not just read. Do the three in order, and the third must become a fifteen-second file.

  1. Write known/unknown for one trending story; source every known.
  2. Count labeled archive shots; mark any missing as unverified.
  3. Compress to thirty seconds: fact, witness, unknown, next.

Regional note for US / UK / EU readers

This English page keeps mainland-China examples where they are part of the method. Read platform names as examples, not as the only market: Douyin ↔ TikTok/Reels, Bilibili ↔ YouTube, Xiaohongshu ↔ Instagram/Pinterest; RMB figures are not converted here. 3 checks

  • On-camera, caption, and secondhand sources are counted separately.
  • Every key fact traces to a source.
  • All secondhand footage is labeled and not passed as firsthand.
