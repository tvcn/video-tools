Coverage: Do Not Shoot One Angle

Coverage is not more takes; it gives the edit different information: wide, close, insert, reverse.

Every scene needs at least three kinds of material: a safe master that explains the space, a reverse or over-the-shoulder, and two or three insertable action details. The edit uses them to change pace, hide cheats and rescue performance.

Coverage gives the edit choices

Before the shoot, list coverage as master–reverse–insert, and write what must be captured to pass. Do not shoot whatever looks interesting on the day.

Limits

More angles do not equal an editable scene. Every angle must share the same action line; if the actor does something different in each take, more coverage makes the cut harder.

On set

Shoot four pieces of one scene: a wide for space, a medium for relationships, a close for reaction, and a hand insert for information. Get at least two takes of each and play them in order on the monitor.

Do this

Coverage is not shooting one angle ten times; it gives the edit different information: a wide for space, a medium for relationship, a close-up for reaction, an insert for detail.

  1. Shoot a wide that explains the whole scene, at least two takes.
  2. Add medium, close-up, and hand inserts, at least two takes per size.
  3. Shoot reverse angles and transitions, then review in order to confirm every beat has an alternative.

Avoid

Do not shoot the whole scene in one size; that is repetition, not coverage.

Judge

This page passes on three observable checks, not slogans:

  • 1. Wide, medium, close-up, and insert all have at least one usable take.
  • 2. The hand or prop insert carries a story beat without voice-over.
  • 3. Every information beat has two usable sizes on the timeline; a missing one triggers a pickup.

Failures

Any one of these means stop and fix it before post:

  • 1. Check 1 is only staged (“Wide, medium, close-up, and insert all have at…”); another camera position or location exposes it.
  • 2. Check 2 is left to post or a verbal explanation (“The hand or prop insert carries a story beat w…”); nothing on set can be rechecked.
  • 3. Do not shoot the whole scene in one size; that is repetition, not coverage.

Extend

10-minute exercise: Shoot three 10-second takes by the same window, changing only “Shoot a wide that explains the whole scene,”. Compare highlights, skin and background; keep the one that is editable.
