Text-to-video test: run and record

A text-to-video test answers three questions: subject stability, believable action and whether it cuts to the next shot. This page gives fixed prompts, a score sheet and a record template; no invented scores.

Fit and no-fit / 适用与不适用

  • Creators running their own same-prompt comparison.
  • Producers needing a reproducible score sheet.
  • Not for: readers wanting “which model is #1.”

Do this / 怎么做

  1. Freeze six prompts: face/hands/motion/product/text/long take.
  2. Three runs, same resolution, duration and audio setting.
  3. Log failure type first: warp/slide/flicker/typo/desync.
  4. Then rate 1–5 with two blind reviewers.
  5. Publish prompt, reference, version, date; no aggregate ranking.

Avoid / 别踩

  • Different prompt per run; results incomparable.
  • Showing only successes; failure rate hidden.
  • Using “cinematic” as a score; not reproducible.

Go deeper / 继续往下

Text-to-video's difficulty is “same prompt.” Models are sensitive to word order, punctuation and language; translating changes results. Freeze the original, run Chinese and English separately and label language.

Do not score only a total. Rate subject, action, light and cut separately so readers know whether 8 means a stable face or good light. Failure type beats score; it tells you how to rewrite.

Checks / 验收

  • Six prompts, three runs, two raters recorded.
  • Failure type logged before score.
  • Resolution/duration/audio fixed.
  • No “#1/strongest” claim.

Regional note for US / UK / EU readers

This English page keeps mainland-China examples where they are part of the method. Read platform names as examples, not as the only market: Douyin ↔ TikTok/Reels, Bilibili ↔ YouTube, Xiaohongshu ↔ Instagram/Pinterest; RMB figures are not converted here. Model availability, training-data rules, disclosure duties and privacy law differ by market; check the EU AI Act, UK GDPR and your local platform policy before publishing.
