2D Camera: Pan and Multiplane

A 2D camera is not one big pan; multiple layers at different speeds create space.

A 2D camera is a stage, not a 3D scene

Camera moves in 2D start with layer layout: foreground, middle and background on separate planes, moving at different speeds to create space. Move them together and the audience sees a sliding drawing.

For a push-in, decide whether to move the camera or scale the layer; scaling softens, camera movement stays sharp.

Limits

A 2D camera cannot replace perspective. If layer scale is wrong, complex moves still look like paper; draw the space first.

A 2D camera is not a zoom; it is layered parallax

The point of a 2D camera move is not to push the whole image closer, but to move foreground, midground and background at different speeds. The speed difference creates space. Without layers, even a large move looks like a flat image scaling.

LayerSpeedCommon error
ForegroundFastest; may leave frameScales with background, loses occlusion
MidgroundMedium; carries subjectMotion blur points the wrong way
BackgroundSlowest; needs overscanEdges show after scaling

Project-level checks

  1. Build the background at twice final size to give the camera room. Do not push a final-size plate.
  2. Use two or three camera keyframes with ease in/out. Linear keys feel like a conveyor belt.
  3. Tune motion blur per layer and speed; foreground and background should not share one setting.
  4. Toggle each layer before output; check blank edges, seams and repeated texture.

For acceptance, watch the film at 50%. If space remains, the layers work. If it only “gets bigger,” return to layer speed.

On set

Split the background into far, middle, and near layers and pan them at 1:2:4 speed. Keep the character on its own layer, still.

Do this

2D camera moves are not one flat pan; layers at different speeds create space.

  1. Split the background into far mountain, midground, near trees.
  2. Pan at speeds of 1:2:4.
  3. Keep the character on a separate layer, still or with a small follow.

Avoid

Do not pan the whole image at one speed; equal layers flatten the space.

3 checks

  • Far, mid, and near layers move at different speeds near 1:2:4.
  • The character layer is separate and does not slide with the background.
  • One pan shows near-fast, far-slow parallax.
