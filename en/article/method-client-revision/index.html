The Client Keeps Revising

Write the objective in one sentence and test every revision against it.

When the client says “one more version,” ask which problem it solves: pacing, information or emotion. Then offer two options with their costs. Let them choose rather than redefine at the next meeting.

Turn revisions into options, not infinity

The contract states revision rounds and scope per round; new requests outside scope get a separate quote.

Limits

Do not hide scope creep behind “the client is always right.” Professionalism is showing the cost of each choice, not unlimited compliance.

Write revisions as enforceable terms, not feelings

When a client says “it doesn't feel right,” it is usually not taste. It is the absence of a shared test. Revision terms must answer three things: what changes, how many rounds, and when it is done.

After two rounds, quote new work

The contract includes two revision rounds; round three bills by hour or extra edit day. This is not punishment; it makes cost visible. If round three introduces a structural change, stop, rewrite the script, then decide whether to continue.

Notes need a timecode and an action

Do not accept “make it more premium.” Ask for “00:12 subtitle is too fast, hold two seconds” or “00:20 music is too full, bring it to -18 LUFS.” One note solves one point, so nobody guesses.

Three signals of scope creep

  • “By the way” requests: an extra product close-up, an English version, a BTS cut.
  • The decision-maker changes: the original contact no longer approves; a new leader restarts notes.
  • Deliverables multiply: one film becomes three ratios, two languages and a social cut.

When any appears, send a one-page change order: new scope, new schedule, new cost. Start only after written approval.

On set

Write the goal in one sentence and check every revision against it. Quote separately for scope creep.

Do this

Endless client revisions: write the goal in one sentence, then judge every change against it.

  1. Put one line in the brief: “this film makes whom do what,” and get both sides to confirm.
  2. Collect notes in rounds, one category per round; quote extra rounds beyond the contract.
  3. After each change, ask whether it makes the goal clearer or fuzzier.

Avoid

Do not accept every note under “the client is always right”; unlimited revisions sink the project.

Boundary

It does not guarantee a story. The template only orders structure; the person, obstacle and cost still need writing.

Alternative

If “Put one line in the brief: “this film” is not the main problem, start with the basic move behind “Collect notes in rounds, one category per round;”. Upgrade only after both hold.

60-second test

Run a 60-second test with the gear you own, focused on “Put one line in the brief: “this film”, and record one number. If the number does not change, do not add budget.

When not to use

Do not use the template when “Put one line in the brief: “this film” is still unclear or “Collect notes in rounds, one category per round;” is only filler. Go back to the person and the problem.

Three failures

  • Every beat exists, but no one makes a decision.
  • The middle changes location, not information.
  • The ending is narrated, with no visible change.

3 checks

  • Notes arrive in rounds, one category per round.
  • Extra rounds are quoted; revisions are not unlimited.
