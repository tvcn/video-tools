Wireless Video: Latency and Interference

Test latency and interference before sending to director or focus puller; a dropped signal stops the set.

3 min read · Field noteTVCN Tools DeskEdited 2026-11

For critical live, long range or heavy RF, prefer wired or local recording; wireless video is convenience, not a reliability guarantee.

What to compare first

  • Latency: confirm end-to-end delay, not the “low latency” ad; follow focus and live broadcast have different limits.
  • Band and interference: check available bands, frequency hopping and antennas; on-site Wi-Fi and crowds compete.
  • Power and backup: both transmitter and receiver must hot-swap batteries; without a wired backup, do not use it for a critical live show.

On set

Walk the set and test latency and dropouts. Give the director and the focus puller separate signals; do not share one.

Do this

Wireless video needs latency and interference tests before deciding director or focus use; a dropout stops the set.

  1. Walk the set and measure latency and dropouts.
  2. Keep director and focus signals separate.
  3. Log dropout spots and add antennas or change channels.

Avoid

Do not share one wireless link between director and focus; one dropout stops both.

Check

  • Latency and dropouts are logged across the set.
  • Director and focus monitoring use two separate links.
  • Dropout spots have a response, such as antennas or channel change.
