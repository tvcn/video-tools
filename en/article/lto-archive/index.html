LTO Archive: Cold, Cheap, Slow

LTO is for cold archive, not same-day editing; calculate restore time before cost per TB.

3 min read · Field noteTVCN Tools DeskEdited 2026-11

What to compare first

  • Generation compatibility: confirm which LTO generation the drive and tapes read and write; a new drive usually reads one generation back, never forward.
  • Software and index: LTO without a catalog and checksums is just a tape; a restore needs to know which volume and partition holds the file.
  • Environment and rotation: tape dislikes humidity, dust and temperature swings; keep at least two copies off-site and plan migration to new media.

When not to use it

For random access, frequent edits or projects under 10TB, LTO is not economical. Use drives plus cold cloud first; move to tape when the archive really grows.

On set

Calculate restore time: how long to copy 1 TB from LTO to disk. If it exceeds the project window, do not use it as a working drive.

Do this

LTO suits long-term cold archive, not same-day editing; calculate restore time before cost per TB.

  1. Time a 1 TB restore from LTO to disk.
  2. If it exceeds the project cycle, it cannot be a work disk.
  3. Then calculate cost per TB and storage conditions.

Avoid

Do not use LTO as an editing disk; a 1 TB restore takes hours and today's cut will not finish.

3 checks

  • A 1 TB restore is timed.
  • Restore time is compared with the project cycle and a conclusion is stated.
  • Cost per TB and storage conditions are recorded.
