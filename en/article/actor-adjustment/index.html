Adjusting Actors: Give Verbs, Not Adjectives

Give actors verbs: slower, hold the anger, do not touch her. Never "be more natural."

Give an action, not an emotion adjective

“Be sadder” rarely works. Replace it with a playable action: push the cup to the edge, glance then look down, stop mid-sentence. Change the action and the feeling follows.

Adjust one action at a time. Three notes at once split attention and leave only “acting.”

Limits

Do not perform it for them. A demonstration is allowed, but they must then do it their way; a copy puts a second director in the frame.

On set

Give one verb at a time, then watch the take. If it is still wrong, change the verb, not the adjective. When the actor asks why, say do it first, we will talk after.

Do this

Give the actor a verb: walk slower, hold the anger, do not touch her. Never “be more natural.”

  1. Give one verb at a time, then watch playback.
  2. If it is still wrong, change the verb, not the adjective.
  3. If the actor asks why, say “do it first, we'll explain after.”

Avoid

Do not give three adjectives at once; the actor plays “confused,” not the character.

3 checks

  • Each adjustment gives one verb with no adjective stack.
  • Two takes differ by one variable and the difference can be named.
  • The actor can repeat their next action.
