Buying a Zoom: Parfocal, Breathing, and Servo

A zoom buys “no lens change,” not “anything.” Confirm it holds focus through the zoom, breathing is controlled, and the servo is quiet; otherwise you bought a prime that can change focal length.

First decision: which link is missing

Zooms suit events, weddings, documentary, and solo moving work; they do not suit maximum shallow depth or minimum weight. Decide the range: 24-70 covers most, 70-200 suits stage/events, 18-35 suits spaces. Do not buy one 18-300 as everything.

Fits:

  • Events/weddings, no lens changes
  • Solo moving, efficiency
  • Documentary, fast framing

Does not fit:

  • Maximum aperture/shallow depth
  • Long handheld, weight-sensitive
  • Macro/extreme close focus

Test protocol: reproducible, no invented scores

Focus at the long end, zoom wide, and see if focus holds. Then push/pull close and watch breathing. Finally record a zoom and listen to the servo.

Alternatives and upgrades: do not add money on the wrong axis

  • Two primes instead of one zoom
  • Rent a 70-200 for events
  • Start with a kit zoom

Buying steps: bring footage, not spec sheets

Run these in order before paying. If one fails, do not tell yourself post will fix it.

  1. List three main scene types
  2. Focus long, zoom wide
  3. Close push/pull for breathing
  4. Record servo noise
  5. Weigh and hold five minutes

Check

  • Range covers main scenes.
  • Parfocal passes.
  • Breathing is acceptable.
  • Servo is quiet.
  • Handhold five minutes.
  • Filter size is reusable.
