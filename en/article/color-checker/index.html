Color Checker: Shoot It Before the Scene

Shoot the chart under scene light before the take; after the first setup, the light has changed.

The chart is not an opening ritual; it is an anchor for post. At each scene, lamp change or color-temperature change, fill one third of frame with the chart, sharp and correctly exposed. Do not shoot it only on take one.

Shoot a color chart every time the light changes

Use it to calibrate white balance and skin, then use scopes to match shots. The chart does not replace creative judgment.

Limits

A chart cannot fix mixed light. With two temperatures in one frame, it gives one reference; fix the light on set first, then shoot the chart.

A color chart is a coordinate, not a ceremony

Place the chart where the subject stands, in the same light, for 3–5 seconds. If the light changes, the chart is invalid. In post, use the neutral patch for white balance, then trim with skin.

  1. Shoot gray first, then the chart. Expose gray to 50–60% on the waveform, not clipped.
  2. Reshoot for every scene or color-temperature change; one chart cannot cover the day.
  3. Match cameras first, then add the look. The style LUT goes after the match.

Acceptance: compare chart frame and final; neutral gray should be neutral and skin natural. Green or magenta cast means check on-set white balance.

On set

Shoot the color chart at the head, filling a third of the frame. Match with the chart in post; do not grade from memory.

Do this

Shoot a color chart under the scene light for a post reference; shooting it later means the light has changed.

  1. Shoot the chart at the start.
  2. Fill about one third of the frame.
  3. Match in post from the chart, not memory.

Avoid

Do not shoot the chart at wrap; the light no longer matches the first take.

Judge

This page passes on three observable checks, not slogans:

  • 1. The chart is shot at the start under scene light.
  • 2. It fills about a third of frame with no glare on patches.
  • 3. Post matching uses the chart, not memory.

Failures

Any one of these means stop and fix it before post:

  • 1. Check 1 is only staged (“The chart is shot at the start under scene lig…”); another camera position or location exposes it.
  • 2. Check 2 is left to post or a verbal explanation (“It fills about a third of frame with no glare…”); nothing on set can be rechecked.
  • 3. Do not shoot the chart at wrap; the light no longer matches the first take.

Extend

10-minute exercise: Run a 60-second test with the gear you already own, measuring only “Shoot the chart at the start.”. Record two numbers: what you need and what the gear actually gives.

Check

  • The chart is shot at the start under scene light.
  • It fills about a third of frame with no glare on patches.
  • Post matching uses the chart, not memory.
