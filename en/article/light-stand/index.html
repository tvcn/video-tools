Light Stand: Sandbag Everything

Sandbag the stand before raising the light; an unweighted stand costs more than the lamp when it falls.

What to compare first

  • Payload and height: calculate light plus softbox plus sandbag, not the head alone; the center changes as it rises.
  • Legs and locks: legs lock without slipping and offer leveling; it must stand on uneven ground.
  • Sandbags and tie-downs: at least one sandbag per stand; outdoors it must take weight and rope, not rely on legs alone.

When not to use it

For a large softbox or height above a person's head, a small stand tips; use a heavy stand or truss. Do not use a backpack as a sandbag.

Light stand safety: sandbags at least twice the fixture weight

A falling stand is worse than a dim lamp. Put at least two sandbags on every stand, total weight at least twice the fixture. When a boom arm extends, counterweight the other side.

  1. Hang bags at the base tripod, not at the end of the arm; end weight increases tipping torque.
  2. Tie cables along the stand and leave a trip loop. Do not cross a walkway.
  3. Lock casters before raising, lock the arm after. At wrap, lower the lamp before removing bags.

Acceptance: push the stand lightly; it should spring back, not keep falling. If it keeps falling, add weight or lower it.

On set

Hang two sandbags on every stand and push it gently at full height. If it moves, lower it before rolling.

Do this

Sandbag the stand before raising the light; an unweighted stand costs more when it falls than the lamp did.

  1. Hang two sandbags on each stand.
  2. Raise to full height and push gently.
  3. Only power on if it does not wobble; otherwise lower it or add weight.

Avoid

Do not raise the light before finding sandbags; at full height it is already unmanageable.

Check

  • Each stand carries two sandbags with recorded weight.
  • A gentle push at full height does not wobble.
  • Any wobbling stand is lowered or weighted before power-on.
