Overheating Fix: Cool, Segment, Lower Bitrate

Cool it, shoot in segments, lower bitrate; do not put a wet towel on the body.

Drop specs before the camera dies

For long takes, lower resolution, frame rate, bitrate and screen brightness to a stable setting; disable IBIS and wireless, remove the battery to cool. Check temperature every twenty to thirty minutes and swap cards early.

Do not blow a fan directly at the sensor; it adds vibration and dust. Use vents and shade.

Limits

Overheating is not “broken.” It appears with heat, direct sun and high bitrate together; change conditions before blaming the body.

On set

Lower the bitrate, turn off stabilization, open the battery door, and shoot in shorter takes. Never put a wet towel on the body.

Do this

Overheating: cool it, shoot in blocks, lower the bitrate. Do not put a wet towel on the body.

  1. Lower resolution or bitrate and turn off in-body stabilization and long monitoring.
  2. Open the battery door or add a heat sink; keep the body out of direct sun.
  3. Stop for five minutes every twenty and shoot in shorter blocks.

Avoid

Do not use a wet towel or ice pack directly; condensation shorts the camera.

3 checks

  • Resolution or bitrate is lower and stabilization and long monitoring are off.
  • The battery door or heat sink is handled and the body is out of direct sun.
  • The camera stops five minutes every twenty and shoots in blocks.
