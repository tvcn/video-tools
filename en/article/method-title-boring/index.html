The Title Says Nothing

Write who meets what result; more adjectives, fewer clicks.

A title with no information: write “who hits what result.” More adjectives means fewer clicks.

On set

Write who meets what result. Fewer adjectives. The title and body must be the same story.

Do this

  1. Write three titles using person plus action plus result or cost.
  2. Cut empty words such as “shocking,” “premium,” and “must-see.”
  3. Choose one that matches the body exactly; do not bait.

Avoid

Do not trade an exaggerated promise for clicks; when the body cannot cash it, the audience leaves.

Three causes

For “The Title Says Nothing”, check three directions first. Change one variable, record a 30-second comparison, and see which link the change follows.

CauseSignal on setFive-minute test
Abstract nouns only“Growth”, “choice” and “goodbye” have no object, so nothing seems to happenReplace the noun with a person and an action
No conflictThe title gives the conclusion and the piece has no questionAdd a concrete obstacle or cost
Keyword stuffingThe title reads like a search box and never says whose story it isRemove two keywords and keep one action

On-set diagnosis

  1. Read the title to someone who has not seen the piece and ask what they think will happen.
  2. If they can only repeat abstract words, the title is under-specified.
  3. Compare title and first scene: does the promised action appear?

Fix

A good title is not a summary of theme; it is a person, an action and a cost. It should predict the first scene, not explain the film.

  1. Write “who + wants what + what blocks it”, then cut to twelve words or fewer.
  2. Put the most specific prop in the title: the cat, the old coat, the last delivery.
  3. Keep one unknown: do not reveal the result, only the price.
  4. If it sounds like a paper, rewrite it as something a character would say.

Failure cases

With “The Title Says Nothing”, these three moves make it worse. Pick one replacement action and do not change three variables at once.

FailureWhy it gets worseReplace with
ClickbaitThe piece cannot pay off the title and the audience leavesPromise only an action the first scene can deliver
RestatementTitle and summary say the same thingTitle gives action; summary gives conditions
Keyword pileIt reads like a tag listKeep at most one search phrase

Acceptance table

Acceptance is not “it feels better”; all four must be identifiable, playable and logged.

CheckPass lineLog
Concrete subjectA person or object, not only an abstract nounCircle the nouns
ActionThe title answers what happensAsk a stranger to retell it
CostThe title implies what may be lostWrite one cost line
PayoffThe first scene contains the promised actionCompare with the body

Boundary

This page only covers “The Title Says Nothing”; it does not replace The Film Is Too Long, The Transition Is Jarring, 16mm: Cinema Left the Theater. When the problem belongs to one of those pages, use that page instead of adding more parameters here.

Alternative

If “Write three titles using person plus action plus result or cost” is not the main problem, start with “Cut empty words such as “shocking,” “premium,” and “must-see.””; only upgrade after both hold.

60-second test

Run one 60-second test against check 1: The title contains person plus action plus result or cost. If check 2 (Empty words such as “shocking,” “premium,” and “must-see” are gone) does not change, do not add gear or budget.

Check

  • The title contains person plus action plus result or cost.
  • Empty words such as “shocking,” “premium,” and “must-see” are gone.
  • The title matches the body exactly; there is no bait.
