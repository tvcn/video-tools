Lens Field-of-View Calculator: Focal Length, Sensor, and Horizontal Angle

Enter sensor width and focal length to estimate horizontal field of view, and decide whether a lens is wide enough for a room, a landscape, or a close-up.

What this calculator solves

“Is a 24mm wide enough indoors?” “Will an 85mm feel too tight for a person?” Do not guess. Estimate the horizontal angle first.

Formula

θ = 2 × arctan((w) / (2f))

Where w is sensor width, f is focal length, and θ is the horizontal field of view.

Common sensor widths

  • Full frame: 36mm
  • APS-C: 23.5mm
  • M4/3: 17.3mm

How to use it

  1. Choose the sensor width.
  2. Enter the focal length.
  3. Read the horizontal angle.
  4. If the angle is too narrow for the space, go wider; if faces distort, go longer.

Three checks

  • You can explain what wide and long lenses change, beyond “zoom.”
  • You know the same focal length gives a different angle on different sensor sizes.
  • You can use the angle to estimate camera distance.

Boundary

This is horizontal field of view. It does not include distortion, focus breathing, or real-world lens marking error. Cinema lenses should be measured on set.

Formula

Horizontal angle = 2 × atan(sensor width ÷ (2 × focal length)), in degrees. Full frame is 36mm wide: 24mm ≈ 73.7°, 35mm ≈ 54.4°, 50mm ≈ 39.6°, 85mm ≈ 23.9°.

Conversion example

APS-C (23.5mm wide) with a 35mm lens: 2 × atan(23.5 ÷ 70) ≈ 37.1°, close to a 52mm full-frame view. That is where “equivalent focal length” comes from, but perspective still belongs to the real focal length.

Limits

Angle is not composition; distortion, crop modes and stabilizer crop change what is visible. Check the monitor after the math.

Field check: measure the frame with a door

Stand against the back wall and shoot the same composition at 24mm, 35mm and 50mm, using the door width in frame as the reference. If 24mm still does not fit, do not back through the wall; go vertical or use a wider lens. If 50mm gives natural facial perspective, do not force 24mm just to include the room. Record sensor width, focal length and door-to-frame ratio on one sheet, then reuse it on the next setup.

Practice

Take a 24mm and a 50mm lens into the same room, from the same tripod position. Calculate each horizontal angle first, then compare the frames: how much wall does the 24mm hold, and how much larger is the person at 50mm? If you can explain why a wider angle is not the same as a bigger subject, you have used this page.
