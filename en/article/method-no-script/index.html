You Cannot Write a Script

If you cannot write a script, board eight shots with one action each; action will force the lines.

3 min read · Field noteTVCN EditorialEdited 2026-11

Write three lines, not ten pages

  • Who: a specific person with a job, an age or an action already in progress.
  • Want: a goal that can be completed today and seen on screen.
  • Blocked by: a specific person, rule, deadline or their own fear.

A one-page test

Turn the three lines into three beats: opening, conflict, ending; one action per beat. Read it to someone who has not seen it and ask them to retell it. If they cannot, the structure is unclear, not the actor.

When not to improvise

Do not improvise without an ending. Improvisation works inside a clear goal and obstacle; it cannot replace structure. Write one page first, then let actors move inside it.

On set

Draw eight panels, one action each, no dialogue. Tell it in order; if it does not flow, change a panel.

Do this

If you cannot write a script, write an eight-panel shot board, one action per panel. Action forces dialogue, not the other way around.

  1. For each panel write “who does what,” with no emotion or explanation.
  2. Give each panel a shootable location, prop, and time.
  3. Read the eight panels in order, cut repeated information, and add one action wherever cause and effect are missing.

Avoid

Do not start with pages of dialogue; lines without action play like a reading on set.

3 checks

  • Each of the eight panels has one action and no emotion adjectives.
  • Every panel has a location, prop, and time you can shoot.
  • Read in order, cause and effect are clear and repeats are gone.
