Edit, Grade & Sound

A reusable post pipeline from ingest to delivery: structure first, sound second, grade last, then a technical check.

How the path runs

The trap is polishing while the cut is still moving. Seven steps in order: ingest, assembly, rough cut, sound, grade, QC, delivery. Do not skip.

  1. Step 1: Ingest and back up: Name folders date_scene and keep original filenames. Verify copies with checksums, not a progress bar. Keep proxies in a separate folder and treat camera originals as read-only. Back up to a second drive and one offsite copy. Output: Two source copies, proxies, and a media list.
  2. Step 2: Assembly: Lay the safe takes on the timeline by scene and shot number; confirm every shot exists. Mark the best take from the log in the filename. Assembly answers “is it there,” not “is it good.” Output: A complete assembly and best-take marks.
  3. Step 3: Rough cut: Every twenty minutes ask what the viewer knows and what the next shot adds. Three same-length shots in a row need a rhythm change. Trim one or two seconds from each shot. Show the rough cut to a stranger and ask who wants what. Output: A clear rough cut and a problem list.
  4. Step 4: Sound: Clean noise, level, and intelligibility on every line; lay ambience so cuts do not live in a vacuum; add music last. If music covers dialogue, drop it six dB or cut it. Output: A sound-clean mix and a music/ambience list.
  5. Step 5: Grade: Unify exposure and white balance so every shot feels like the same day; then secondary work on skin, sky, and product color. Style comes last; a LUT is not a correction. Calibrate monitor brightness first. Output: A primary-corrected version and a styled version.
  6. Step 6: QC: Run the delivery sheet: runtime, frame, resolution, bitrate, frame rate, loudness, true peak, captions, filename. Listen on headphones and a speaker; watch the vertical cut on a phone; mute it and look for black frames. Output: A QC sheet and a fixed master.
  7. Step 7: Deliver: Keep one master with fixed resolution, bitrate, audio, and captions. Platform cuts derive from it; the master never changes. Name files title_platform_version_date; delivery notes state what is included, excluded, and how many revisions remain. Output: Master, platform cuts, delivery notes, project backup.

Three exercises

  1. Exercise 1: Three-minute assembly: Assemble one scene by shot number for three minutes; do not fix pacing, just confirm coverage. Output: One assembly.
  2. Exercise 2: Sound-only pass: Turn off picture and listen for three minutes; log unclear, broken, or repeated sound. Output: A sound problem list.
  3. Exercise 3: Ten-point QC: Check ten items: runtime, frame, resolution, bitrate, frame rate, loudness, peak, captions, black frames, filename. Output: A QC sheet.

Deliverables

  • Two source copies and proxies
  • An assembly
  • A clear rough cut
  • A sound-clean mix
  • Primary and styled versions
  • A QC sheet
  • Master, platform cuts, delivery notes

Where it goes wrong

  • Grading before structure: every structural change wastes the grade.
  • Covering dialogue with music: viewers forgive ordinary images, not unintelligible words.
  • Naming files “final for real”: version chaos loses the master.

Next step

Check

  • Two source copies exist and the project opens offline.
  • A stranger can say who wants what.
  • Every line is audible without subtitles.
  • No color jumps between shots and skin is neutral.
  • All ten QC items have pass/fail.
  • One master exists; platform cuts derive from it.
