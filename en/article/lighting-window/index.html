Window Light Fill: Reflector Placement

Place the reflector on the shadow side, not by the window; it fills shadow, not adds light.

Window light is not a fixed source; it cools, dims and moves. Lock the shooting window, then use sheer curtains, blinds or bounce to control hardness and direction. Do not mix morning and afternoon light in one scene.

Window light changes with time and direction

The closer the subject, the higher the contrast; to soften, move them back or add diffusion.

Limits

Window light is beautiful and unstable. For long interviews, prepare fill; a cloud passing breaks shot-to-shot continuity.

Window fill: where the bounce goes, and how far

With window light as key, closer is not better. Put the bounce 45° to the subject's side, 0.5–1 meter away. Too close flattens the shadow; too far does nothing.

  1. White is the general choice; silver is harder and useful for an eye light without adding a lamp.
  2. Keep the board below eye line so it does not light nostrils and chin. For softer fill, use a fabric surface or diffuser.
  3. Shoot one frame with window light only, then add the board. Compare whether shadow detail appears without milkiness.

Acceptance: shadow detail, highlights not clipped, skin not gray. If the image dies when the board is removed, the key is too weak; move closer to the window first.

On set

Put the reflector on the shadow side, one meter away at 45 degrees. Check that shadows lift without clipping.

Do this

Window fill puts the reflector on the shadow side, not by the window; it fills darkness, not adds brightness.

  1. Place the reflector on the shadow side, one meter from the face.
  2. Angle it at 45 degrees.
  3. Check that shadows lift without blowing out.

Avoid

Do not put the reflector by the window; it brightens the bright side and leaves shadows black.

Judge

This page passes on three observable checks, not slogans:

  • 1. The reflector sits on the shadow side, one meter away at about 45 degrees.
  • 2. Shadows lift without highlight clipping.
  • 3. Before-and-after shows more shadow detail.

Failures

Any one of these means stop and fix it before post:

  • 1. Check 1 is only staged (“The reflector sits on the shadow side, one met…”); another camera position or location exposes it.
  • 2. Check 2 is left to post or a verbal explanation (“Shadows lift without highlight clipping”); nothing on set can be rechecked.
  • 3. Do not put the reflector by the window; it brightens the bright side and leaves shadows black.

Extend

10-minute exercise: Run a 60-second test with the gear you already own, measuring only “Place the reflector on the shadow side, one”. Record two numbers: what you need and what the gear actually gives.

Check

  • The reflector sits on the shadow side, one meter away at about 45 degrees.
  • Shadows lift without highlight clipping.
  • Before-and-after shows more shadow detail.
