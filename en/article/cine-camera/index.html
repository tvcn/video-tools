Cine Camera: Sensor, Mount, Codec

Cine cameras are sensor, mount, and codec first; the badge and body do not decide the job.

3 min read · Field noteTVCN Tools DeskEdited 2026-11

With a limited budget, a crew under three or a need for a light single camera, a cinema camera is not required; a mirrorless with external recording may fit better. Do not substitute camera class for story.

What to compare first

  • Sensor and format: format, crop modes and high-frame-rate crops; do not buy by pixel count.
  • Mount and recording: mount ecosystem, internal ND, codec and monitoring output; these decide on-set speed.
  • Power and cooling: battery runtime, external power and fan noise; a paid shoot cannot rely on reboots.

On set

Bring your own PL or EF lens and card, record five minutes of 4K 60p, feel the mount temperature, and check rolling shutter and shadow noise.

Do this

A cinema camera is judged by sensor, mount, and recording format; the name and shape do not decide whether it shoots your project.

  1. Bring your own PL or EF lens and card, record five minutes of 4K 60p.
  2. Feel the mount temperature and play back for rolling shutter and shadow noise.
  3. Only discuss rental price after all three pass.

Avoid

Do not judge by the spec sheet alone; mount heat, rolling shutter, and shadow noise must show in your own footage.

Check

  • Five minutes of 4K 60p is recorded on your own lens and card and plays back.
  • Mount temperature is logged and not too hot to change lenses.
  • Playback shows rolling shutter and shadow noise levels, not just a demo reel.
