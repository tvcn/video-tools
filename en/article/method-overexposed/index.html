The Highlights Are Gone

Stop down, add ND, kill unnecessary practicals; clipped pixels do not come back.

On set

Cut a stop, add ND, and turn off practicals in frame. Clipped highlights cannot be saved.

Do this

Clipped highlights: stop down a stop, add ND, and kill unnecessary practicals. Blown pixels do not return.

  1. Use ND or close the iris until highlights sit inside the waveform.
  2. Turn off unneeded bulbs or windows in frame to reduce the ratio.
  3. Check faces and sky with zebras or false color before rolling.

Avoid

Do not expect post to recover highlights; pure white has no detail.

Three causes

For “The Highlights Are Gone”, check three directions first. Change one variable, record a 30-second comparison, and see which link the change follows.

CauseSignal on setFive-minute test
No NDAt a wide aperture in daylight the shutter is maxed and highlights clipAdd 2–4 stops of ND
Specular highlightsLamps, windows and metal reflect straight into the lensChange the angle or diffuse/polarize
Overexposed Log“Expose right” without a ceiling leaves no highlight detailKeep highlights below 95%

On-set diagnosis

  1. Turn on 95% zebras and look for large blinking areas; confirm with false colour.
  2. Check whether the histogram is pinned at the right edge; clipped areas cannot be recovered.
  3. Shoot the same shot one stop down and compare highlight texture with shadow noise.

Fix

Clipped highlights are not a post fix. Cut light first, then decide which highlight must survive; you cannot keep all of them.

  1. Add ND or stop down; keep the shutter near 1/50 at 25p.
  2. Change the angle on windows, tubes and metal, or diffuse the source.
  3. Expose to the right with a third-stop margin; keep peaks below 95%.
  4. If the highlight is the subject, underexpose the shadows a little and keep the texture.

Failure cases

With “The Highlights Are Gone”, these three moves make it worse. Pick one replacement action and do not change three variables at once.

FailureWhy it gets worseReplace with
Pull highlights in postThey turn dead white with halosReduce light on set
Darken the whole imageMidtones die and the frame looks muddyLower highlights only
Add contrastHighlights get harder and detail disappearsRecover highlights before contrast

3 checks
