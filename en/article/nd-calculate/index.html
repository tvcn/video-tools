ND Calculation: Wide Open in Daylight

Set shutter and aperture, then calculate stops; guessing the filter leaves you dark nine times out of ten.

ND calculation fixes shutter and aperture first, then counts stops; guessing the filter is dark nine times out of ten.

On set

Set 1/50 and f/2.8, meter the scene EV, then calculate the stops to cut and choose the ND.

Do this

  1. Set 1/50 and f/2.8.
  2. Meter ambient EV with a meter or the camera.
  3. Calculate the stops needed and fit the matching ND.

Avoid

Do not swap ND by look; wrong stops put the face two stops under.

Three causes

For “ND Calculation: Wide Open in Daylight”, check three directions first. Change one variable, record a 30-second comparison, and see which link the change follows.

CauseSignal on setFive-minute test
ND labelling is inaccurateThe real stop differs by half a stop and exposure missesMeasure against a shutter baseline
Stacked filtersTwo NDs stack into vignetting and colour shiftUse one filter; if stacking, test the edges
Wide-angle vignettingA thick filter blocks light at the cornersUse a thin frame or larger diameter

On-set diagnosis

  1. Set the baseline shutter and aperture without ND.
  2. Add ND, keep aperture, lower shutter to target, and check whether highlights return to the pass line.
  3. Shoot a white wall at the wide end and inspect corners for darkening or colour shift.

Fix

ND calculation is not a formula recital; set baseline exposure, then measure the actual filter offset. The marked stop is only a starting point.

  1. Baseline: 1/50 at 25p; set aperture by depth of field.
  2. Test each ND once and log its real light loss.
  3. Prefer a single ND; if stacking, use thin frames and stop down one stop.
  4. Check corners at the wide end and move to a larger diameter if needed.

Failure cases

With “ND Calculation: Wide Open in Daylight”, these three moves make it worse. Pick one replacement action and do not change three variables at once.

FailureWhy it gets worseReplace with
Trust the markingThe filter is half a stop off and highlights still clipCalibrate by shooting
Stack two filters for three stopsVignetting and colour shift arrive togetherUse a single variable ND or a larger diameter
Ignore shutterPushing shutter to 1/1000 makes motion hardND first, shutter second

Judge

This page passes on three observable checks, not slogans:

  • 1. Shutter 1/50 and aperture f/2.8 are locked first.
  • 2. Ambient EV is metered, not estimated.
  • 3. Calculated stops match the ND used and exposure lands on target.

Failures

Any one of these means stop and fix it before post:

  • 1. Check 1 is only staged (“Shutter 1/50 and aperture f/2.8 are locked fir…”); another camera position or location exposes it.
  • 2. Check 2 is left to post or a verbal explanation (“Ambient EV is metered, not estimated”); nothing on set can be rechecked.
  • 3. Do not swap ND by look; wrong stops put the face two stops under.

Extend

10-minute exercise: Run a 60-second test with the gear you already own, measuring only “Set 1/50 and f/2.8.”. Record two numbers: what you need and what the gear actually gives.

Check

  • Shutter 1/50 and aperture f/2.8 are locked first.
  • Ambient EV is metered, not estimated.
  • Calculated stops match the ND used and exposure lands on target.
