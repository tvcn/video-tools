Experimental Breakdown: Rule, Repetition, Break

An eight-beat breakdown of a four-minute experimental film: pre-rule, rule, three repetitions, break, consequence, stop.

Fix Three Variables First

Fix three variables: four minutes, festival/art platform, and the goal is “the viewer can name the rule and the break.” Experimental is not random; it needs a rule more than narrative. Find the rule, then the variable in repetition.

Sample note: the timecodes below are a teaching sample for this format's usual length. They do not correspond to one specific film and are not frame-accurate analysis. Use the same positions on your own film; do not treat these beats as the only answer.

Timecode Breakdown: 8 Beats

Watch in order; do not read commentary first. For each beat record four things: picture, sound, job, and steal or do-not-copy.

  1. 0:00–0:10 | Picture: before the rule: an empty space, object, or preparation. Sound: ambience, no music. Job: quiet the viewer. Steal: open on a still frame. Do not copy: fast montage.
  2. 0:10–0:20 | Picture: rule established: locked frame, left to right, one action. Sound: the action sound complete for the first time. Job: signal repetition. Steal: first pass complete. Do not copy: fragment the first pass.
  3. 0:20–0:60 | Picture: repetition one: change one variable (light/slow/bright). Sound: same action sound. Job: build expectation. Steal: make the variable visible. Do not copy: change three variables.
  4. 1:00–1:40 | Picture: repetition two: the variable intensifies (heavy/fast/dark). Sound: action grows. Job: give repetition direction. Steal: use weight/speed. Do not copy: narrate it.
  5. 1:40–2:20 | Picture: repetition three: variable at limit; the person begins not to finish. Sound: breath close. Job: make expectation about to fail. Steal: almost complete the action. Do not copy: break immediately.
  6. 2:20–3:00 | Picture: break: camera still locked, action/person exits. Sound: remaining sound in the empty space. Job: make the rule fail. Steal: break once. Do not copy: break three times.
  7. 3:00–3:40 | Picture: consequence: empty space/object/trace remains. Sound: ambience. Job: the break costs something. Steal: stay on the consequence. Do not copy: cut to black.
  8. 3:40–4:00 | Picture: stop: no return to normal, no explanation. Sound: silence. Job: let viewers carry it out. Steal: stop unfinished. Do not copy: add a summary title.

Read the Structure

The spine is rule, repetition, variable, limit, break, consequence, stop. The clearer the rule, the more tension repetition creates; break once, and leave the consequence on screen.

Ask only three questions: what proof arrives in the first ten seconds; whether every ten to fifteen seconds adds information; whether the end lands one action, one change, or one question. If any is missing, a 4 min film falls apart.

Budget Order of Magnitude

Budget is a rough mainland-China production range, excluding media spend; it is an order of magnitude, not a quote. At the same length, a stage, cast, effects, or music rights can each double the cost.

  • Low: RMB 1,000–5,000, one to two people, one space, natural light, one day.
  • Mid: RMB 5,000–20,000, two to four people, controlled light/sound, two to three days.
  • High: RMB 20,000–80,000+, director, camera, sound, art, set or post, five to ten days.

Three Structures You Can Steal

Steal structure, not images. These three still work when you change the subject.

  1. Make the first pass complete so the rule is clear.
  2. Change one variable per repetition.
  3. Stay on the consequence after the break; do not cut to black.

Three Reasons Not to Copy

Not “bad,” but built on different conditions; copying exposes your budget, platform, and audience.

  1. Do not copy “long take equals experimental”: without a rule, long is just slow.
  2. Do not copy “no narrative equals high art”: without expectation, a break is an accident.
  3. Do not copy narration: explanation kills the viewer's own discovery.

Breakdown Exercises

Do them before shooting; do not just read. Do the three in order, and the third must become a fifteen-second file.

  1. Write the film's rule in one sentence.
  2. Count repetitions and variables; more than one variable per pass means recut.
  3. Watch muted; can the picture build expectation?

Check

  • The rule is established in the first twenty seconds.
  • The first repetition is complete, not fragmented.
  • One variable changes per repetition.
  • The third repetition brings expectation close to failure.
  • The break happens once.
  • The consequence holds for at least ten seconds.
  • No narration explains.
