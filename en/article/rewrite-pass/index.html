Rewrite: Delete the Explanation, Add the Action

In a rewrite, delete the explanation and add a visible action; often they are the same fix.

Rewrite by deleting explanation first, then adding one visible action; often they are the same fix.

On set

Search the draft for because, so, and felt. Replace each with an action or an object; keep at most one explanatory sentence per page.

Do this

  1. Search for “because,” “so,” and “feels”; delete every hit.
  2. Replace the deleted information with an action or object.
  3. Keep at most one explanatory line per page and let the image carry the rest.

Avoid

Do not patch holes with explanation; more explanation means fewer actions.

Three causes

For “Rewrite: Delete the Explanation, Add the Action”, check three directions first. Change one variable, record a 30-second comparison, and see which link the change follows.

CauseSignal on setFive-minute test
Explaining themeA character says “I learned to let go” and the scene diesTurn theme into action and cost
Repeated informationThe same fact arrives three times and the audience leaves earlyOne new fact per scene
No actionThe page talks and nobody does anythingAt least one action changes the relationship

On-set diagnosis

  1. Mark “says” and “does” on every page; a page of pure talk gets cut or changed.
  2. Write the change of each scene in one sentence; if you cannot, there is no change.
  3. Delete the last line and see whether the previous line still stands.

Fix

A rewrite is not a word-count cut; every page must produce a change. Remove explanation first, then hide information in action.

  1. Cut lines where a character explains why they are like this.
  2. Put backstory in a prop, space or action: an old photo, a locked door.
  3. End each scene with a change in relationship or information; do not return to neutral.
  4. Save the theme for the final action; do not let a character state it.

Failure cases

With “Rewrite: Delete the Explanation, Add the Action”, these three moves make it worse. Pick one replacement action and do not change three variables at once.

FailureWhy it gets worseReplace with
Cut words onlyThe page is shorter and still unchangedCheck change per scene
Add more eventsEvents pile up but nobody choosesEvery event must force a choice
Turn explanation into voice-overThe information survives with a new mouthMake it visible action

Acceptance table

Acceptance is not “it feels better”; all four must be identifiable, playable and logged.

CheckPass lineLog
Change per sceneEvery scene has a from-X-to-Y sentenceScene table
Say/do ratioAt least one action per pagePage marks
ExplanationCharacters do not explain their psychologySearch “because/actually”
EndingThe last action carries the themeRead the final three pages

Judge

This page passes on three observable checks, not slogans:

  • 1. The page has no “because,” “so,” or “feels” explaining the action.
  • 2. Deleted information now appears as a filmable action or object.
  • 3. At most one explanatory line remains per page; the image carries the rest.

Failures

Any one of these means stop and fix it before post:

  • 1. Check 1 is only staged (“The page has no “because,” “so,” or “feels” ex…”); another camera position or location exposes it.
  • 2. Check 2 is left to post or a verbal explanation (“Deleted information now appears as a filmable…”); nothing on set can be rechecked.
  • 3. Do not patch holes with explanation; more explanation means fewer actions.

Extend

10-minute exercise: Write one 30-second scene with only “Search for “because,” “so,” and “feels”; delete every” and “Replace the deleted information with an action or”. Cut every explanation and ask one person to retell what the character wants.

Check

  • The page has no “because,” “so,” or “feels” explaining the action.
  • Deleted information now appears as a filmable action or object.
  • At most one explanatory line remains per page; the image carries the rest.
