COB Light: One Source, One Soft Box

A COB is a bare source; pair it with a soft box or lantern or the face gets hard shadows.

4 min read · Field noteTVCN Tools DeskEdited 2026-11

In small rooms, close distance or when you need extreme softness, a COB and box may not fit; use a panel or reflector. Never point a bare COB at a face.

What to compare first

  • Output and color: check real illuminance, color range and low-output stability; wattage is not brightness.
  • Rendering and fan: shoot skin and red cloth, and listen to the fan; in a quiet interview the fan becomes a sound problem.
  • Mount and control: confirm Bowens mount, softbox and grid; a COB without modifiers is just hard light.

On set

Fit a 90-centimeter softbox, place it one meter from the face at 50 percent, and check the shadow edge. If it is still hard, add diffusion or move closer.

Do this

A COB is a bare source and needs a softbox or lantern; bare on a face, shadows turn unusable.

  1. Fit a 90 cm softbox, place one meter from the face at 50 percent.
  2. Watch the shadow edge.
  3. If it is still hard, add diffusion or move closer.

Avoid

Do not point a bare COB at a face; skipping the softbox makes shadows too hard to fix.

Check

  • The COB runs through a softbox, not bare.
  • A one-meter, 50 percent sample shows the shadow edge.
  • A diffusion or closer-position comparison exists if the edge stays hard.
