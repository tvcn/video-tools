Armature: It Must Stand Before It Acts

A stop-motion puppet must stand, walk, and repeat before acting. A soft armature collapses in ten frames.

The armature must stand before it performs

Test three moves: standing without wobble, raising an arm without sag, sitting without collapse. Use a metal armature with silicone pads and enlarge the feet; do not start with fingers before the center of gravity and knees are stable.

Before touching the puppet each frame, ask whether it will fall; shoot a test and check the pivot frame by frame.

Limits

Harder is not better. Too rigid looks robotic; leave softness where it bends and stiffness where it carries weight.

Rigging: run extreme-pose tests before animation

A rig is not done when bones are connected. Problems appear when an animator pulls: elbow collapse, knee inversion, shoulder intersections. Before handoff, run extreme-pose tests; do not start animation until they pass.

TestPosePass condition
ShoulderArm raised 180°No armpit collapse, no cloth intersection
ElbowBend 140°Volume holds, no candy-wrapper twist
KneeDeep squat + reverseNo inversion, feet do not slide
SpineTwist + side bendChest and pelvis transition smoothly

Project-level checks

  1. Standardize controller names: left/right, FK/IK, mirror rules on one page. Animators should not guess.
  2. Lock axes that should not move and hide unused controls. A rig that can be bumped will be bumped.
  3. Save a screenshot of every extreme pose as the rig acceptance record. “It works” is not evidence.
  4. Weight joints before muscles; a full-body weight pass hides problems inside other problems.

If the schedule is under three days, do not build film-level facial rigging. Use simplified facial controls and spend the time on performance and rendering.

On set

Make the puppet stand for ten seconds without wobble, then walk three steps without collapsing. Use a metal armature and weight the feet.

Do this

A stop-motion puppet must stand, walk, and repeat before performance; a soft armature collapses in ten frames.

  1. Stand the puppet for ten seconds without wobble.
  2. Walk three steps without collapse.
  3. Use a metal armature and add weight to the feet.

Avoid

Do not let the armature stay soft; it collapses within ten frames.

3 checks

  • The puppet stands ten seconds without wobble and the feet are weighted.
  • Three steps hold without collapse or sudden joint lock.
  • Ten frames of one pose show no positional drift.
